\begin{abstract}
Exact Kalman linear attention carries a dense posterior covariance but pays a sequential dense
covariance dependency at every token.  A natural approximation is to hold the latent state fixed
inside a short panel, condition all panel observations jointly, and add the accumulated process
noise only at the boundary.  That approximation admits compact panel solves and is internally
consistent, but a sealed
quality study rejects it: when the transition strongly contracts, the observations primarily
measure newly injected process noise, precisely the information that the delayed-noise model
discards.

This note develops a different panel algorithm.  It retains the process-noise contribution to the
observation covariance, to every causal read--observation cross-covariance, and to the terminal
state--observation cross-covariance.  The resulting panel update is algebraically identical to the
tokenwise Kalman filter.  For the damped rotational process model of KDN-2 and block-isotropic
process noise, these dynamic terms can be constructed in $O(b^2d)$ work for panel width $b$ and
state dimension $d$, alongside the unavoidable $O(bd^2)$ dense-boundary work.  Thus panel width is
an execution tile rather than a statistical approximation knob.  Performance remains an
empirical kernel question; this document defines the mathematics, a coherent nested fallback
approximation, and the gates required before making a speed claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Decision and scope}

The exact-static, panel-refreshed proposal is not promoted as ``ChunkKLA v1.''  Its Cholesky
conditioning is exact for a static latent variable, but relative to the tokenwise dynamic model it
sets all within-panel process-noise observation correlations to zero and adds only the open-loop
terminal covariance.  In a preregistered FP64 study, width $b=1$ was an exact control, while every
tested width $b\geq2$ failed at least one regime.  At $b=2$, the near-zero-transition regime had
median output relative error $0.69719$ and median final-mean relative error $0.99999$.  This is a
modeling failure rather than a factorization or roundoff failure.

The replacement is \emph{structured dynamic panel conditioning}.  It preserves

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item the token-timed damped rotational transition;
  \item every process-noise contribution that can be inferred from a panel observation;
  \item causal read-after-write outputs;
  \item the exact dense posterior covariance at every panel boundary; and
  \item the tokenwise KDN-2 result in exact arithmetic, for every panel width.
\end{enumerate}

KDN-2 is the model name.  Panel conditioning is an implementation strategy, not a second model.
The old exact-static construction is retained only as the \emph{delayed-noise ablation}.
Production files remain out of scope until a sandbox implementation passes forward, gradient,
finite-precision, resource, and end-to-end speed gates.

\section{Tokenwise KDN-2}

Let $\ve{S}_t^\star\in\R^{d\times d_v}$ be the latent associative state and
$\ve{v}_t,\ve{e}_t,\ve{o}_t\in\R^{d_v}$.  Conditional on the incoming filtration, its value
columns are independent with a shared address-space covariance,
\begin{equation}
  \ve{S}_{0,:,a}^\star\mid\mathcal F_0
  \sim\mathcal N(\ve{S}_{0,:,a},\ve{P}_0),
  \qquad \ve{P}_0\succeq\ve{0},
  \qquad a=1,\ldots,d_v.
\end{equation}
The latent process and observation are
\begin{align}
  \ve{S}_t^\star
    &=\ve{D}_t\ve{S}_{t-1}^\star+\ve{W}_t,
  &
  \ve{W}_{t,:,a}&\sim\mathcal N(\ve{0},\ve{\Omega}_t),
  \label{eq:process}\\
  \ve{v}_t
    &=\ve{S}_t^{\star\tr}\ve{k}_t+\ve{e}_t,
  &
  \ve{e}_t&\sim\mathcal N(\ve{0},r_t\ve{I}_{d_v}),
  \qquad r_t>0 .
  \label{eq:observation}
\end{align}
Every $\ve{\Omega}_t$ is symmetric positive semidefinite.  The process noises, observation
noises, incoming state error, and different value-coordinate noise columns are mutually
independent.  All covariance and conditioning identities below are derived for one arbitrary value
column and then applied to all $d_v$ columns with shared factors.
The transition, keys, queries, and noise parameters are conditioned design quantities known
causally at their token.  Value coordinates are conditionally independent and share the same
address-space covariance.  To connect with the lifted Direction-A formulation, the symbols used
below denote the effective state-space addresses
\begin{equation}
  \ve{k}_t=\ve{E}_t\ve{k}_t^{\mathrm{raw}},
  \qquad
  \ve{q}_t=\ve{F}_t\ve{q}_t^{\mathrm{raw}},
\end{equation}
where the lifts $\ve{E}_t$ and $\ve{F}_t$ are also token-causal design quantities.

The tokenwise filter is
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\ve{S}}_t
    &=\ve{D}_t\ve{S}_{t-1},
  &
  \widehat{\ve{P}}_t
    &=\ve{D}_t\ve{P}_{t-1}\ve{D}_t^{\tr}+\ve{\Omega}_t,
  \label{eq:token-predict}\\
  \ve{\kappa}_t
    &=\frac{\widehat{\ve{P}}_t\ve{k}_t}
      {r_t+\ve{k}_t^{\tr}\widehat{\ve{P}}_t\ve{k}_t},
  &
  \ve{S}_t
    &=\widehat{\ve{S}}_t+
      \ve{\kappa}_t
      \bigl(\ve{v}_t-\widehat{\ve{S}}_t^{\tr}\ve{k}_t\bigr)^{\tr},
  \label{eq:token-correct}\\
  \ve{P}_t
    &=\widehat{\ve{P}}_t-
      \frac{(\widehat{\ve{P}}_t\ve{k}_t)
      (\widehat{\ve{P}}_t\ve{k}_t)^{\tr}}
      {r_t+\ve{k}_t^{\tr}\widehat{\ve{P}}_t\ve{k}_t},
  &
  \ve{o}_t&=\ve{S}_t^{\tr}\ve{q}_t .
  \label{eq:token-read}
\end{align}
The covariance is dense.  The challenge is not the formula but the tokenwise chain of dense
$d\times d$ updates.

\subsection{Damped rotational process}

Partition the address dimension into fixed modes of size $d_m=2r_m$.  KDN-2 uses
\begin{equation}
  \ve{D}_t^{(m)}
  =\rho_{t,m}\ve{R}(\theta_{t,m})\otimes\ve{I}_{r_m},
  \qquad
  \ve{D}_t=\blockdiag_m\ve{D}_t^{(m)},
  \label{eq:direction-a-transition}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \ve{R}(\theta)
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
    \sin\theta& \cos\theta
  \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The radius controls survival, the angle controls phase, and the key/query geometry controls
visibility.  Process noise is block isotropic,
\begin{equation}
  \ve{\Omega}_t
  =\blockdiag_m\bigl(\nu_{t,m}\ve{I}_{d_m}\bigr),
  \qquad \nu_{t,m}\geq0.
  \label{eq:block-noise}
\end{equation}
This structure is a model choice.  The covariance $\ve{P}_t$ itself is not projected: measurement
updates create anisotropy and cross-mode correlations, and the panel boundary retains them.
Ordinary learned transitions use $0<\rho_{t,m}\leq1$.  A hard reset is an explicit override
$\ve{D}_t=\ve{0}$ while retaining $\ve{\Omega}_t$; it may occur inside a mathematical panel.
At such a token, every effective mode radius used by the covariance builder is zero even if the
network emitted a nonzero pre-reset radius.

\section{One exact dynamic panel}

Let a panel contain local tokens $1{:}b$ and begin after posterior
$(\ve{S}_0,\ve{P}_0)$.  The first prediction occurs at its original time:
\begin{equation}
  \ve{X}^-=\ve{D}_1\ve{S}_0,
  \qquad
  \ve{P}^-=\ve{D}_1\ve{P}_0\ve{D}_1^{\tr}+\ve{\Omega}_1.
  \label{eq:first-prediction}
\end{equation}
The token-one state is the common panel latent variable.  This convention makes $b=1$ exactly
the tokenwise filter and leaves the later noises $\ve{w}_{2:b}$ to be handled dynamically.
A partial final panel uses its actual token count as $b$; padding is excluded from every covariance,
factorization, solve, and output rather than represented as a finite-noise observation.
An implementation may use a fixed physical tile only if inactive rows are algebraically
block-decoupled from the true $b\times b$ system.

\subsection{Deterministic transport and dynamic residual state}

Define forward products
\begin{equation}
  \ve{\Phi}_1=\ve{I},
  \qquad
  \ve{\Phi}_i=\ve{D}_i\ve{D}_{i-1}\cdots\ve{D}_2
  \quad(i\geq2),
  \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
and transport the observation and read addresses into the token-one frame:
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\ve{k}}_i=\ve{\Phi}_i^{\tr}\ve{k}_i,
  \qquad
  \widetilde{\ve{q}}_i=\ve{\Phi}_i^{\tr}\ve{q}_i.
  \label{eq:transported-addresses}
\end{equation}
No inverse transition or prefix ratio is used.

Separate the later process noise from the transported entry state:
\begin{equation}
  \ve{s}_i^\star
  =\ve{\Phi}_i\ve{x}^\star+\ve{\xi}_i,
  \qquad
  \ve{\xi}_1=\ve{0},
  \qquad
  \ve{\xi}_i=\ve{D}_i\ve{\xi}_{i-1}+\ve{w}_i .
  \label{eq:state-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Here $\ve{x}^\star$ has conditional mean $\ve{X}^-$ and covariance $\ve{P}^-$.  Let
\begin{equation}
  \ve{Q}_1=\ve{0},
  \qquad
  \ve{Q}_i
  =\ve{D}_i\ve{Q}_{i-1}\ve{D}_i^{\tr}+\ve{\Omega}_i .
  \label{eq:dynamic-q}
\end{equation}
For $i\geq j$, define
\begin{equation}
  \ve{\Phi}_{i:j}=\ve{D}_i\ve{D}_{i-1}\cdots\ve{D}_{j+1},
  \qquad
  \ve{\Phi}_{j:j}=\ve{I}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \ve{Q}_{ij}^{W}
  :=\Cov(\ve{\xi}_i,\ve{\xi}_j)
  =\ve{\Phi}_{i:j}\ve{Q}_j .
  \label{eq:cross-time-q}
\end{equation}
This identity remains valid for an exact-zero transition: earlier covariance is erased, and the
current process noise starts a new independent component.

\subsection{Joint innovation covariance}

Stack
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\ve{K}}
  =[\widetilde{\ve{k}}_1,\ldots,\widetilde{\ve{k}}_b],
  \qquad
  \widetilde{\ve{Q}}
  =[\widetilde{\ve{q}}_1,\ldots,\widetilde{\ve{q}}_b],
\end{equation}
and let row $i$ of $\ve{V}\in\R^{b\times d_v}$ be $\ve{v}_i^{\tr}$.  With
$\ve{R}_v=\diag(r_1,\ldots,r_b)$, the innovation matrix is
\begin{equation}
  \ve{\Delta}
  =\ve{V}-\widetilde{\ve{K}}^{\tr}\ve{X}^-.
  \label{eq:panel-innovation}
\end{equation}

The later process noises induce the observation covariance
\begin{equation}
  [\ve{\Sigma}_W]_{ij}
  =\ve{k}_i^{\tr}\ve{Q}_{ij}^{W}\ve{k}_j
  \quad(i\geq j),
  \qquad
  [\ve{\Sigma}_W]_{ji}=[\ve{\Sigma}_W]_{ij}.
  \label{eq:sigma-w}
\end{equation}
Therefore the exact innovation covariance is
\begin{equation}
  \ve{G}
  =\ve{R}_v
   +\widetilde{\ve{K}}^{\tr}\ve{P}^-\widetilde{\ve{K}}
   +\ve{\Sigma}_W.
  \label{eq:dynamic-gram}
\end{equation}
Because $\ve{R}_v\succ0$, $\ve{G}$ is positive definite in exact arithmetic.  Factor and whiten
the innovations:
\begin{equation}
  \ve{G}=\ve{J}\ve{J}^{\tr},
  \qquad
  \ve{Z}=\ve{J}^{-1}\ve{\Delta},
  \label{eq:chol-z}
\end{equation}
where $\ve{J}$ is lower triangular.

\subsection{All causal outputs}

For $j\leq i$, the state--observation cross-covariance is
\begin{equation}
  \ve{C}_{i,j}
  =\Cov(\ve{s}_i^\star,v_j)
  =\ve{\Phi}_i\ve{P}^-\widetilde{\ve{k}}_j
   +\ve{Q}_{ij}^{W}\ve{k}_j .
  \label{eq:state-observation-cross}
\end{equation}
Define a lower-triangular read--observation matrix
\begin{equation}
  [\ve{H}]_{ij}
  =
  \begin{cases}
    \ve{q}_i^{\tr}\ve{C}_{i,j},&j\leq i,\\
    0,&j>i.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:read-observation-cross}
\end{equation}
Every read-after-write output is then produced by
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \ve{O}
  =\widetilde{\ve{Q}}^{\tr}\ve{X}^-
   +\tril\!\left(\ve{H}\ve{J}^{-\tr}
   \right)\ve{Z}.}
  \label{eq:causal-outputs}
\end{equation}
The triangular mask is essential.  For row $i$, the first $i$ columns of the full triangular
solve equal the solve using the leading $i\times i$ Cholesky block.  Hence output $i$ is the
conditional mean using observations $1{:}i$ and cannot depend on a future token.  Masking a full
$\ve{G}^{-1}$ update is not equivalent.

\subsection{Dense boundary posterior}

At the terminal token, collect
\begin{equation}
  \ve{C}_b=[\ve{C}_{b,1},\ldots,\ve{C}_{b,b}],
  \qquad
  \ve{B}_b=\ve{C}_b\ve{J}^{-\tr}.
  \label{eq:boundary-factor}
\end{equation}
The exact posterior mean and covariance are
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \ve{S}_b
  =\ve{\Phi}_b\ve{X}^-+\ve{B}_b\ve{Z},}
  \label{eq:boundary-mean}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \ve{P}_b
  =\ve{\Phi}_b\ve{P}^-\ve{\Phi}_b^{\tr}
   +\ve{Q}_b
   -\ve{B}_b\ve{B}_b^{\tr}.}
  \label{eq:boundary-covariance}
\end{equation}
The next panel consumes this dense $\ve{P}_b$ without projection.  Because terminal process noise
also appears in the observations, the ordinary independent-noise Joseph formula is not the right
reference.  Let
\begin{equation}
  \ve{\Pi}_b
  =\ve{\Phi}_b\ve{P}^-\ve{\Phi}_b^{\tr}+\ve{Q}_b,
  \qquad
  \ve{L}_b=\ve{C}_b\ve{G}^{-1}=\ve{B}_b\ve{J}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The generalized estimator-error form
\begin{equation}
  \ve{P}_b
  =\ve{\Pi}_b
   -\ve{L}_b\ve{C}_b^{\tr}
   -\ve{C}_b\ve{L}_b^{\tr}
   +\ve{L}_b\ve{G}\ve{L}_b^{\tr}
  \label{eq:generalized-error-covariance}
\end{equation}
is an independent FP64 numerical reference and reduces algebraically to
equation~\eqref{eq:boundary-covariance}.  The direct downdate is admitted to an FP32 candidate only
if the full-sequence numerical gate accepts it.

A stronger positive-semidefinite reference exposes the independent Gaussian sources.  For
$s=2{:}b$, let $\ve{T}_s=\ve{\Phi}_{b:s}$ and let
$\ve{A}_s\in\R^{b\times d}$ have row
\begin{equation}
  [\ve{A}_s]_{i,:}
  =
  \begin{cases}
    \ve{k}_i^{\tr}\ve{\Phi}_{i:s},&i\geq s,\\
    \ve{0}^{\tr},&i<s.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
  \ve{P}_b
  ={}&
  (\ve{\Phi}_b-\ve{L}_b\widetilde{\ve{K}}^{\tr})
  \ve{P}^-
  (\ve{\Phi}_b-\ve{L}_b\widetilde{\ve{K}}^{\tr})^{\tr}
  \nonumber\\
  &+\sum_{s=2}^{b}
  (\ve{T}_s-\ve{L}_b\ve{A}_s)
  \ve{\Omega}_s
  (\ve{T}_s-\ve{L}_b\ve{A}_s)^{\tr}
  +\ve{L}_b\ve{R}_v\ve{L}_b^{\tr}.
  \label{eq:source-square-covariance}
\end{align}
This square-sum form, rather than an independent-noise Joseph formula on the terminal state, is the
PSD oracle.

\section{Direction-A makes the dynamic builder structured}

The apparent obstacle is the family $\ve{Q}_{ij}^{W}$.  A generic implementation could
materialize $b^2$ vectors of width $d$, reproducing the expensive dynamic builder that made the
earlier ExactKLA kernel unattractive.  Equations~\eqref{eq:direction-a-transition} and
\eqref{eq:block-noise} remove the need for dense covariance propagation inside the panel.

For mode $m$,
\begin{equation}
  \ve{Q}_i^{(m)}=\tau_{i,m}\ve{I}_{d_m},
  \qquad
  \tau_{1,m}=0,
  \qquad
  \tau_{i,m}=\rho_{i,m}^2\tau_{i-1,m}+\nu_{i,m}.
  \label{eq:tau-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Here $\rho_{i,m}$ means the effective radius after applying the reset override, so it is zero at a
hard reset.  Equivalently, equation~\eqref{eq:tau-recurrence} is the blockwise form of
equation~\eqref{eq:dynamic-q} using the effective $\ve{D}_i$.
For each source observation $j$, initialize
\begin{equation}
  \ve{u}_{j,j}^{(m)}=\tau_{j,m}\ve{k}_j^{(m)}
  \label{eq:u-initialize}
\end{equation}
and propagate forward without a ratio,
\begin{equation}
  \ve{u}_{i,j}^{(m)}
  =\ve{D}_i^{(m)}\ve{u}_{i-1,j}^{(m)},
  \qquad i>j.
  \label{eq:u-propagate}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
  [\ve{\Sigma}_W]_{ij}
    &=\sum_m
      \ve{k}_i^{(m)\tr}\ve{u}_{i,j}^{(m)},
  \label{eq:structured-sigma}\\
  [\ve{H}_W]_{ij}
    &=\sum_m
      \ve{q}_i^{(m)\tr}\ve{u}_{i,j}^{(m)},
  \label{eq:structured-h}\\
  [\ve{C}_W]_{:,j}
    &=\ve{u}_{b,j}.
  \label{eq:structured-c}
\end{align}

The contribution of the dense incoming covariance is computed separately.  Form
\begin{equation}
  \ve{U}=\ve{P}^-\widetilde{\ve{K}}.
  \label{eq:dense-u}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
  \ve{G}
    &=\ve{R}_v+\widetilde{\ve{K}}^{\tr}\ve{U}+\ve{\Sigma}_W,
  \label{eq:assembled-g}\\
  \ve{H}
    &=\tril\!\left(
      \widetilde{\ve{Q}}^{\tr}\ve{U}+\ve{H}_W
      \right),
  \label{eq:assembled-h}\\
  \ve{C}_b
    &=\ve{\Phi}_b\ve{U}+\ve{C}_W.
  \label{eq:assembled-c}
\end{align}

The factor and all right-hand sides can be expressed as one combined solve,
\begin{equation}
  [\ve{Y}_H\mid\ve{Y}_C\mid\ve{Z}]
  =\ve{J}^{-1}
   [\ve{H}^{\tr}\mid\ve{C}_b^{\tr}\mid\ve{\Delta}],
  \qquad
  \ve{A}_{\mathrm{out}}=\tril(\ve{Y}_H^{\tr}),
  \qquad
  \ve{B}_b=\ve{Y}_C^{\tr}.
  \label{eq:combined-solve}
\end{equation}
Its right-hand-side width is $b+d+d_v$.  At $b=32$ and $d=d_v=128$, the solve is
$32\times288$.  The delayed-noise exact-static path solved only $d+d_v=256$ right-hand sides;
its solver timing is therefore not a matched proxy for the dynamic algorithm.

The reference implementation may materialize $\ve{u}_{i,j}$ for clarity.  A measured kernel must
not store a $b\times b\times d$ tensor in global memory.  It should propagate a tiled set of live
source vectors, accumulate the two $b\times b$ lower triangles, and retain only the $d\times b$
terminal columns.

Crucially, $\ve{\Sigma}_W$, $\ve{H}_W$, and $\ve{C}_W$ depend only on
$(\ve{D},\ve{\Omega},\ve{k},\ve{q})$, not on the recurrent dense
$(\ve{S},\ve{P})$ boundary.  One implementation arm may therefore construct the dynamic
corrections for all sequence panels in parallel before the dependent dense-boundary loop.  The
alternative is to fuse their construction into each resident panel.  The first arm spends global
traffic on packed triangles and terminal columns; the second spends registers and recomputation.
Both implement the same equations and must be compared as complete dependent recurrences.
A candidate GPU mapping assigns one warp or cooperative tile to each source $j$, retains one live
$d$-vector, advances it over $i=j{:}b$, emits packed lower-triangular entries of
$\ve{\Sigma}_W$ and $\ve{H}_W$, and writes $\ve{C}_{W,:,j}$ only at $i=b$.  Radius/angle
parameterization is converted to $\rho\cos\theta$ and $\rho\sin\theta$ before this inner source
loop; it may not execute a transcendental operation for every $(i,j,m)$ triple.

For the production training shape $B=4,H=16,T=4096,b=32,d=128$ and $64$ mode variances, a fully
materialized FP32 prepass stores, per panel--head, two packed $528$-entry triangles, one
$128\times32$ terminal matrix, and $64$ variance scalars.  This is $20{,}864$ bytes per
panel--head and exactly $163$ MiB over $8{,}192$ panel--heads.  This is only the dynamic-prepass
payload, not the total layer workspace.  It is transient and preallocated; backward recomputes it
rather than checkpointing another copy.  The dependent state/factor arenas and backward
checkpoint/replay storage require a new complete liveness witness.  The existing
$248.03125$ MiB core witness belongs to the rejected static schedule and cannot be reused as the
new total.  End-to-end timing includes both the prepass writes and the dependent consumer reads.
This schedule targets training and prefill.  Token-at-a-time decode remains the $b=1$ recurrence
unless a real token block is available.

\subsection{Cost}

For one panel, excluding value-width products, the leading work is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Region & Arithmetic \\
\midrule
transition prefixes and mode variances & $O(bd)$ \\
dense $\ve{P}^-\widetilde{\ve{K}}$ and boundary downdate & $O(bd^2)$ \\
static and dynamic Gram/read systems & $O(b^2d)$ \\
factorization and address-side triangular solves & $O(b^3+b^2d)$ \\
value solve, outputs, and mean commit & $O(b^2d_v+b d d_v)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The forward live workspace is $O(d^2+d d_v+bd+b^2+bd_v)$ per batch--head item; this excludes
batch--head replication and backward checkpoints.  The dynamic correction is lower-order
than the dense covariance products when $b<d$; in the intended first regime,
$b\leq32<128=d$.  This is an arithmetic observation, not a speed result: synchronization, factor
latency, register pressure, occupancy, and the number of dependent panels must be measured on H200.

\section{Exactness and causality}

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (panel equivalence).}
Under equations~\eqref{eq:process}--\eqref{eq:observation}, exact arithmetic evaluation of
equations~\eqref{eq:first-prediction}--\eqref{eq:boundary-covariance} gives the same outputs,
terminal posterior mean, and terminal posterior covariance as the tokenwise recursion
\eqref{eq:token-predict}--\eqref{eq:token-read}, for every panel width and every positive
observation-noise variance.

\paragraph{Reason.}
The panel equations construct the joint Gaussian distribution of the panel states and
observations.  Conditioning this joint distribution on the first $i$ observations gives the same
conditional distribution as applying $i$ scalar Gaussian conditioning steps.  The leading block
of a lower Cholesky factor is the Cholesky factor of the corresponding leading principal block,
so equation~\eqref{eq:causal-outputs} selects exactly those prefix conditionals.  The terminal
Schur complement gives equations~\eqref{eq:boundary-mean} and
\eqref{eq:boundary-covariance}.  A complete algebraic proof appears in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.

\paragraph{Proposition 2 (causality).}
Changing any future $\ve{k}_t,\ve{v}_t,\ve{q}_t,\ve{D}_t,\ve{\Omega}_t$, or $r_t$ with $t>i$
cannot change output $\ve{o}_i$.

\paragraph{Reason.}
The first $i$ rows and columns of $\ve{G}$ and $\ve{J}$ use only tokens $1{:}i$; the first $i$
entries of $\ve{Z}$ solve the corresponding leading triangular system.  Row $i$ of
$\tril(\ve{H}\ve{J}^{-\tr})$ has support only on columns $1{:}i$.  The full-panel boundary becomes
visible only to the next panel.

\section{Where approximation is allowed}

No statistical approximation is required by panelization itself.  If the exact structured
builder misses the end-to-end speed target, approximation is confined to the within-panel
process-noise kernel.

Let $\mathcal A$ be a fixed union of complete rotational modes and decompose
\begin{equation}
  \ve{Q}_{ij}^{W}
  =\ve{Q}_{ij}^{W,\mathcal A}
   +\ve{Q}_{ij}^{W,\overline{\mathcal A}}.
\end{equation}
Use $\ve{Q}^{W,\mathcal A}$ consistently in all three inferential objects:
\begin{equation}
  \ve{\Sigma}_W,
  \qquad
  \ve{H}_W,
  \qquad
  \ve{C}_W,
  \label{eq:matched-approximation}
\end{equation}
while retaining the full $\ve{Q}_b$ in the unconditional terminal covariance.  This defines a
coherent surrogate: active-mode noise occurs at its original token and is inferred, whereas
inactive-mode noise is injected independently at the boundary.  The family is nested:
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{active}}=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \text{delayed-noise exact-static ablation},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{active}}=d
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \text{exact dynamic conditioning}.
\end{equation}

The active set must be fixed before timing and training; selecting it using future panel values
would be noncausal.  A temporal approximation is also possible, but an arbitrary banding of a
covariance matrix need not remain positive semidefinite.  It must therefore be derived from an
explicit truncated-noise generative model rather than imposed entrywise.

The following shortcuts are not allowed:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item adding only $\diag(\ve{\Sigma}_W)$ without matching state--observation terms;
  \item adding $\ve{C}_W$ without the corresponding innovation covariance;
  \item correcting terminal conditioning while leaving causal reads under a different model;
  \item moving all dynamics to the panel boundary; or
  \item claiming that the delayed-noise covariance is calibrated to the tokenwise process.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Why the delayed-noise ablation fails}

The exact-static ablation makes the replacement
\begin{equation}
  \ve{\Sigma}_W\leftarrow\ve{0},
  \qquad
  \ve{H}_W\leftarrow\ve{0},
  \qquad
  \ve{C}_W\leftarrow\ve{0},
  \label{eq:delayed-noise}
\end{equation}
but still adds $\ve{Q}_b$ at publication.  It preserves the open-loop amount of terminal process
uncertainty, yet discards the fact that observations reveal part of that uncertainty.  No later
boundary addition can reconstruct the missing conditional mean.

The sealed FP64 decision used widths $1,2,4,8,16,32$, twenty held-out seeds, nine regimes, and
$T=256$.  The key failures were

\section{Implementation and evidence plan}

All development occurs under a new sandbox.  Existing production mixers and the sealed
delayed-noise evidence are read-only.

\subsection{Stage 1: independent FP64 references}

Implement three paths:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item the dense tokenwise recursion in equations~\eqref{eq:token-predict}--\eqref{eq:token-read};
  \item a source-loading joint-Gaussian oracle that does not use filtering or the panel
        identities; and
  \item the structured panel equations, first with explicit $\ve{u}_{i,j}$ and then with the
        intended streaming builder.
\end{enumerate}
Compare all outputs, every true panel boundary, and the final state for widths
$1,2,3,4,8,16,32$; angles $0,\pi/2,\pi$, random angles, and time-varying angles; radii
$0,0.05,0.999$ and random contractions; zero, small, and high process noise; repeated, collinear,
and zero keys; partial tails; arbitrary dense incoming covariance; and interior resets.  Required
controls are
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item $b=1$ equals tokenwise filtering;
  \item $\nu_{2:b}=0$ equals exact-static conditioning;
  \item the pair-tied $\theta=0$ case equals the frozen diagonal-transition direct-conditioning
        reference; and
  \item independent mutations deleting $\ve{\Sigma}_W$, $\ve{H}_W$, or $\ve{C}_W$ are each
        detected by a named failing test.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: causality and gradients}

Mutate every future input independently and prove that earlier outputs are unchanged.  Poison
future triangular storage and inactive tail lanes.  Run FP64 \texttt{gradcheck} and independent
vector--Jacobian comparisons for
\begin{equation}
  \ve{q},\ \ve{k},\ \ve{v},\ r,\ \rho,\ \theta,\ \nu,
  \ \ve{S}_0,\ \ve{P}_0,
\end{equation}
reporting every input separately.  Losses must touch current outputs, terminal mean, and terminal
covariance.  Symmetric $\ve{P}_0$ cotangents are part of the contract.

\subsection{Stage 3: FP32 numerical gate}

Use BF16 layer inputs promoted to FP32 state arithmetic.  Begin with IEEE FP32 and TF32 disabled.
Compare full $T=4096$ dependent sequences and gradients with FP64.  Immediately before Cholesky,
form $\ve{G}=\tfrac12(\ve{G}_{\mathrm{raw}}+\ve{G}_{\mathrm{raw}}^{\tr})$ from the fully
assembled static and dynamic terms.  Apart from this Gram symmetrization, symmetrize only complete
physical covariance matrices; do not silently jitter, clamp, or project eigenvalues.  Any retry
or fallback is part of the declared algorithm and final timing.  Causal prefix factors use an
unpivoted chronological Cholesky.  A panel-global jitter, pivot, or precision decision triggered by
a future row may not revise an earlier output; any causal-output retry must itself be prefix-local,
while a terminal-only retry may affect only the boundary published after the panel.
For a tensor with $n$ entries, require
\begin{equation}
  \|x-x_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_F
  \leq \mathrm{atol}\sqrt n+\mathrm{rtol}\|x_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_F.
\end{equation}
The proposed limits, to be sealed in a new manifest before any candidate results are inspected,
are $(2\times10^{-5},5\times10^{-4})$ for random outputs/means,
$(5\times10^{-5},10^{-3})$ for random covariances,
$(2\times10^{-4},5\times10^{-3})$ for stress outputs/means,
$(5\times10^{-4},10^{-2})$ for stress covariances, and
$(10^{-4},10^{-2})$ for each VJP.  Every boundary must satisfy
$\lambda_{\min}(\ve{P})\geq-10^{-5}\max(1,\lambda_{\max}(\ve{P}))$, with normalized
pre-symmetrization antisymmetric residual at most $10^{-4}$.  Any nonfinite value or Cholesky
failure rejects the arm.

\subsection{Stage 4: H200 feasibility attribution}

At production geometry $B=4,T=4096,D=2048,H=16,d=d_v=128$, compare five named arms:
the complete KDA layer; the runnable exact direct-conditioning fallback; the fastest same-source
delayed-noise solve with $256$ right-hand sides; a schedule-matched delayed-noise control padded to
$288$ right-hand sides; and structured-exact dynamic conditioning with $288$ right-hand sides.
The existing G5 work is a scheduling ancestor and a $\theta=0$ semantic/resource control, not a
headline speed baseline until its own full qualification is complete.  For context only, the old
runnable generic exact layer measured $77.3749$ ms versus $2.63338$ ms for KDA in a non-final
screen; its materializing builder/factor/solve/epilogue medians were
$54.937/7.600/6.980/4.659$ ms, while a prepared two-CTA $32$-row solve alone measured
$1.3401$ ms.  None of those historical component numbers is new candidate evidence.
The historical fallback uses the older transition model.  Before it can define a speed ratio, add
or rerun a correctness-qualified exact fallback on the identical rotational inputs and layer
peripherals; otherwise report the historical row only as context.

Attribute
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item transport and mode-variance prefixes;
  \item dense $\ve{P}^-\widetilde{\ve{K}}$;
  \item static Gram/read construction;
  \item the structured dynamic correction;
  \item factorization and combined triangular solve;
  \item causal output, mean commit, and dense covariance downdate; and
  \item the complete dependent panel loop.
\end{enumerate}
Profile $b\in\{4,8,16,32\}$, mode counts $M\in\{8,16,64\}$,
$T\in\{128,512,1024,4096\}$, and batch--head counts $1,6,16,64$.  The primary decision point is
$B=4,H=16,T=4096,b=32,d=d_v=128$.  A component sum is not a performance claim.  The decisive call is
one literal recurrence in which every panel consumes the actual dense boundary emitted by its
predecessor.  Report compiler resources, spills, allocator traffic, launch count, peak memory,
and achieved throughput.  A persistent outer scheduling tile may contain several statistical
panels, but it may not change equations~\eqref{eq:causal-outputs}--\eqref{eq:boundary-covariance}.
Compare an all-panels-parallel dynamic prepass with inline construction.  Attribute its packed
global storage and traffic explicitly; a fast prepass timed without its consumption by the
boundary recurrence is only a component result.
The inline arm must report zero unintended stack, local, spill, or global scratch and capacity for
at least $64$ active programs or clusters at batch--head $64$; the prepass arm targets at least two
resident CTAs per SM.  Both must account for the raw $(\rho,\theta)$ to rotation-coefficient
transform.  Phase-dead storage may be aliased only after a liveness audit proves the alias.

\subsection{Stage 5: exact versus controlled approximation}

First benchmark the structured-exact builder.  Only if it misses the frozen complete-layer target
should the mode-selective family in equation~\eqref{eq:matched-approximation} be evaluated.  Freeze
the active-mode sets and quality thresholds before reading held-out results.  A nontrivial
approximation must pass sequence-level output, boundary-mean, true-MSE, and language-model quality
gates; one-panel covariance agreement is insufficient.  The already rejected
$d_{\mathrm{active}}=0$ endpoint cannot be reconsidered under a new name.

\subsection{Stage 6: production admission}

Use a source-pinned, allocation-free, same-process interleaved AB/BA benchmark against the real
incumbent ExactKLA path and the complete KDA layer.  Median and minimum ratios must agree on the
decision.  Forward and forward--backward are separate gates, and mandatory predicates, tails,
symmetrization, empty fallback dispatches, and layer peripherals remain inside the timed call.
Use $15$ AB/BA warm-up pairs followed by $40$ measured pairs over identical preallocated inputs,
with a KDA sentinel every five pairs; reject more than $5\%$ first-to-last-quartile sentinel drift.
The following are new proposed admission gates for structured dynamic panels.  They are not
inherited from the historical ExactKLA contract and must be sealed before timing.  The complete
forward and forward--backward paths must each be at most $3.0\times$ KDA by both
median/median and minimum/minimum ratios, at least $4.0\times$ faster than the runnable exact
fallback, peak at most $2.0\times$ KDA allocated memory and below the fallback, and perform no
steady-state allocator calls.  ``Allocation-free'' means no calls after setup with the complete
workspace declared by the new forward/backward liveness witness; this includes, but is not limited
to, the $163$ MiB prepass payload.  A prepass design may use at most two forward core
launches (prepass plus dependent core); an inline persistent design may use one.  Backward
recomputes dynamic corrections and has its own complete timing and memory gate.
Only after correctness, gradients, numerical behavior, resources, memory, and speed pass may the
candidate be copied into \texttt{lit\_gpt/}.

\section{Limits}

The derivation does not prove that a fast kernel exists.  It removes a statistical approximation
by exploiting model structure, but still retains dense $O(Td^2)$ covariance arithmetic and a
dependent boundary recurrence.  Small-panel Cholesky latency, the $b+d+d_v$ right-hand-side solve,
limited batch--head parallelism, prepass traffic, rotation-coefficient preparation,
and backward checkpointing may dominate despite favorable operation counts.  Conversely, the
old generic dynamic builder timings do not directly reject this formulation because they
materialized substantially more transition-conditioned data and targeted larger dense solves.
Only the complete dependent measurement can decide.

The block-isotropic restriction applies to process noise, not to the posterior covariance.  A
general dense $\ve{\Omega}_t$ destroys the scalar mode recurrence in
equation~\eqref{eq:tau-recurrence}; it remains mathematically compatible with the panel equations
but not with the claimed structured-builder cost.  Likewise, state-dependent transitions that
are unknown before the scan fall outside this formulation.

\section{Proof of panel equivalence}
\label{app:proof}

For one value coordinate, stack the observations
$\ve{y}=[v_1,\ldots,v_b]^{\tr}$.  Equations~\eqref{eq:state-decomposition} and
\eqref{eq:observation} give
\begin{equation}
  v_i
  =\widetilde{\ve{k}}_i^{\tr}\ve{x}^\star
   +\ve{k}_i^{\tr}\ve{\xi}_i+e_i.
\end{equation}
The conditional mean of $\ve{y}$ before observing the panel is
$\widetilde{\ve{K}}^{\tr}\ve{X}^-$.  Independence of the entry-state error
$\ve{x}^\star-\ve{X}^-$, $\ve{w}_{2:b}$, and $\ve{e}_{1:b}$ gives
\begin{align}
  \Cov(v_i,v_j)
  &=\widetilde{\ve{k}}_i^{\tr}\ve{P}^-
    \widetilde{\ve{k}}_j
    +\ve{k}_i^{\tr}\ve{Q}_{ij}^{W}\ve{k}_j
    +\mathbf{1}_{\{i=j\}}r_i,
\end{align}
for $i\geq j$, with the other triangle obtained by symmetry.  This is
equation~\eqref{eq:dynamic-gram}.  For $j\leq i$,
\begin{align}
  \Cov(\ve{s}_i^\star,v_j)
  &=\Cov(\ve{\Phi}_i\ve{x}^\star+\ve{\xi}_i,
    \widetilde{\ve{k}}_j^{\tr}\ve{x}^\star
    +\ve{k}_j^{\tr}\ve{\xi}_j+e_j)\\
  &=\ve{\Phi}_i\ve{P}^-\widetilde{\ve{k}}_j
    +\ve{Q}_{ij}^{W}\ve{k}_j,
\end{align}
which is equation~\eqref{eq:state-observation-cross}.

For any jointly Gaussian variables $a$ and $y$,
\begin{equation}
  \E[a\mid y]
  =\E[a]+\Cov(a,y)\Cov(y)^{-1}(y-\E[y]),
  \label{eq:gaussian-conditional-mean}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \Cov(a\mid y)
  =\Cov(a)-\Cov(a,y)\Cov(y)^{-1}\Cov(y,a).
  \label{eq:gaussian-conditional-covariance}
\end{equation}
Taking $a=\ve{s}_b^\star$, using
$\ve{G}^{-1}=\ve{J}^{-\tr}\ve{J}^{-1}$, and defining
$\ve{B}_b=\ve{C}_b\ve{J}^{-\tr}$ yields
equations~\eqref{eq:boundary-mean}--\eqref{eq:boundary-covariance}.

For output $i$, apply equation~\eqref{eq:gaussian-conditional-mean} to
$a=\ve{q}_i^{\tr}\ve{s}_i^\star$ and the prefix $\ve{y}_{1:i}$.  The leading principal block
$\ve{J}_{1:i,1:i}$ factors $\ve{G}_{1:i,1:i}$.  The first $i$ entries of
$\ve{Z}$ equal $\ve{J}_{1:i,1:i}^{-1}\ve{\Delta}_{1:i}$, and the first $i$ entries of row $i$
of $\ve{H}\ve{J}^{-\tr}$ equal
$\ve{H}_{i,1:i}\ve{J}_{1:i,1:i}^{-\tr}$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:causal-outputs} follows.

Sequential Kalman filtering and joint Gaussian conditioning are two factorizations of the same
conditional distribution.  Applying the argument panel by panel proves equality for a complete
sequence, including partial final panels.

\section{The exact-static discrepancy}

Let $\ve{L}_X=\ve{P}^-\widetilde{\ve{K}}
(\ve{R}_v+\widetilde{\ve{K}}^{\tr}\ve{P}^-
\widetilde{\ve{K}})^{-1}$ be the block gain used by the delayed-noise surrogate, and set
\begin{equation}
  \ve{L}_b=\ve{\Phi}_b\ve{L}_X,
  \qquad
  \ve{A}_b=\ve{\Phi}_b
  (\ve{I}-\ve{L}_X\widetilde{\ve{K}}^{\tr}).
\end{equation}
If $\ve{E}^-$ is the actual incoming estimation-error second moment and
$\ve{P}^-$ is the covariance published by the surrogate, define
$\ve{\Delta}_P^-=\ve{E}^--\ve{P}^-$.  The true terminal MSE of the surrogate differs from its
published covariance by
\begin{equation}
  \ve{E}_b-\ve{P}_b^{\mathrm{static}}
  =\ve{A}_b\ve{\Delta}_P^-\ve{A}_b^{\tr}
   -\ve{L}_b\ve{C}_W^{\tr}
   -\ve{C}_W\ve{L}_b^{\tr}
   +\ve{L}_b\ve{\Sigma}_W\ve{L}_b^{\tr}.
\end{equation}
The difference need not be positive semidefinite: intermediate process noise both contaminates
the observations and contains information about the terminal state.  This identity explains why
adding the correct open-loop $\ve{Q}_b$ cannot calibrate a gain that ignored how the same noise
entered the observations.
