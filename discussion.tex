\section{Setup}

The diagonal Kalman Delta Network (KDN) predicts a diagonal covariance and uses it to form
an uncertainty-dependent delta-rule write. For key $\vct{k}_t$, transition
$\vct{\alpha}_t$, process noise $\vct{\omega}_t$, and observation noise $r_t$,
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\vct{p}}_t
    &= \vct{\alpha}_t^2\odot\vct{p}_{t-1}+\vct{\omega}_t, \\
  \vct{\kappa}_t
    &= \frac{\widehat{\vct{p}}_t\odot\vct{k}_t}
      {r_t+\sum_i\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2}.
\end{align}
The memory update remains a delta update,
\begin{equation}
  \vct{S}_t
  = \vct{D}_t\vct{S}_{t-1}
  + \vct{\kappa}_t
    \left(\vct{v}_t-(\vct{D}_t\vct{S}_{t-1})^\top\vct{k}_t\right)^\top,
  \qquad \vct{D}_t=\diag(\vct{\alpha}_t).
\end{equation}
The implementation additionally tracks diagonal information $\vct{c}_t=1/\vct{p}_t$ with
the calibrated projected update
\begin{equation}
  \vct{c}_t
  = \frac{1}{\widehat{\vct{p}}_t}
  + \frac{d_k}{r_t}\vct{k}_t^2.
\end{equation}
This is a scan-compatible diagonal-information approximation, not an exact diagonal Kalman
posterior.

\section{The Scale Symmetry}
\label{sec:discussion-scale-symmetry}

For any constant $\lambda>0$, consider
\begin{equation}
  \vct{p}_t' = \lambda\vct{p}_t,
  \qquad
  \vct{\omega}_t'=\lambda\vct{\omega}_t,
  \qquad
  r_t'=\lambda r_t,
  \qquad
  \mu'=\frac{\mu}{\lambda},
\end{equation}
where $\mu=\vct{c}_0$ is the initial information and hence
$\vct{p}_0=1/\mu$. The predicted covariance scales as
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\vct{p}}_t'
  = \vct{\alpha}_t^2\odot(\lambda\vct{p}_{t-1})
    +\lambda\vct{\omega}_t
  = \lambda\widehat{\vct{p}}_t.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the gain is invariant:
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_t'
  = \frac{\lambda\widehat{\vct{p}}_t\odot\vct{k}_t}
    {\lambda r_t+\sum_i\lambda\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2}
  = \vct{\kappa}_t.
\end{equation}
The information update also transforms consistently as
$\vct{c}_t'=\vct{c}_t/\lambda$. Therefore $\vct{\omega}$, $r$, and $\mu$
contain a common covariance-scale degree of freedom that cannot be identified from the memory
output. Positive floors weaken the symmetry near their boundaries but do not remove the general
conditioning problem.

The transition $\vct{\alpha}_t$ is different. It affects both covariance prediction and the
memory transition $\vct{D}_t\vct{S}_{t-1}$, so it is not part of the exact scale symmetry.
Nevertheless, $\vct{\alpha}_t$ and $\vct{\omega}_t$ are partially confounded in the covariance
path because multiple pairs can produce similar $\widehat{\vct{p}}_t$.

\section{What the Noisy-MQAR Results Establish}

The original diagonal model obtains $76.2\pm22.3$ percent over the $5\times5$ initialization
and data grid. Its initialization component of variation (19.2 points) is larger than its data
component (11.4 points). This is not uniform under-capacity: many cells reach 88--96 percent,
while a few fail severely.

The most informative intervention is isotropic gain initialization. Zeroing the output map of
the process-noise projection makes $\vct{\omega}_t$ uniform across channels at initialization,
after which anisotropy is learned. At the same learning rate and with the same optimizer, this
changes performance from
\begin{equation}
  76.2\pm22.3 \quad\longrightarrow\quad 83.7\pm10.4,
\end{equation}
and halves the initialization variation from 19.2 to 9.0 points. It also rescues both severe
collapse cells. Thus the current evidence identifies random initial gain anisotropy as the
primary failure, not the nominal learning rate or scale symmetry.

The scale symmetry remains relevant as a secondary optimization defect. It creates a flat
direction, gives equivalent physical models different raw parameter values, and lets coupled
quantities evolve under different optimizer geometries. In the current Muon setup, matrix
weights use Muon with adjusted learning rates, while biases and the one-dimensional prior
parameter use AdamW. A single printed learning rate therefore does not imply uniform motion of
the physical uncertainty variables.

The prior $\mu$ is unlikely to explain late failures. It only controls the initial information,
and its influence should decay rapidly over a long sequence when process noise has a positive
floor. Learning it adds a weakly identified parameter with little expected benefit.

\section{Ways to Remove or Reduce the Symmetry}

\subsection{Fix the observation-noise scale}

The strongest and simplest gauge choice is
\begin{equation}
  r_t\equiv1, \qquad \mu\equiv1.
\end{equation}
The model then learns process uncertainty in units of observation noise:
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\omega}_t
  = \omega_{\min}+\operatorname{softplus}(f_\omega(\vct{x}_t)).
\end{equation}
Because $r$ can no longer be rescaled, the common covariance-scale symmetry is removed. This
parameterization is the preferred baseline unless token-dependent observation reliability is
shown empirically to matter.

\subsection{Learn bounded relative observation reliability}

If heteroscedastic observation noise is useful, it should change confidence relative to a fixed
reference rather than learn an unrestricted global scale. One option is
\begin{equation}
  r_t=\exp\!\left(s\tanh f_r(\vct{x}_t)\right),
\end{equation}
with fixed $s$. For $s=\log4$, $r_t\in[1/4,4]$. The projection should have no learnable global
bias, and the prior can remain fixed at $\mu=1$. A soft anchoring penalty can further control
drift,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scale}}
  = \gamma\left(\mathbb{E}[\log r_t]\right)^2.
\end{equation}
This reduces rather than mathematically eliminates every possible input-dependent rescaling,
but it prevents the practically harmful unbounded common-scale direction.

\subsection{Parameterize a stationary uncertainty level}

The $\vct{\alpha}$--$\vct{\omega}$ interaction can be made more interpretable with
\begin{equation}
  \omega_{t,i}
  = \omega_{\min}+(1-\alpha_{t,i}^2)\rho_{t,i},
  \qquad
  \rho_{t,i}=\operatorname{softplus}(f_\rho(\vct{x}_t)).
\end{equation}
Here $\alpha$ controls retention and $\rho$ is the uncertainty level toward which prediction
moves. This is exactly the completed \texttt{DiagKLA1} experiment. Coupling alone changes
$76.2\pm22.3$ to $76.9\pm20.9$, which is effectively a wash. The completed
\texttt{DiagKLA1-zi} experiment combines coupling with isotropic gain initialization and obtains
$80.1\pm13.3$, below the simpler \texttt{DiagKLA-zi} result of $83.7\pm10.4$. Thus this
parameterization has already been tested: it neither replaces isotropic initialization nor
improves on free additive process noise once that initialization is used.

\section{Why DiagKLA Does Not Yet Dominate IsoKLA}

The available implementation tests establish that the Triton kernels reproduce the intended
PyTorch recurrence; they do not establish that this recurrence is the best approximation for an
associative memory. Empirically, the extra diagonal uncertainty state has not produced a general
advantage over the isotropic state. This section separates the approximation question from the
kernel-correctness question and states the experiment needed to identify the useful part of the
diagonal model.

\subsection{Current evidence}

The controlled 220M/20B information-scale runs do not establish a general DiagKLA advantage.
The best default-calibrated variants give:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Metric & DiagKLA, $s=d_k$ & IsoKLA, $s=1$ \\
\midrule
WikiText word perplexity $\downarrow$ & 28.99 & 28.79 \\
LAMBADA perplexity $\downarrow$ & 35.12 & 35.66 \\
Five-task multiple-choice average $\uparrow$ & 0.472 & 0.477 \\
S-NIAH-2 at 4K $\uparrow$ & 0.272 & 0.046 \\
S-NIAH-3 at 2K $\uparrow$ & 0.004 & 0.008 \\
MK-NIAH-1 at 4K $\uparrow$ & 0.178 & 0.182 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
General language-modeling quality is mixed and effectively tied. DiagKLA has a large, specific
S-NIAH-2 advantage, a direction that also appears in the earlier 220M comparison, but it does
not improve the harder single-key or multikey probes. The recent sweeps use one training seed
and different GPU world sizes, and a current-code 750M DiagKLA evaluation is not yet available.
The evidence therefore supports a task-specific single-key retrieval signal, not a general
DiagKLA ranking above IsoKLA.

The noisy-MQAR grid gives a similar result. Uniform process-noise initialization raises DiagKLA
from $76.2\pm22.3$ to $83.7\pm10.4$, while the best stabilized IsoKLA variant obtains
$83.0\pm8.4$. These are effectively tied at this resolution. The older trained-gain probe that
reports large off-key gain and over-writing predates the current initialization and memory
kernel; it motivates the mechanism test below but should not be treated as a diagnosis of the
current default.

\subsection{Approximation and calibration}

With predicted diagonal covariance
$\widehat{\vct{P}}_t=\diag(\widehat{\vct{p}}_t)$, the exact Gaussian measurement update produces
a dense posterior whose diagonal is
\begin{equation}
  p^{\mathrm{exact}}_{t,i}
  = \widehat p_{t,i}
  - \frac{\widehat p_{t,i}^2 k_{t,i}^2}
    {r_t+\sum_j\widehat p_{t,j}k_{t,j}^2}.
  \label{eq:discussion-exact-posterior-diagonal}
\end{equation}
The scan-compatible DiagKLA state instead uses
\begin{equation}
  p^{\mathrm{proj}}_{t,i}
  = \left(\widehat p_{t,i}^{-1}
    +s\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}\right)^{-1},
  \qquad s=d_k \quad\text{by default}.
  \label{eq:discussion-projected-posterior-diagonal}
\end{equation}
Even at $s=1$, Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-projected-posterior-diagonal} is not the diagonal of
the exact posterior in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-posterior-diagonal}. For $s\ne1$, the
gain and information update additionally correspond to different measurement scales: the
current gain retains precision $1/r_t$, while the state used by future gains receives precision
$s/r_t$. Thus $s=d_k$ is a dimension calibration of a gain-conditioning state, not an exact
Kalman consequence.

For a spread-out unit key, $\mathbb{E}[k_i^2]=1/d_k$, so $s=d_k$ gives a unit expected
per-coordinate increment. Averaging these calibrated coordinates recovers the IsoKLA scale,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{d_k}\sum_i d_k\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}
  = \frac{\lVert\vct{k}_t\rVert_2^2}{r_t}
  = \frac{1}{r_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-diag-iso-average}
\end{equation}
Matching mean information does not match covariance or gain because inversion is nonlinear.
Moreover, for a uniformly distributed unit key,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Var}(d_k k_i^2)
  = \frac{2(d_k-1)}{d_k+2}
  \longrightarrow 2.
  \label{eq:discussion-coordinate-information-variance}
\end{equation}
The coordinate noise therefore does not vanish with width. DiagKLA can interpret coordinate
energy fluctuations as persistent uncertainty structure. The projection is also basis
dependent: $(1,1)/\sqrt{2}$ and $(1,-1)/\sqrt{2}$ are orthogonal but have identical elementwise
squares and hence identical information increments. Whether the learned key basis aligns this
diagonal statistic with useful uncertainty is an empirical question.

\subsection{From exact Gaussian filtering to an online variational M\"obius state}
\label{subsec:discussion-projection-derivation}

At $s=1$, the projected rule is exactly a mean-field variational-inference update, not merely a
Gaussian-inspired heuristic. Each token defines a dense Gaussian filtering posterior, and that
posterior is projected onto a diagonal Gaussian family by minimizing the reverse KL divergence.
Repeating this projection after every token gives an online variational filter. The production
factor $s=d_k$ is a separate calibration applied after this variational derivation; it is not the
optimum of the same variational objective.

To make the variational step explicit, use the memory notation of the main paper. Let
$\vct{S}_t^\star\in\mathbb{R}^{d_k\times d_v}$ be the random latent associative memory and let
$\vct{S}_t$ be its posterior-mean estimate. Fix a value coordinate $j$, and denote column $j$ of
these matrices by $\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star$ and $\vct{s}_{t,j}$, respectively. Thus the latent vector
in the following scalar observation model is not a new state: it is one column of
$\vct{S}_t^\star$. Its predicted model is
\begin{equation}
  \vct{s}_{t,j}^\star\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
  \sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j},\widehat{\vct{P}}_t),
  \qquad
  v_{t,j}=\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star+e_{t,j},
  \qquad
  e_{t,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,r_t),
  \label{eq:discussion-scalar-observation-model}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j}$ is column $j$ of the predicted mean
$\widehat{\vct{S}}_t$. Let $\pi_{t,j}$ denote the exact one-step posterior density of
$\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star$ after observing $v_{t,j}$. Its negative log density, evaluated at a possible
column value $\vct{s}$, has quadratic part
\begin{equation}
  -\log \pi_{t,j}(\vct{s})
  =\frac{1}{2}(\vct{s}-\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j})^\top
    \widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{-1}
    (\vct{s}-\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j})
  +\frac{(v_{t,j}-\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{s})^2}{2r_t}
  +\mathrm{const}.
  \label{eq:discussion-posterior-objective}
\end{equation}
Its Hessian gives the exact posterior information matrix
\begin{equation}
  \vct{C}_t^\star
  =(\vct{P}_t^\star)^{-1}
  =\widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{-1}
  +\frac{1}{r_t}\vct{k}_t\vct{k}_t^\top.
  \label{eq:discussion-exact-information-update}
\end{equation}
Applying the Woodbury identity to Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-information-update}
recovers Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-posterior-diagonal}. Its mean is
\begin{equation}
  \vct{s}_{t,j}
  =\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j}
  +\vct{\kappa}_t
    \left(v_{t,j}-\vct{k}_t^\top\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j}\right),
  \label{eq:discussion-single-column-mean-update}
\end{equation}
which is column $j$ of the matrix update for $\vct{S}_t$ in the setup section. Thus
$\vct{S}_t$ is the point estimate (posterior mean), whereas $\vct{P}_t^\star$ describes the
uncertainty around each of its columns. Although this one-step posterior is analytically
tractable, carrying its dense covariance would destroy the independent per-coordinate state used
by the associative scan. Variational inference enters here as a structured computational
approximation.

Choose the mean-field Gaussian family for this memory column,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{Q}
  =\left\{
    q_{t,j}(\vct{s})
    =\mathcal{N}\!\left(\vct{\mu}_{t,j},\diag(\vct{p}_t)\right)
    :\vct{p}_t>0
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:discussion-mean-field-family}
\end{equation}
For the one-token model in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-scalar-observation-model}, its ELBO is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{t,j}(q_{t,j})
  =\mathbb{E}_{q_{t,j}}\!\left[
    \log p(v_{t,j}\mid\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star)
  \right]
  -\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
    q_{t,j}(\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star)\,\|\,
    p(\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})
  \right).
  \label{eq:discussion-one-step-elbo}
\end{equation}
The standard variational identity gives
\begin{equation}
  \log p(v_{t,j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})
  =\mathcal{L}_{t,j}(q_{t,j})
  +\operatorname{KL}(q_{t,j}\,\|\,\pi_{t,j}).
  \label{eq:discussion-elbo-kl-identity}
\end{equation}
Therefore the variational update can be written equivalently as
\begin{equation}
  q_{t,j}^{\mathrm{VI}}
  =\arg\max_{q_{t,j}\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathcal{L}_{t,j}(q_{t,j})
  =\arg\min_{q_{t,j}\in\mathcal{Q}}
    \operatorname{KL}(q_{t,j}\,\|\,\pi_{t,j}).
  \label{eq:discussion-online-vi-objective}
\end{equation}
Because the target $\pi_{t,j}=\mathcal{N}(\vct{s}_{t,j},\vct{P}_t^\star)$ is Gaussian, this KL
reduces, up to terms independent of $\vct{\mu}_{t,j}$ and $\vct{p}_t$, to
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{KL}(q_{t,j}\,\|\,\pi_{t,j})
  =\frac{1}{2}(\vct{\mu}_{t,j}-\vct{s}_{t,j})^\top\vct{C}_t^\star
    (\vct{\mu}_{t,j}-\vct{s}_{t,j})
  +\frac{1}{2}\sum_i
    \left(C_{t,ii}^\star p_{t,i}-\log p_{t,i}\right)
  +\mathrm{const}.
  \label{eq:discussion-reverse-kl-objective}
\end{equation}
Since $\vct{C}_t^\star$ is positive definite, the mean optimum is the exact filtering mean,
$\vct{\mu}_{t,j}=\vct{s}_{t,j}$. The variance objective separates over key coordinates $i$, and
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial}{\partial p_{t,i}}
  \operatorname{KL}(q_{t,j}\,\|\,\pi_{t,j})
  =\frac{1}{2}\left(C_{t,ii}^\star-\frac{1}{p_{t,i}}\right),
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial^2}{\partial p_{t,i}^2}
  \operatorname{KL}(q_{t,j}\,\|\,\pi_{t,j})
  =\frac{1}{2p_{t,i}^2}>0.
  \label{eq:discussion-reverse-kl-coordinate-derivative}
\end{equation}
The positive second derivative shows that the stationary point is the unique minimum. Setting the
first derivative to zero gives $C_{t,ii}^\star=1/p_{t,i}$ and hence
\begin{equation}
  p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{rev}}
  =\frac{1}{C_{t,ii}^\star}
  =\left(\widehat p_{t,i}^{-1}+\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}\right)^{-1}.
  \label{eq:discussion-reverse-kl-projection}
\end{equation}
The final equality follows directly from the diagonal of the exact information update,
$C_{t,ii}^\star=\widehat p_{t,i}^{-1}+k_{t,i}^2/r_t$.
Thus Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-projected-posterior-diagonal} at $s=1$ is exactly the
reverse-KL mean-field variational optimum: diagonalize the posterior precision and then invert.
Using the previous diagonal approximation to form the next predictive prior and repeating this
ELBO optimization token by token gives online mean-field variational inference. It is a
reverse-KL project-after-each-token filter, rather than a single batch approximation to the full
latent trajectory.

For comparison, the forward-KL, or moment, projection onto the same diagonal family satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \arg\min_{\vct{p}>0}
  \operatorname{KL}(\pi_{t,j}\,\|\,q_{t,j})
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  p_{t,i}=P_{t,ii}^\star=p^{\mathrm{exact}}_{t,i}.
  \label{eq:discussion-forward-kl-projection}
\end{equation}
This inclusive-KL projection matches the exact marginal variances, but it is not the
ELBO/reverse-KL variational objective above. The two projections differ because diagonalization
and inversion do not commute,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{C_{t,ii}^\star}
  \ne
  \left[(\vct{C}_t^\star)^{-1}\right]_{ii}
  =P_{t,ii}^\star
  \qquad\text{in general}.
  \label{eq:discussion-projection-inversion-noncommute}
\end{equation}
For a Gaussian posterior these two quantities have different meanings:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{C_{t,ii}^\star}
  =\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{t,j}}
    \!\left((\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star)_i
      \mid(\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star)_{-i}\right),
  \qquad
  P_{t,ii}^\star
  =\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{t,j}}
    \!\left((\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star)_i\right).
  \label{eq:discussion-conditional-versus-marginal-variance}
\end{equation}
The reverse-KL diagonal approximation therefore carries conditional variances, as if the other
coordinates were known, rather than their larger marginal variances. This is why it is generally
overconfident when the exact posterior contains cross-coordinate correlations.
Indeed, Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-diag-information} shows that marginalizing the other
coordinates replaces $r_t$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-reverse-kl-projection} by
$r_t+\sum_{\ell\ne i}\widehat p_{t,\ell}k_{t,\ell}^2$. Consequently
$p^{\mathrm{rev}}_{t,i}\leq p^{\mathrm{exact}}_{t,i}$, with equality if $k_{t,i}=0$ or if the
other coordinates contribute no key-weighted uncertainty. The selected projection is therefore
systematically no less confident than the exact marginal.

The computational payoff of this mean-field variational family appears after returning to
information variables. Define
\begin{equation}
  c_{t-1,i}=p_{t-1,i}^{-1},
  \qquad
  u_{t,i}=s\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-projected-info-increment}
\end{equation}
Diagonal prediction followed by the projected measurement update gives
\begin{align}
  \widehat c_{t,i}
  &=\frac{c_{t-1,i}}
    {\alpha_{t,i}^2+\omega_{t,i}c_{t-1,i}}, \\
  c_{t,i}
  &=\widehat c_{t,i}+u_{t,i}
  =\frac{(1+u_{t,i}\omega_{t,i})c_{t-1,i}
      +u_{t,i}\alpha_{t,i}^2}
    {\omega_{t,i}c_{t-1,i}+\alpha_{t,i}^2}.
  \label{eq:discussion-projected-info-mobius}
\end{align}
Each token therefore supplies an input-only $2\times2$ matrix
\begin{equation}
  \vct{M}_{t,i}
  =\begin{pmatrix}
    1+u_{t,i}\omega_{t,i} & u_{t,i}\alpha_{t,i}^2\\[2pt]
    \omega_{t,i} & \alpha_{t,i}^2
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  u_{t,i}=s\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-mobius-matrix}
\end{equation}
M\"obius maps compose by matrix multiplication, so the prefix product
$\vct{M}_{t,i}\cdots\vct{M}_{1,i}$ supplies every information state by an associative scan,
independently for each channel. The production kernel implements this as chunk-local map
composition, a short carry scan, and a chunk-local emit pass. The cross-channel sum in the current gain is
evaluated only after the exclusive information prefixes are available, so it is a token-local
reduction rather than part of the recurrence. By contrast, the moment projection makes each
coordinate's information increment depend on all the other predicted variances, as in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-diag-information}; it retains $O(d_k)$ state but loses these
independent input-only M\"obius maps.

The variational optimum above has $s=1$. Production deliberately departs from that optimum and
uses $s=d_k$ as a width calibration. The keys are $\ell_2$ normalized, so
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{d_k}\sum_i k_{t,i}^2=\frac{1}{d_k},
  \qquad
  \mathbb{E}[k_{t,i}^2]=\frac{1}{d_k}
  \quad\text{for exchangeable coordinates}.
  \label{eq:discussion-normalized-key-coordinate-scale}
\end{equation}
The literal projection therefore adds only $1/(d_kr_t)$ information to a typical coordinate.
Standardizing a coordinate to unit second moment,
$\widetilde k_{t,i}=\sqrt{d_k}\,k_{t,i}$, gives
\begin{equation}
  u_{t,i}=\frac{\widetilde k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}
  =d_k\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t},
  \label{eq:discussion-standardized-coordinate-update}
\end{equation}
which is precisely $s=d_k$. Equivalently, in the symmetric case
$\widehat p_{t,i}=\widehat p_t$ and $k_{t,i}^2=1/d_k$,
\begin{equation}
  p^{\mathrm{proj},s}_{t,i}
  =\frac{\widehat p_t}{1+(s/d_k)\widehat p_t/r_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-symmetric-projected-scale}
\end{equation}
Matching the scalar normalized-key update
$(\widehat p_t^{-1}+r_t^{-1})^{-1}$ selects $s=d_k$. This is the same mean-scale matching stated
in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-diag-iso-average}, now expressed as a feature standardization and
a covariance-timescale match.

This calibration is not variational inference for the original observation model. At $s=d_k$,
the total added diagonal precision is $d_k/r_t$, whereas the trace of the exact rank-one increment
is only $1/r_t$. One could obtain the scaled precision from the power-posterior ELBO for memory
column $j$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{t,j}^{(s)}(q_{t,j})
  =s\,\mathbb{E}_{q_{t,j}}\!\left[
    \log p(v_{t,j}\mid\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star)
  \right]
  -\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
    q_{t,j}(\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star)\,\|\,
    p(\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})
  \right),
  \label{eq:discussion-power-posterior-elbo}
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to replacing $r_t$ by $r_t/s$. Such a coherent tempered variational update
would use $r_t/s$ in both the current gain and the posterior covariance. DiagKLA instead keeps
$r_t$ in the current gain and applies $s$ only to the information state passed forward.

The production formulation should therefore be read as two distinct operations: (i) at $s=1$,
a reverse-KL mean-field variational projection that preserves a positive $O(d_k)$ state and an
associative gain scan; and (ii) after that derivation, a $d_k$ coordinate calibration that makes
the future-confidence timescale independent of head width. The resulting state is a calibrated
gain-conditioning state, not the ordinary-ELBO covariance---or exact covariance---of one coherent
linear-Gaussian model. Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-split-variational-objective} below gives a
generalized variational objective for this deliberate separation.

\subsection{Information scale separates present writing from future protection}
\label{subsec:discussion-info-scale-temporal}

The role of $s$ is clearest after separating the memory update at token $t$ from the uncertainty
state passed to later tokens. Define the key-weighted predicted variance and the
\emph{effective write strength}
\begin{equation}
  z_t=\sum_i\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2,
  \qquad
  \beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}
  =\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\kappa}_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-effective-write}
\end{equation}
Contracting the diagonal gain with its own key evaluates $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ in closed form,
\begin{equation}
  \beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}
  =\sum_i k_{t,i}
    \frac{\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}}
      {r_t+\sum_j\widehat p_{t,j}k_{t,j}^2}
  =\frac{\sum_i\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2}{r_t+z_t}
  =\frac{z_t}{r_t+z_t},
  \qquad
  1-\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{r_t}{r_t+z_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-effective-write-closed}
\end{equation}
Positive variances and observation noise give $z_t\geq0$ and $r_t>0$, hence
$\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\in[0,1)$.

\paragraph{Residual contraction.}
Write $\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t=\vct{D}_t\vct{S}_{t-1}$ for the decayed memory and
$\vct{e}_t=\vct{v}_t-\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t$ for the prediction error that the delta
rule corrects, so that the memory recurrence is the rank-one update
\begin{equation}
  \vct{S}_t
  =\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t+\vct{\kappa}_t\vct{e}_t^\top.
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-rank-one}
\end{equation}
Transposing Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-memory-rank-one} gives
$\vct{S}_t^\top=\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t^\top+\vct{e}_t\vct{\kappa}_t^\top$. Reading the updated
memory at the same key, the scalar $\vct{\kappa}_t^\top\vct{k}_t=\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ factors
out of the rank-one term:
\begin{align}
  \vct{S}_t^\top\vct{k}_t
  &=\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t
    +\vct{e}_t\left(\vct{\kappa}_t^\top\vct{k}_t\right)
  =\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t
    +\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\vct{e}_t,
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-readout}
  \\
  \vct{v}_t-\vct{S}_t^\top\vct{k}_t
  &=\bigl(\underbrace{\vct{v}_t-\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t}_{=\,\vct{e}_t}\bigr)
    -\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\vct{e}_t
  =\left(1-\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\right)\vct{e}_t
  =\frac{r_t}{r_t+z_t}\,\vct{e}_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-residual}
\end{align}
Thus $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the fraction of the current residual corrected at its own key.
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-memory-residual} is the delta-rule instance of the standard Kalman
innovation contraction $\vct{I}-\vct{K}_t\vct{H}_t$, which for the scalar-key observation
$\vct{H}_t=\vct{k}_t^\top$ collapses to the scalar $1-\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\kappa}_t$. Vanilla
DeltaNet is the special case $\vct{\kappa}_t=\beta_t\vct{k}_t$ with
$\lVert\vct{k}_t\rVert_2=1$, which recovers $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}=\beta_t$; here the same
quantity is instead an emergent function $z_t/(r_t+z_t)$ of the uncertainty state.

Two properties of Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-memory-residual} are used below. First, the same
$\vct{\kappa}_t$ controls both the value write $+\vct{\kappa}_t\vct{v}_t^\top$ and the erasure
$-\vct{\kappa}_t\vct{k}_t^\top\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t$, so a gain that assimilates the current
observation more strongly necessarily also erases more of what the memory already held at that
key. Second, the contraction depends on $\vct{\kappa}_t$ only through its component along
$\vct{k}_t$: any off-key part $\vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp}$ satisfies
$\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp}=0$ and leaves
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-memory-residual} unchanged while still perturbing the readout at
other keys. The same-token residual is therefore blind to exactly the degree of freedom that
distinguishes DiagKLA from IsoKLA, which is what
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-gain-direction-ablation} is designed to isolate.

To expose causal ordering, temporarily allow a token-specific scale $s_t$. Current DiagKLA first
computes $\widehat{\vct{p}}_t$ and $\vct{\kappa}_t$ from the incoming state
$\vct{c}_{t-1}$, and only afterward computes the tracked projected covariance below. In this
subsection, plain $\vct{p}_t$ denotes that DiagKLA state, not the exact posterior diagonal.
\begin{equation}
  p_{t,i}(s_t)
  =\left(\widehat p_{t,i}^{-1}
    +s_t\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}\right)^{-1}
  =\frac{\widehat p_{t,i}}
    {1+s_t\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2/r_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-current-scale-update}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for a local intervention that holds the incoming state and token projections fixed,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\vct{\kappa}_t}{\partial s_t}=0,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial\vct{S}_t}{\partial s_t}=0,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial p_{t,i}}{\partial s_t}
  =-\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}p_{t,i}^2\leq0.
  \label{eq:discussion-current-scale-derivatives}
\end{equation}
The scale at token $t$ therefore does not write that observation harder or change its same-token
residual. It only increases the confidence state supplied to the future. In causal order,
$\vct{c}_{t-1}$ determines $\widehat{\vct{p}}_t$, which determines
$\vct{\kappa}_t$ and $\vct{S}_t$; $s_t$ then enters $\vct{c}_t$ and can first affect the
memory gain at token $t+1$.

This token-local statement must not be confused with a comparison between two complete runs that
use different global values of $s$. Except at the first token, a global $s$ has already changed
$\vct{c}_{t-1}$ and therefore generally changes the current gain. Conditional on fixed projected
$\vct{k}$, $\vct{\alpha}$, $\vct{\omega}$, and $r$, its first delayed effect is explicit:
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial z_{t+1}}{\partial s_t}
  &=-\frac{1}{r_t}\sum_i
    \alpha_{t+1,i}^2p_{t,i}^2k_{t,i}^2k_{t+1,i}^2\leq0, \\
  \frac{\partial\beta_{t+1}^{\mathrm{eff}}}{\partial s_t}
  &=\frac{r_{t+1}}{(r_{t+1}+z_{t+1})^2}
    \frac{\partial z_{t+1}}{\partial s_t}\leq0.
  \label{eq:discussion-delayed-scale-effect}
\end{align}
More generally, for $u>t$ the channelwise sensitivity propagates as
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial p_{u,i}}{\partial s_t}
  =-\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}p_{t,i}^2
    \prod_{n=t+1}^{u}
    \frac{\alpha_{n,i}^2}
      {(1+s_n\widehat p_{n,i}k_{n,i}^2/r_n)^2}.
  \label{eq:discussion-scale-sensitivity-propagation}
\end{equation}
A larger past scale therefore reduces the later effective correction
$\vct{k}_u^\top\vct{\kappa}_u$. It need not reduce every component or the norm of an
anisotropic $\vct{\kappa}_u$: reducing the numerator coordinates also reduces their shared
denominator. End-to-end trained runs need not be monotone either, because their learned
projections can compensate for the changed recurrence.

Equations~\eqref{eq:discussion-delayed-scale-effect}--\eqref{eq:discussion-scale-sensitivity-propagation}
also show what is being protected. Suppression is controlled by elementwise-energy overlap such
as $\sum_i p_{t,i}^2k_{t,i}^2k_{u,i}^2$, not by the signed directional overlap
$(\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{k}_u)^2$. At fixed projections, a future key with disjoint coordinate
support receives no direct effective-gain suppression through this pathway, although the full
memory can still differ through intervening writes. Dense orthogonal keys can instead have large
energy overlap, including the maximum compatible with orthogonality in the two-dimensional
example above. The tracker gives those two keys identical confidence increments and, under a
symmetric incoming covariance, identical effective-gain suppression. Their signed memory updates
and residuals need not be identical. Large $s$ can consequently suppress a genuinely new
orthogonal association rather than selectively preserve only the old one.

Prediction limits the duration of this protection. As $s_t\to\infty$, $p_{t,i}\to0$ wherever
$k_{t,i}\ne0$, but
\begin{equation}
  \widehat p_{t+1,i}=\alpha_{t+1,i}^2p_{t,i}+\omega_{t+1,i}
  \longrightarrow\omega_{t+1,i}.
  \label{eq:discussion-process-noise-plasticity-floor}
\end{equation}
Positive process noise restores a floor on uncertainty and hence future plasticity; small
$\alpha$ also rapidly attenuates the confidence sensitivity in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-scale-sensitivity-propagation} while directly decaying the memory.
Thus larger $s$ does not store more content or create capacity by itself. It changes the
retention--plasticity operating point until $\vct{\omega}$ and $\vct{\alpha}$ wash out that
change. If $\vct{\omega}=0$ and $\vct{\alpha}\simeq1$, an extreme $s$ can instead freeze future
gains on the affected coordinates.

The different scale sensitivities of DiagKLA and IsoKLA are consistent with their different
recurrences. For a spread-out normalized key, the DiagKLA increment in one coordinate has expectation
$s/(d_kr_t)$, so $s=d_k$ cancels the coordinate dilution without changing the current DiagKLA
gain locally. IsoKLA has one scalar state and $\lVert\vct{k}_t\rVert_2^2=1$, so its information
increment is already $s/r_t$ with no $1/d_k$ dilution. Moreover, the current IsoKLA
implementation uses, locally holding the incoming $\widehat b_t$ fixed,
\begin{equation}
  \beta_t^{\mathrm{iso}}(s)
  =\frac{\widehat b_t}{r_t+s\widehat b_t},
  \qquad
  c_t^{\mathrm{iso}}(s)=\widehat b_t^{-1}+\frac{s}{r_t},
  \qquad
  \left.\frac{\partial\beta_t^{\mathrm{iso}}}{\partial s}\right|_{\widehat b_t}
  =-\frac{\widehat b_t^2}{(r_t+s\widehat b_t)^2}<0.
  \label{eq:discussion-iso-scale}
\end{equation}
A token-local increase in $s$ in IsoKLA therefore weakens the current write and increases future
confidence at the same time. With fixed projections, the lower uncertainty inherited from past
larger scales reinforces this reduction in the global-$s$ recurrence. At $s=d_k$ these two
anti-plasticity effects can be excessive, whereas DiagKLA obtains delayed protection without the
same local write penalty. This gives a mechanistic interpretation of why the production defaults
$s=d_k$ and $s=1$ behave differently. The corrected 220M sweeps do not establish unique optima:
general quality is nearly flat across scales, while larger $s$ trades toward stronger long-context
retention. This is not yet causal evidence that retention alone produces the downstream differences.

A coherent Gaussian observation-precision multiplier has a different meaning. For a local
intervention that holds the predicted prior $\widehat{\vct{P}}_t$ fixed, increasing precision by
$s$ means using $r_t^{\mathrm{eff}}=r_t/s$ in both the current gain and posterior. With
$z_t=\vct{k}_t^\top\widehat{\vct{P}}_t\vct{k}_t$, this gives
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{coh}}(s)
  =\frac{s\widehat{\vct{P}}_t\vct{k}_t}{r_t+sz_t},
  \qquad
  \beta_t^{\mathrm{coh}}(s)
  =\frac{sz_t}{r_t+sz_t},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial\beta_t^{\mathrm{coh}}}{\partial s}
  =\frac{r_tz_t}{(r_t+sz_t)^2}>0.
  \label{eq:discussion-coherent-scale-gain}
\end{equation}
The remaining current residual is then
$1-\beta_t^{\mathrm{coh}}=r_t/(r_t+sz_t)$, so a more precise observation is assimilated more
aggressively. At the same time, its exact posterior is
\begin{equation}
  \vct{P}_t(s)
  =\widehat{\vct{P}}_t
  -\frac{s\widehat{\vct{P}}_t\vct{k}_t\vct{k}_t^\top
      \widehat{\vct{P}}_t}{r_t+sz_t},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial\,\vct{\ell}^\top\vct{P}_t(s)\vct{\ell}}{\partial s}
  =-\frac{r_t(\vct{\ell}^\top\widehat{\vct{P}}_t\vct{k}_t)^2}
    {(r_t+sz_t)^2}\leq0.
  \label{eq:discussion-coherent-scale-posterior}
\end{equation}
Thus coherent higher precision has the two temporally distinct effects expected of a Kalman
filter: write the observation harder now, then reduce later corrections in covariance-overlapping
directions. Neither the current DiagKLA split nor Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-iso-scale} for
$s\ne1$ has this interpretation. A coherent implementation should use $r_t/s$ once; replacing
$r_t$ by $r_t/s$ and also retaining a separate factor $s$ in the information update would
double-count the scale and produce an $s^2$ increment.

These mechanisms can be separated explicitly with a current-write scale $s_{\mathrm{write}}$ and
a post-write confidence scale $s_{\mathrm{protect}}$:
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_t(s_{\mathrm{write}})
  =\frac{s_{\mathrm{write}}\widehat{\vct{p}}_t\odot\vct{k}_t}
    {r_t+s_{\mathrm{write}}z_t},
  \qquad
  c_{t,i}=\widehat p_{t,i}^{-1}
    +s_{\mathrm{protect}}\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-two-information-scales}
\end{equation}
Current DiagKLA is $(s_{\mathrm{write}},s_{\mathrm{protect}})=(1,s)$. Setting both to $s$
makes the scale consistent within the diagonal precision projection, although that projection
still is not the exact posterior diagonal.

\paragraph{A split variational objective.}
The two-scale rule is the stationary point of a single generalized variational free energy. For
the mean-field family in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-mean-field-family}, write
$q_{t,j}=\mathcal{N}(\vct{\mu}_{t,j},\diag(\vct{p}_t))$ and let the predicted prior be
$\widehat\pi_{t,j}=\mathcal{N}(\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j},\widehat{\vct{P}}_t)$. Define
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{J}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{split}}
    (q_{t,j};s_{\mathrm{write}},s_{\mathrm{protect}})
  &=\operatorname{KL}(q_{t,j}\,\|\,\widehat\pi_{t,j})
    +\frac{s_{\mathrm{write}}}{2r_t}
      \left(v_{t,j}-\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\mu}_{t,j}\right)^2 \notag\\
  &\quad
    +\frac{s_{\mathrm{protect}}}{2r_t}
      \vct{k}_t^\top\diag(\vct{p}_t)\vct{k}_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-split-variational-objective}
\end{align}
The first data term penalizes error at the posterior mean and therefore controls current
assimilation. The second penalizes predictive variance and therefore controls how much confidence
is passed to later tokens. For diagonal
$\widehat{\vct{P}}_t=\diag(\widehat{\vct{p}}_t)$, differentiating
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-split-variational-objective} gives the unique optimum
\begin{align}
  \vct{\mu}_{t,j}^{\star}
  &=\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j}
    +\frac{s_{\mathrm{write}}\widehat{\vct{P}}_t\vct{k}_t}
      {r_t+s_{\mathrm{write}}z_t}
    \left(v_{t,j}-\vct{k}_t^\top\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j}\right),
  \label{eq:discussion-split-variational-mean}\\
  (p_{t,i}^{\star})^{-1}
  &=\widehat p_{t,i}^{-1}
    +s_{\mathrm{protect}}\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-split-variational-variance}
\end{align}
The mean coefficient in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-split-variational-mean} is exactly the
gain in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-two-information-scales}, and
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-split-variational-variance} is its protection update. Current
DiagKLA is therefore the optimizer of
$\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{split}}(q_{t,j};1,s)$.

The connection to the power-posterior ELBO in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-power-posterior-elbo} follows by expanding its expected Gaussian
negative log likelihood. Define
\begin{equation}
  R_{t,j}
  =\left(v_{t,j}-\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\mu}_{t,j}\right)^2,
  \qquad
  V_t=\vct{k}_t^\top\diag(\vct{p}_t)\vct{k}_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-mean-variance-loss-terms}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}_{q_{t,j}}
  \!\left[\left(v_{t,j}-\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{s}_{t,j}^\star\right)^2\right]
  =R_{t,j}+V_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-mean-variance-loss-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the negative power ELBO is
\begin{equation}
  -\mathcal{L}_{t,j}^{(s)}(q_{t,j})
  =\operatorname{KL}(q_{t,j}\,\|\,\widehat\pi_{t,j})
   +\frac{s}{2r_t}(R_{t,j}+V_t)
   +\frac{s}{2}\log(2\pi r_t).
  \label{eq:discussion-negative-power-elbo-expanded}
\end{equation}
Remove only the Gaussian normalizer, which is independent of $q_{t,j}$ for fixed $r_t$, by
defining
\begin{equation}
  \overline{\mathcal{J}}_{t,j}^{(s)}(q_{t,j})
  =-\mathcal{L}_{t,j}^{(s)}(q_{t,j})
   -\frac{s}{2}\log(2\pi r_t).
  \label{eq:discussion-negative-power-elbo-q-objective}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-split-variational-objective} can then be written exactly as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{J}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{split}}
    (q_{t,j};s_{\mathrm{write}},s_{\mathrm{protect}})
  =\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{t,j}^{(s_{\mathrm{write}})}(q_{t,j})
   +\frac{s_{\mathrm{protect}}-s_{\mathrm{write}}}{2r_t}V_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-split-versus-power-elbo}
\end{equation}
Thus equal scales recover the power-posterior variational objective up to its $q$-independent
normalizer. Current DiagKLA has the particularly simple form
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{J}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{split}}(q_{t,j};1,s)
  =\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{t,j}^{(1)}(q_{t,j})
   +\frac{s-1}{2r_t}V_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-current-diagkla-variational-objective}
\end{equation}
It is ordinary mean-field variational inference plus an additional predictive-variance penalty:
the extra term increases future protection without strengthening the current mean update. A
single Gaussian likelihood must weight $R_{t,j}$ and $V_t$ equally, so unequal scales do not
define the ELBO of one coherent observation model. They instead define a convex,
decision-oriented mean--uncertainty objective with separate costs for present assimilation and
future protection. If $r_t$ is optimized through this objective rather than held fixed during the
variational update, its Gaussian normalizer in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-negative-power-elbo-expanded} must be retained.

A factorial comparison of $(1,1)$, $(1,d_k)$,
$(d_k,1)$, and $(d_k,d_k)$ would separate current assimilation from future protection; because
$s_{\mathrm{write}}=d_k$ may saturate the current correction, a gain-matched intermediate value
should also be included. Log the same-token $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ and residual contraction,
the next-token and repeated-key gains, write/erase magnitudes, covariance quantiles, and effective
memory half-life. Repeated-key, replacement, and orthogonal-key tasks can then identify whether
the benefit comes from retention, impaired rewriting, or diagonal geometry.

A large learned $r_t$ could make the $s=1$ confidence increment too weak, but the preferred
$s=d_k$ does not by itself diagnose observation noise as too large: increasing $r_t$ also weakens
the current gain, whereas increasing only $s_{\mathrm{protect}}$ does not. The relevant
dimensionless quantities are $z_t/r_t$ for the current write and
$s\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2/r_t$ for the confidence update. These, together with
$\omega_{t,i}/\widehat p_{t,i}$, should be measured before attributing the result to $r_t$.
More generally, if $r_t$ is freely learned, the triplet
$(r_t,s_{\mathrm{write}},s_{\mathrm{protect}})$ is overparameterized: the updates depend only on
\begin{equation}
  r_t^{\mathrm{write}}=\frac{r_t}{s_{\mathrm{write}}},
  \qquad
  \rho_t=\frac{s_{\mathrm{protect}}}{s_{\mathrm{write}}}.
  \label{eq:discussion-write-protect-identifiable-parameters}
\end{equation}
Here $r_t^{\mathrm{write}}$ sets current assimilation and $\rho_t$ is the identifiable
protection-to-write ratio. The present decoupled scale is not redundant: current DiagKLA fixes
$s_{\mathrm{write}}=1$ and uses $\rho_t=s$ to change future protection without changing that
observation's same-token write locally.

\subsection{An exact posterior-diagonal alternative}

Although the full posterior is dense, its diagonal after one measurement can be computed without
materializing a $d_k\times d_k$ covariance. Define
\begin{equation}
  D_t
  = r_t+\sum_j\widehat p_{t,j}k_{t,j}^2,
  \qquad
  q_{t,i}=\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2.
  \label{eq:discussion-exact-diag-denominator}
\end{equation}
The denominator $D_t$ is already required for the diagonal Kalman gain. The exact posterior
diagonal from Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-posterior-diagonal} can therefore be updated as
\begin{equation}
  p^{\mathrm{mom}}_{t,i}
  = \widehat p_{t,i}
    \frac{D_t-q_{t,i}}{D_t}
  = \widehat p_{t,i}
    \left(1-\kappa_{t,i}k_{t,i}\right),
  \qquad
  \kappa_{t,i}=\frac{\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}}{D_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-exact-diag-recurrence}
\end{equation}
This costs $O(d_k)$ state and arithmetic per token. The multiplicative form in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-diag-recurrence} is preferable to subtracting two nearly
equal covariance terms. Since $D_t-q_{t,i}=r_t+\sum_{j\ne i}q_{t,j}>0$, it also makes positivity
explicit. Equivalently, the marginal information update is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{p^{\mathrm{mom}}_{t,i}}
  = \frac{1}{\widehat p_{t,i}}
  + \frac{k_{t,i}^2}
    {r_t+\sum_{j\ne i}\widehat p_{t,j}k_{t,j}^2}.
  \label{eq:discussion-exact-diag-information}
\end{equation}
Unlike the current update, this expression retains the competition from the other key
coordinates instead of replacing its denominator by $r_t$.

The word ``exact'' applies only to the diagonal of this one posterior when the predicted prior is
diagonal. The same update creates off-diagonal entries
\begin{equation}
  P_{t,ij}
  = -\frac{\widehat p_{t,i}\widehat p_{t,j}k_{t,i}k_{t,j}}{D_t},
  \qquad i\ne j.
  \label{eq:discussion-exact-posterior-offdiag}
\end{equation}
These entries affect the next gain. Discarding them and carrying only
$\vct{p}^{\mathrm{mom}}_t$ is therefore a moment-projected, diagonal assumed-density filter, not
the exact multi-token Kalman filter. Exact filtering across tokens requires the dense
$\vct{P}_t$, with $O(d_k^2)$ state and arithmetic per token, as in ExactKLA.

This alternative also changes the parallelization structure. All channels in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-diag-recurrence} are coupled through $D_t$, so the recurrence
does not decompose into the independent $2\times2$ M\"obius maps used by the current gain scan.
The straightforward implementation is a token-sequential $O(Td_k)$ gain recurrence, preferably
fused in Triton; after the gains are produced, the existing chunked Kalman memory kernel remains
applicable. Thus the exact posterior-diagonal update saves the dense $O(d_k^2)$ covariance but
trades away the current logarithmic-depth prefix scan for the gain.

For the moment projection, the coherent scale from
Subsection~\ref{subsec:discussion-info-scale-temporal} specializes to
$r_t^{\mathrm{eff}}=r_t/s$ and gives
\begin{equation}
  D_t^{(s)}
  = \frac{r_t}{s}+\sum_j\widehat p_{t,j}k_{t,j}^2,
  \qquad
  \kappa_{t,i}^{(s)}
  = \frac{\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}}{D_t^{(s)}},
  \qquad
  p_{t,i}^{(s)}
  = \widehat p_{t,i}
    -\frac{\widehat p_{t,i}^2k_{t,i}^2}{D_t^{(s)}}.
  \label{eq:discussion-exact-diag-scaled}
\end{equation}
This is the exact-posterior-diagonal specialization of
Equations~\eqref{eq:discussion-coherent-scale-gain}--\eqref{eq:discussion-coherent-scale-posterior}.
A direct ablation should compare the current M\"obius precision scan against this recurrent moment
projection first with $s=1$, and then, separately, with the coherent calibrated choice
$r_t^{\mathrm{eff}}=r_t/d_k$.

\subsection{A tighter one-step approximation that preserves the scan}
\label{subsec:discussion-process-floor-approximation}

Yes, there is a better approximation in either of two relevant senses. If only one-step posterior
fidelity matters, the moment projection above exactly matches the marginal variances. If the
independent scan is a hard constraint, the process-noise-floor rule below is pointwise no worse
than the $s=1$ and $s=d_k$ rules, and generically better, in conditional one-step
marginal-variance error at the cost of a token-local reduction.

Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-diag-information} is the forward-KL moment projection of this
one-step posterior: it matches every exact marginal variance. Its obstacle is not arithmetic cost
but scan structure, because the update for coordinate $i$ depends on the recurrent uncertainty in
every coordinate $j\ne i$. If the independent $2\times2$ M\"obius scan is a hard constraint, a
pointwise no-worse approximation can nevertheless retain the part of this cross-coordinate
interference that is known before the scan, with strict improvement under the conditions below.

In particular, the prediction step and nonnegative process noise imply
\begin{equation}
  \widehat p_{t,j}
  =\alpha_{t,j}^2p_{t-1,j}+\omega_{t,j}
  \geq \omega_{t,j}.
  \label{eq:discussion-predicted-variance-floor}
\end{equation}
Define the token-local process-noise energy outside coordinate $i$ by
\begin{equation}
  L_t^{\omega}=\sum_j\omega_{t,j}k_{t,j}^2,
  \qquad
  L_{t,-i}^{\omega}=L_t^{\omega}-\omega_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2,
  \label{eq:discussion-process-floor-energy}
\end{equation}
and use the information increment
\begin{equation}
  u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}
  =\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t+L_{t,-i}^{\omega}},
  \qquad
  p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}
  =\left(\widehat p_{t,i}^{-1}+u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}\right)^{-1}.
  \label{eq:discussion-process-floor-update}
\end{equation}
Compared with the $s=1$ reverse-KL rule, this update no longer pretends that the other key
coordinates have zero uncertainty: it accounts for the uncertainty injected at the current
prediction step. It becomes the exact moment projection whenever
$\widehat p_{t,j}=\omega_{t,j}$ for every active $j\ne i$, for example when there is no carried
uncertainty in those coordinates.

The improvement is pointwise, not merely heuristic. Let
\begin{equation}
  S_{t,-i}=\sum_{j\ne i}\widehat p_{t,j}k_{t,j}^2,
  \qquad
  u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{mom}}=\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t+S_{t,-i}},
  \qquad
  u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{rev}}=\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-floor-comparison-increments}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:discussion-predicted-variance-floor}--
\eqref{eq:discussion-process-floor-energy} give
$0\leq L_{t,-i}^{\omega}\leq S_{t,-i}$. Hence, for $d_k\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
  d_k\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t}
  \geq u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{rev}}
  \geq u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}
  \geq u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{mom}},
  \qquad
  p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{proj},d_k}
  \leq p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{rev}}
  \leq p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}
  \leq p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{mom}}.
  \label{eq:discussion-floor-ordering}
\end{equation}
Thus, holding the incoming $\widehat{\vct{p}}_t$ fixed,
$p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}$ is always at least as close in absolute variance to the exact one-step
marginal as either the $s=1$ rule or the production $s=d_k$ rule. The improvement over $s=1$ is
strict when $k_{t,i}\ne0$ and
$L_{t,-i}^{\omega}>0$. This ordering concerns posterior fidelity only; it does not imply that the
less confident state is a better associative-memory controller, nor does it directly order the
trajectories produced after the different states are fed into later predictions.

Crucially, $L_t^{\omega}$ is a current-token reduction: it depends only on
$(\vct{\omega}_t,\vct{k}_t)$ and not on a recurrent state. Substituting
$u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}$ for $u_{t,i}$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-mobius-matrix} gives
\begin{equation}
  \vct{M}_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}
  =\begin{pmatrix}
    1+u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}\omega_{t,i}
      &u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}\alpha_{t,i}^2\\[2pt]
    \omega_{t,i}&\alpha_{t,i}^2
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:discussion-floor-mobius-matrix}
\end{equation}
The mathematical prefix-scan and carry structure is therefore unchanged. An implementation still
has to modify the token-map builder and its backward pass: it must either store $L_t^{\omega}$ or
recompute one $O(d_k)$ cross-coordinate reduction wherever token maps are reconstructed, and the
backward acquires coupled gradients through that reduction. In finite precision,
$L_{t,-i}^{\omega}$ should be evaluated as
$\max\{L_t^{\omega}-\omega_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2,0\}$ to preserve the analytic nonnegativity when a key is
nearly one-hot.

There is also a sharp reason not to expect an exact marginal update from any independent
input-only scan. Holding the current-token projections fixed, for $j\ne i$
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-diag-information} has
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial (p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{mom}})^{-1}}
    {\partial p_{t-1,j}}
  =-\frac{k_{t,i}^2\alpha_{t,j}^2k_{t,j}^2}
    {(r_t+S_{t,-i})^2},
  \label{eq:discussion-moment-cross-jacobian}
\end{equation}
which is nonzero precisely when $k_{t,i}k_{t,j}\alpha_{t,j}\ne0$. By contrast, a per-coordinate map
$c_{t,i}=F_{t,i}(c_{t-1,i};\vct{x}_t)$ whose coefficients depend only on the current token has
zero derivative with respect to $p_{t-1,j}$. Therefore no single independent raw-input
$2\times2$ M\"obius scan can reproduce the exact transition for arbitrary prior diagonal states
when $k_{t,i}k_{t,j}\alpha_{t,j}\ne0$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-process-floor-update} is a principled compromise: it uses the
simple, universally valid current-step lower bound on the omitted interference that requires no
recurrent state, while preserving the production scan.

For direct fidelity to Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-posterior-diagonal}, the rule should use
$r_t$ as written and no $d_k$ multiplier. If stronger protection is useful for the mixer, introduce
it explicitly rather than calling it a covariance approximation,
\begin{equation}
  c_{t,i}
  =\widehat p_{t,i}^{-1}
   +g_{\mathrm{protect}}u_{t,i}^{\mathrm{floor}}.
  \label{eq:discussion-floor-protection-scale}
\end{equation}
Here $g_{\mathrm{protect}}=1$ is the posterior-fidelity candidate, while
$g_{\mathrm{protect}}>1$ is a retention calibration analogous to the current
$s_{\mathrm{protect}}$. To preserve the input-only scan, $g_{\mathrm{protect}}$ must be constant or
a function only of the current token, not of a recurrent statistic. Once
$g_{\mathrm{protect}}\ne1$, the ordering in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-floor-ordering} need not
hold.

\subsection{Posterior fidelity is not mixer quality}

The moment projection is exact for the one-step posterior diagonal, and the process-floor rule is
a tighter conditional one-step marginal approximation than the current rule, but neither is
necessarily a better drop-in strategy for associative memory. Consider the symmetric case
$\widehat p_{t,i}=\widehat p_t$ and $k_{t,i}^2=1/d_k$, and define the effective write strength
\begin{equation}
  \beta_t
  = \frac{\widehat p_t}{r_t+\widehat p_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-symmetric-beta}
\end{equation}
The current $s=d_k$ precision projection and the moment projection then give
\begin{equation}
  p_t^{\mathrm{proj},d_k}
  = \widehat p_t(1-\beta_t),
  \qquad
  p_t^{\mathrm{mom}}
  = \widehat p_t\left(1-\frac{\beta_t}{d_k}\right).
  \label{eq:discussion-proj-versus-moment}
\end{equation}
One scalar measurement correctly removes only rank-one uncertainty, so the moment projection
reduces each marginal variance by only a $\beta_t/d_k$ fraction. The current rule reduces every
coordinate by a $\beta_t$ fraction, or $d_k$ times as much total trace in this symmetric example.
It therefore acts as a strong confidence regularizer: future gains are smaller and existing
associations are overwritten less aggressively. Indeed, the current $s=1$ projection also gives
smaller posterior variance than the moment projection,
\begin{equation}
  p_t^{\mathrm{proj},1}
  = \frac{\widehat p_t d_k r_t}{d_k r_t+\widehat p_t}
  \leq p_t^{\mathrm{mom}},
  \label{eq:discussion-proj-one-versus-moment}
\end{equation}
with strict inequality for $d_k>1$.
The corrected information-scale sweep found standard-suite quality nearly flat and some
long-context recall improved at larger $s$; it did not establish $d_k$ as a unique optimum.
For $d_k>1$, even the $s=1$ projection retains less variance than the moment projection, so an
unretuned moment variant is likely to produce larger future write/erase gains and more
interference. It may nevertheless help tasks that require rapid adaptation or frequent replacement
of old associations. Posterior faithfulness and memory quality are therefore distinct hypotheses.

The process-noise scale makes a raw comparison especially unfavorable to the moment variant.
Prediction injects $O(d_k\omega_t)$ total diagonal uncertainty per token, whereas a rank-one
moment update removes only $O(1)$ total uncertainty. Near $\alpha_t^2=1$, matching the stationary
effective gain of the current $s=d_k$ rule requires approximately, when the retention-loss term
proportional to $1-\alpha_t^2$ is negligible,
\begin{equation}
  \omega_t^{\mathrm{mom}}
  \simeq \frac{\omega_t^{\mathrm{proj}}}{d_k},
  \label{eq:discussion-moment-omega-calibration}
\end{equation}
or, at fixed process-noise scale and matching the stationary covariance-to-noise ratio, an
observation-noise scale roughly $d_k$ times larger. Without this recalibration, the current initialization
$\omega_t\simeq\omega_{\min}+\operatorname{softplus}(0)$ can drive the moment filter toward high
uncertainty and $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ close to one.

The appropriate comparison is therefore seven-way:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the production M\"obius projection with $s=d_k$;
  \item the same projection with $s=1$;
  \item the process-floor approximation with $g_{\mathrm{protect}}=1$;
  \item the process-floor approximation with a fixed $g_{\mathrm{protect}}$ chosen offline to match
    the production next-token gain or memory-half-life distribution;
  \item the recurrent moment projection with the same $\omega/r$ parameterization and
    $r_t^{\mathrm{eff}}=r_t$;
  \item the recurrent moment projection with the coherent likelihood scale
    $r_t^{\mathrm{eff}}=r_t/d_k$ in both its same-token gain and posterior update;
  \item the recurrent moment projection with $\omega$ initialized and floored at approximately
    $1/d_k$ of the production scale, or otherwise calibrated to match the initial and stationary
    $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ distribution.
\end{enumerate}
The raw comparisons measure the behavior of each formula under identical parameters; the
gain- or half-life-matched comparisons isolate posterior geometry from overwrite temperature.
All variants should log
$\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$, covariance and process-noise quantiles, effective memory half-life, and
write/erase magnitude. Repeated-key and orthogonal-key synthetic tasks should precede language
model pretraining, because they directly separate rapid rewriting from protection against
cross-talk. Among covariance approximations, the process-floor rule is the first candidate to test
because it improves the conditional one-step marginal approximation without losing the parallel
M\"obius gain scan. The moment projection should replace it only if the matched comparison
justifies the additional recurrent gain computation.

\subsection{Anisotropic write direction and initialization}

For a unit key, decompose
\begin{equation}
  \beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}=\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\kappa}_t,
  \qquad
  \vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp}
  = \vct{\kappa}_t-\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\vct{k}_t,
  \qquad
  \vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp}=0.
  \label{eq:discussion-gain-decomposition}
\end{equation}
IsoKLA enforces $\vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp}=0$: uncertainty changes the strength of a write while
preserving the binding direction. DiagKLA can rotate both the write and erase directions away
from the current key. This can be useful uncertainty preconditioning, or it can write the value
into unrelated key directions and create cross-talk. Greater covariance expressivity alone does
not distinguish these interpretations.

The current initialization is also not an exact IsoKLA fallback. Zeroing the output map of the
process-noise projection makes $\omega_{t,i}$ uniform, but
\begin{equation}
  \widehat p_{t,i}=\alpha_{t,i}^2/\mu+\omega_t
\end{equation}
is already channel dependent when $\alpha_{t,i}$ is channel dependent. After one observation,
$d_k k_{t,i}^2/r_t$ further leaves the isotropic subspace. The initialization is a useful
stabilizer, but the model cannot choose an exact IsoKLA function and add anisotropy only when it
is beneficial.

\subsection{Value-wise isotropic uncertainty}
\label{subsec:discussion-value-isotropic}

The columnwise Gaussian model in the main paper assigns the same key-space covariance to every
value coordinate. In particular,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde{\vct{s}}_{t,j}\mid\mathcal{F}_t
  &\sim\mathcal{N}(\vct{s}_{t,j},\vct{P}_t)
    \quad\text{independently for }j=1,\ldots,d_v,
  \\
  \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(
    \operatorname{vec}(\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t)\mid\mathcal{F}_t
  \right)
  &=\vct{I}_{d_v}\otimes\vct{P}_t.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-shared-family}
\end{equation}
Here and throughout this subsection, a tilde denotes the latent random memory, a bare symbol
denotes its tracked mean, and a hat denotes a one-step prediction. A complementary structured
approximation retains a different scalar key-space variance for each value coordinate:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde{\vct{s}}_{t,j}\mid\mathcal{F}_t
  &\sim\mathcal{N}(\vct{s}_{t,j},b_{t,j}\vct{I}_{d_k})
    \quad\text{independently across }j,
  \\
  \vct{B}_t&=\diag(\vct{b}_t),
  \\
  \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(
    \operatorname{vec}(\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t)\mid\mathcal{F}_t
  \right)
  &=\vct{B}_t\otimes\vct{I}_{d_k}.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-family}
\end{equation}
The subscript $j$ is essential: using the same $b_t\vct{I}$ for every column recovers Isotropic
KDN rather than a new model. Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-value-iso-family} still assumes
cross-column independence, but allows different value coordinates to have different reliability.

\paragraph{State-space model and projected prediction.}
To make the different column variances nontrivial, allow the process and observation uncertainty
to depend on the value coordinate:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde{\vct{s}}_{t,j}
  &=\vct{D}_t\widetilde{\vct{s}}_{t-1,j}+\vct{w}_{t,j},
  &
  \vct{w}_{t,j}
  &\sim\mathcal{N}(\vct{0},\omega_{t,j}\vct{I}_{d_k}),
  \\
  v_{t,j}
  &=\vct{k}_t^\top\widetilde{\vct{s}}_{t,j}+e_{t,j},
  &
  e_{t,j}
  &\sim\mathcal{N}(0,r_{t,j}),
  \qquad
  \vct{D}_t=\diag(\vct{\alpha}_t).
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-model}
\end{equation}
The noises are independent across columns and time. The current observation model is recovered by
setting $r_{t,j}=r_t$, and shared process uncertainty is recovered by setting
$\omega_{t,j}=\omega_t$. A non-diagonal value-noise covariance would couple the columns after
conditioning and leave the factorized family in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-value-iso-family}.

The mean prediction remains shared,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j}=\vct{D}_t\vct{s}_{t-1,j},
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-mean-prediction}
\end{equation}
whereas the exact covariance prediction for column $j$ is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\vct{P}}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{exact}}
  =b_{t-1,j}\vct{D}_t\vct{D}_t^\top
   +\omega_{t,j}\vct{I}_{d_k}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-exact-prediction}
\end{equation}
Following Isotropic KDN, project this covariance to its average marginal variance. Defining
\begin{equation}
  a_t
  =\frac{1}{d_k}\operatorname{Tr}(\vct{D}_t\vct{D}_t^\top)
  =\frac{1}{d_k}\sum_{i=1}^{d_k}\alpha_{t,i}^2
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-transition-average}
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\vct{P}}_{t,j}
  =\widehat b_{t,j}\vct{I}_{d_k},
  \qquad
  \widehat b_{t,j}=a_tb_{t-1,j}+\omega_{t,j}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-prediction}
\end{equation}
This prediction is exact when
$\vct{D}_t\vct{D}_t^\top=a_t\vct{I}_{d_k}$; otherwise it is the covariance-coordinate trace
projection used by the shared isotropic model.

\paragraph{Columnwise Kalman correction.}
Conditioned on the projected prediction, define the scalar innovation
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{t,j}
  =v_{t,j}-\vct{k}_t^\top\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-innovation}
\end{equation}
The predictive cross-covariance and innovation variance are
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(
    \widetilde{\vct{s}}_{t,j},v_{t,j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},\vct{k}_t
  \right)
  =\widehat b_{t,j}\vct{k}_t,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{Var}\!\left(
    v_{t,j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},\vct{k}_t
  \right)
  =r_{t,j}+\widehat b_{t,j}\|\vct{k}_t\|_2^2.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-joint-moments}
\end{equation}
The Gaussian conditional-mean identity therefore gives a different Kalman gain for every value
coordinate,
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_{t,j}
  =\beta_{t,j}\vct{k}_t,
  \qquad
  \beta_{t,j}
  =\frac{\widehat b_{t,j}}
    {r_{t,j}+\widehat b_{t,j}\|\vct{k}_t\|_2^2},
  \qquad
  \vct{s}_{t,j}
  =\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j}
   +\beta_{t,j}\vct{k}_t\delta_{t,j}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-gain}
\end{equation}
Thus all columns write along the same key direction, but they need not use the same write strength.
The residual remaining at that key is
\begin{equation}
  v_{t,j}-\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{s}_{t,j}
  =\frac{r_{t,j}}
    {r_{t,j}+\widehat b_{t,j}\|\vct{k}_t\|_2^2}
    \delta_{t,j},
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-residual-contraction}
\end{equation}
so every coordinate remains contractive even though the contraction factor is value dependent.

The exact posterior covariance and information for column $j$ are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \vct{P}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{exact}}
  &=\widehat b_{t,j}\vct{I}_{d_k}
    -\frac{\widehat b_{t,j}^2\vct{k}_t\vct{k}_t^\top}
      {r_{t,j}+\widehat b_{t,j}\|\vct{k}_t\|_2^2},
  \\
  \vct{C}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{exact}}
  &=\left(\vct{P}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{exact}}\right)^{-1}
   =\widehat c_{t,j}\vct{I}_{d_k}
    +\frac{1}{r_{t,j}}\vct{k}_t\vct{k}_t^\top,
  \qquad
  \widehat c_{t,j}=\widehat b_{t,j}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-exact-posterior}
\end{equation}
The rank-one term leaves the isotropic family, so the posterior must again be projected before it
can be carried to the next token.

\paragraph{Reverse-KL isotropic projection.}
Let $\pi_{t,j}$ be the exact Gaussian posterior in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-value-iso-exact-posterior} and consider
\begin{equation}
  q_{t,j}
  =\mathcal{N}(\vct{m}_{t,j},b_{t,j}\vct{I}_{d_k}).
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-variational-family}
\end{equation}
Up to terms independent of $\vct{m}_{t,j}$ and $b_{t,j}$, the reverse KL divergence is
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{KL}(q_{t,j}\,\|\,\pi_{t,j})
  =\frac{1}{2}\left[
    (\vct{m}_{t,j}-\vct{s}_{t,j})^\top
      \vct{C}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{exact}}
      (\vct{m}_{t,j}-\vct{s}_{t,j})
    +b_{t,j}\operatorname{Tr}(\vct{C}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{exact}})
    -d_k\log b_{t,j}
  \right]+\mathrm{const}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-kl}
\end{equation}
The first-order conditions give
\begin{equation}
  \vct{m}_{t,j}=\vct{s}_{t,j},
  \qquad
  b_{t,j}
  =\frac{d_k}{\operatorname{Tr}(\vct{C}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{exact}})}
  =\left(
    \widehat c_{t,j}
    +\frac{\|\vct{k}_t\|_2^2}{d_kr_{t,j}}
  \right)^{-1}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-variational-solution}
\end{equation}
The projection therefore preserves the exact one-step posterior mean under the projected
predictive prior and retains one scalar variance for each memory column. In information variables
$c_{t,j}=b_{t,j}^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat c_{t,j}
  =\frac{c_{t-1,j}}{a_t+\omega_{t,j}c_{t-1,j}},
  \qquad
  u_{t,j}=\frac{\|\vct{k}_t\|_2^2}{d_kr_{t,j}},
  \qquad
  c_{t,j}=\widehat c_{t,j}+u_{t,j}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-information-update}
\end{equation}
This is the ordinary reverse-KL solution. Multiplying $u_{t,j}$ by an additional information
scale would be a separate calibration rather than the variational optimum for the observation
model in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-value-iso-model}.

\paragraph{Associative uncertainty scan and memory update.}
Substituting the prediction into the information update yields
\begin{equation}
  c_{t,j}
  =\frac{(1+u_{t,j}\omega_{t,j})c_{t-1,j}+u_{t,j}a_t}
    {\omega_{t,j}c_{t-1,j}+a_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-mobius}
\end{equation}
Hence each value coordinate has the M\"obius matrix
\begin{equation}
  \vct{M}_{t,j}
  =\begin{bmatrix}
    1+u_{t,j}\omega_{t,j} & u_{t,j}a_t \\
    \omega_{t,j} & a_t
  \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \begin{bmatrix}n_{t,j}\\d_{t,j}\end{bmatrix}
  =\vct{M}_{t,j}
   \begin{bmatrix}n_{t-1,j}\\d_{t-1,j}\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  c_{t,j}=\frac{n_{t,j}}{d_{t,j}},
  \qquad
  \begin{bmatrix}n_{0,j}\\d_{0,j}\end{bmatrix}
  =\begin{bmatrix}c_{0,j}\\1\end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-mobius-matrix}
\end{equation}
Matrix multiplication therefore gives an associative gain scan, now comprising $d_v$ independent
scalar scans rather than one shared scalar scan.

Let $\vct{\beta}_t=(\beta_{t,1},\ldots,\beta_{t,d_v})^\top$ and
$\widehat{\vct{S}}_t=\vct{D}_t\vct{S}_{t-1}$. Stacking the exact columnwise means from
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-value-iso-gain} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \vct{S}_t
  &=\widehat{\vct{S}}_t
    +\vct{k}_t
      \left[
        \vct{\beta}_t\odot
        \left(\vct{v}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t\right)
      \right]^\top
  \\
  &=\widehat{\vct{S}}_t
    -\vct{k}_t\vct{k}_t^\top\widehat{\vct{S}}_t\diag(\vct{\beta}_t)
    +\vct{k}_t(\vct{\beta}_t\odot\vct{v}_t)^\top.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-memory-update}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Comparison with Gated DeltaNet-2}
\label{subsubsec:discussion-value-iso-gdn2}

Using the same predicted memory
$\widehat{\vct{S}}_t=\vct{D}_t\vct{S}_{t-1}$, Gated DeltaNet-2 (GDN-2) can be written as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \vct{S}_t^{\mathrm{GDN2}}
  &=\widehat{\vct{S}}_t
    -\vct{k}_t
      (\vct{b}_t^{\mathrm{erase}}\odot\vct{k}_t)^\top
      \widehat{\vct{S}}_t
    +\vct{k}_t(\vct{w}_t\odot\vct{v}_t)^\top
  \\
  &=\widehat{\vct{S}}_t
    +\vct{k}_t\left[
      \vct{w}_t\odot\vct{v}_t
      -\widehat{\vct{S}}_t^\top
       (\vct{b}_t^{\mathrm{erase}}\odot\vct{k}_t)
    \right]^\top,
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-gdn2-comparison-update}
\end{equation}
where $\vct{b}_t^{\mathrm{erase}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ is a key-channel erase gate and
$\vct{w}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{d_v}$ is a value-channel write gate. Both are predicted directly from
the current token. Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-value-iso-memory-update} and GDN-2 therefore share
the outer write direction $\vct{k}_t$ and both permit value-wise scaling of the incoming value.
Their erase terms, however, act on different axes:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \text{value-wise isotropic:}\quad
  &-\vct{k}_t\vct{k}_t^\top
    \widehat{\vct{S}}_t\diag(\vct{\beta}_t),
  \\
  \text{GDN-2:}\quad
  &-\vct{k}_t
    (\vct{b}_t^{\mathrm{erase}}\odot\vct{k}_t)^\top
    \widehat{\vct{S}}_t.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-gdn2-erase-comparison}
\end{equation}
In the value-wise isotropic filter, column $j$ uses the same uncertainty-derived
$\beta_{t,j}$ to scale both the observation write and the predicted-value erasure. GDN-2 instead
uses one key-wise pseudo-key
$\vct{b}_t^{\mathrm{erase}}\odot\vct{k}_t$ to erase every value column, while the independent
$w_{t,j}$ scales only the incoming value. Thus GDN-2 deliberately decouples the two terms that a
Kalman innovation ties together.

The equality conditions make the relationship precise. For
Equations~\eqref{eq:discussion-value-iso-memory-update} and
\eqref{eq:discussion-gdn2-comparison-update} to agree for arbitrary predicted memories and values,
we must have
\begin{equation}
  w_{t,j}=\beta_{t,j},
  \qquad
  \vct{b}_t^{\mathrm{erase}}\odot\vct{k}_t
  =\beta_{t,j}\vct{k}_t
  \quad\text{for every }j.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-gdn2-equality}
\end{equation}
For a generic key, the second condition forces all $\beta_{t,j}$ to equal one scalar $\beta_t$
and forces $b_{t,i}^{\mathrm{erase}}=\beta_t$ on the support of $\vct{k}_t$. Together with the
first condition,
\begin{equation}
  \vct{b}_t^{\mathrm{erase}}=\beta_t\vct{1}_{d_k},
  \qquad
  \vct{w}_t=\beta_t\vct{1}_{d_v},
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-gdn2-intersection}
\end{equation}
which is exactly the KDA recurrence; a scalar transition further recovers Gated DeltaNet.
Consequently, neither general family contains the other. Their common intersection is the
scalar-gated delta rule.

GDN-2 can still be written as a fixed-gain Kalman-like residual after defining the pseudo-key
$\vct{e}_t=\vct{b}_t^{\mathrm{erase}}\odot\vct{k}_t$, pseudo-value
$\vct{z}_t=\vct{w}_t\odot\vct{v}_t$, and prescribing the gain $\vct{k}_t$. It is not, however,
the posterior-mean update of the original observation model
$\vct{v}_t=\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t+\text{noise}$ under a column-specific isotropic
covariance. In that model the same gain must multiply both halves of each innovation.

The correction remains rank one, but it is no longer of the shared-gain form
$\widehat{\vct{S}}_t+\vct{\kappa}_t\vct{\delta}_t^\top$. Equivalently, column $j$ has transition
\begin{equation}
  \vct{s}_{t,j}
  =\underbrace{
    (\vct{I}-\beta_{t,j}\vct{k}_t\vct{k}_t^\top)\vct{D}_t
  }_{\vct{A}_{t,j}}
  \vct{s}_{t-1,j}
  +\beta_{t,j}\vct{k}_t v_{t,j}.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-affine-column}
\end{equation}
The information stage remains scan-compatible, and the sequential memory update still costs
$O(d_kd_v)$ per token. However, $\vct{A}_{t,j}$ varies with $j$, so the single compact-WY system
shared across all value columns in the current KDN kernel no longer applies directly. An exact
parallel implementation requires value-wise batched systems, or a grouped-value approximation in
which columns within each group share one scalar uncertainty and gain.

\paragraph{When the extension is genuinely different.}
If
\begin{equation}
  b_{0,j}=b_0,
  \qquad
  \omega_{t,j}=\omega_t,
  \qquad
  r_{t,j}=r_t
  \qquad\text{for every }j,
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-symmetry}
\end{equation}
then induction through Equations~\eqref{eq:discussion-value-iso-prediction} and
\eqref{eq:discussion-value-iso-information-update} gives
$b_{t,j}=b_t$ and $\beta_{t,j}=\beta_t$ for every column and token. The observed values cannot
break this symmetry because Gaussian covariance updates do not depend on the realized
$v_{t,j}$. At least one of the initial uncertainty, process noise, or observation noise must
therefore vary across value coordinates for the extension to differ from Isotropic KDN.

If hardware constraints instead force one common gain, minimizing total conditional Frobenius
risk over the restricted family
$\vct{s}_{t,j}=\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j}+\vct{\kappa}_t\delta_{t,j}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{shared}}
  =\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d_v}\widehat b_{t,j}}
    {\sum_{j=1}^{d_v}r_{t,j}
      +\|\vct{k}_t\|_2^2\sum_{j=1}^{d_v}\widehat b_{t,j}}
    \vct{k}_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-value-iso-common-gain}
\end{equation}
For shared $r_t$, this is the isotropic gain formed from
$\overline b_t=d_v^{-1}\sum_j\widehat b_{t,j}$. It is the best common-gain compromise, but it is
not the columnwise Kalman posterior mean unless all $\beta_{t,j}$ coincide; enforcing it largely
averages away the extra uncertainty state at the current write.

Allowing independent $\omega_{t,j}$ and $r_{t,j}$ also introduces one covariance-scale gauge per
value coordinate: jointly scaling $b_{t,j}$, $\omega_{t,j}$, and $r_{t,j}$ leaves
$\beta_{t,j}$ unchanged. A fixed or anchored observation-noise scale is therefore even more
important in this model; tying $r_{t,j}=r_t$ reduces these independent gauges to the common gauge
already discussed above.

This model and Diagonal KDN spend a similar scalar state budget on different axes. Diagonal KDN
tracks key-channel uncertainty $p_{t,i}$ and produces one anisotropic gain shared by all value
columns. The value-wise isotropic model tracks value-coordinate uncertainty $b_{t,j}$ and
produces value-dependent scalar gains that always point along $\vct{k}_t$. It is principled when
value coordinates have genuinely different volatility or observation reliability, but it does
not represent the key-direction uncertainty responsible for associative interference. It also
depends on the learned value basis and weakens the shared-kernel implementation. The cleanest use
is therefore as a value-wise or grouped-value ablation rather than a drop-in replacement for
Diagonal KDN.

\subsection{Decisive ablation}

The cleanest next experiment holds the diagonal information tracker and effective write strength
fixed while intervening only on the gain direction:
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_t^{(\eta)}
  = \beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\vct{k}_t
  +\eta\vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp},
  \qquad \eta\in\{0,1\}.
  \label{eq:discussion-gain-direction-ablation}
\end{equation}
Here $\eta=0$ removes only the off-key component and $\eta=1$ recovers the current DiagKLA. A
gain for $\eta=0$ would implicate the off-key direction; parity would show little measurable
benefit from that direction; a robust gain for $\eta=1$ would provide direct evidence that
anisotropic writes add useful capacity. The comparison should log $\beta^{\mathrm{eff}}$, gain
angle, $\vct{p}$ and $\vct{c}$ quantiles, $\omega/r$, and cold-channel fraction on current
checkpoints.

Two supporting controls are needed:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compare the projected information state in
    Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-projected-posterior-diagonal}, its $s=1$ version, and the
    process-floor state in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-process-floor-update} with both the
    sequential moment-projected oracle in
    Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-posterior-diagonal} and the dense ExactKLA covariance
    diagonal on a short synthetic task, including orthogonal keys with identical elementwise
    squares.
  \item Run a matched three-seed 220M comparison of DiagKLA, IsoKLA, and the stabilized IsoKLA
    variant with identical world size, data order, and parameter count. Validate the S-NIAH-2
    advantage across context lengths and needle depths before spending on a new large-scale run.
\end{enumerate}

The immediate scale control is to complete the full long-context grid for the existing $s=d_k$
checkpoint and compare it with the completed $s=4d_k$ arm; the currently comparable cells do not
resolve their ordering. Only if $4d_k$ wins consistently is a point above that boundary warranted.
This is an overwrite-temperature diagnostic: larger $s$ increases the post-write confidence
state. A token-local change does not strengthen that token's write, while a global change alters
later current writes through their covariance prefix. The sweep can locate a better
retention--plasticity operating point, but it cannot resolve the posterior inconsistency or
establish that off-key anisotropy is useful.

\section{The Zero-Process-Noise Limit}
\label{sec:discussion-zero-process-noise}

Because $\vct{\omega}$ carries part of the covariance-scale symmetry of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion-scale-symmetry} and is what makes the information prediction
intractable in closed form, it is natural to ask whether the degenerate choice
$\vct{\omega}_t\equiv0$ is preferable. It is not. The limit buys a cheaper scan, does not improve
the fidelity of the diagonal approximation at all, and removes the plasticity floor that makes
the layer a memory rather than an estimator.

\subsection{It does not restore exactness}

Prediction never creates off-diagonal covariance, at any $\vct{\omega}$. With
$\vct{D}_t=\diag(\vct{\alpha}_t)$ and $\vct{\Omega}_t=\diag(\vct{\omega}_t)$,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat P_{t,ij}
  = \alpha_{t,i}\alpha_{t,j}P_{t-1,ij}
    +\omega_{t,i}\delta_{ij},
  \label{eq:discussion-predict-preserves-pattern}
\end{equation}
so the predict step rescales the existing sparsity pattern and adds only to the diagonal. The
measurement is the sole source of off-diagonal mass, and by
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-exact-posterior-offdiag} it contributes
$P_{t,ij}=-\widehat p_{t,i}\widehat p_{t,j}k_{t,i}k_{t,j}/D_t$, which vanishes for every
$i\ne j$ if and only if $k_{t,i}k_{t,j}=0$. The exactness condition for the diagonal family is
therefore a condition on the keys alone,
\begin{equation}
  \vct{P}_t\ \text{diagonal for all }t
  \iff
  \text{every }\vct{k}_t\ \text{is supported on a single coordinate},
  \label{eq:discussion-diagonal-exactness-condition}
\end{equation}
and process noise does not appear in it. Setting $\vct{\omega}_t=0$ leaves the approximation
error of the diagonal state exactly where it was.

What the limit does make exact is the \emph{information} prediction: for invertible
$\vct{D}_t$ it becomes the conjugation
$\widehat{\vct{C}}_t=\vct{D}_t^{-\top}\vct{C}_{t-1}\vct{D}_t^{-1}$ rather than an inverse of a
sum. That is a statement about the parameterization of an already-diagonal state, not about how
faithfully that state represents the true posterior. The two senses of ``exact'' should not be
conflated.

\subsection{What the limit does buy}

The general per-channel information map in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-projected-info-mobius} is represented by the matrix in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-mobius-matrix}; its $2\times2$ products admit a logarithmic-depth
prefix scan. At $\omega_{t,i}=0$ the lower-left entry vanishes, $\vct{M}_{t,i}$ becomes upper
triangular, and the M\"obius scan degenerates to the affine recurrence
\begin{equation}
  c_{t,i}=\frac{c_{t-1,i}}{\alpha_{t,i}^2}+s\frac{k_{t,i}^2}{r_t},
  \label{eq:discussion-affine-info-recurrence}
\end{equation}
the ordinary decay-accumulate scan of gated linear attention. This is a real implementation
saving, and it is the only advantage of the limit.

\subsection{Covariance windup}

A Kalman filter without process noise is recursive least squares, and it inherits the classical
covariance windup of that estimator: information only accumulates, so the gain decays to zero and
the layer stops writing. By Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-memory-residual} a vanishing
$\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ leaves the residual untouched,
$\vct{v}_t-\vct{S}_t^\top\vct{k}_t\to\vct{e}_t$, so the write becomes a no-op rather than merely
a weak one. There are two regimes.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\alpha<1$. Measurement only decreases variance, so
    $p_{t,i}\leq\widehat p_{t,i}=\alpha_{t,i}^2p_{t-1,i}$ and hence
    $p_{t,i}\leq\alpha^{2t}p_{0,i}$. The gain dies geometrically while $\vct{D}_t$ is
    simultaneously draining $\vct{S}_t$: the memory empties and cannot be refilled. This is the
    degenerate corner.
  \item $\alpha=1$. For spread-out unit keys, $\mathbb{E}[k_i^2]=1/d_k$ gives
    $c_{t,i}=\mu+ts/(d_kr)$ and therefore
    \begin{equation}
      \beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}
      =\frac{d_k}{d_k(1+\mu r)+st}
      \xrightarrow[\ t\ \text{large}\ ]{}\frac{d_k}{st}.
      \label{eq:discussion-rls-gain-decay}
    \end{equation}
    At the default $s=d_k$ this is $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq1/t$, about $2\times10^{-4}$ by
    token $4096$. Larger $s$ freezes the memory proportionally faster, with no floor to stop it.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The plasticity floor set by process noise}

Positive process noise is precisely what prevents this. With $\alpha\simeq1$, $s=d_k$, and
spread-out unit keys, the tracked variance obeys $p_t=\widehat p_t/(1+\widehat p_t/r)$ and
$\widehat p_{t+1}=p_t+\omega$, whose fixed point solves
$\widehat p_\infty^2-\omega\widehat p_\infty-\omega r=0$:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat p_\infty
  =\frac{\omega+\sqrt{\omega^2+4\omega r}}{2}
  =\Theta\!\left(\sqrt{\omega r}\right)
  \ \ (\omega\ll r),
  \qquad
  \beta_\infty^{\mathrm{eff}}
  =\frac{\widehat p_\infty}{r+\widehat p_\infty}.
  \label{eq:discussion-stationary-plasticity-floor}
\end{equation}
The uncertainty floor is the geometric mean of process and observation noise. It therefore
degrades gracefully rather than abruptly, but it vanishes continuously as $\omega\to0$,
recovering the windup above. Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-stationary-plasticity-floor} is the
stationary counterpart of Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-process-noise-plasticity-floor}, which
states the same protection only in the $s\to\infty$ limit at a single token. At the current
initialization---$\omega_{\min}=r_{\min}=0.05$ with zeroed output maps, giving
$\omega\simeq r\simeq0.74$---it evaluates to $\widehat p_\infty\simeq1.20$ and
$\beta_\infty^{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq0.62$, a healthy retention--plasticity operating point rather
than a frozen one.

\subsection{Two things the limit does not do}

It does not remove the scale symmetry. Section~\ref{sec:discussion-scale-symmetry} survives with
one fewer parameter: $\vct{p}_t'=\lambda\vct{p}_t$, $r_t'=\lambda r_t$, $\mu'=\mu/\lambda$ still
leaves $\vct{\kappa}_t$ invariant. The gauge choice $r\equiv1$, $\mu\equiv1$ is still required
and is still the recommended fix.

It is also not the intervention that produced the noisy-MQAR improvement. That result came from
zeroing the \emph{output map} of the process-noise projection, which makes $\omega_{t,i}$ uniform
across channels at $\omega_{\min}+\operatorname{softplus}(0)\simeq0.74$---isotropic and large,
not small. Setting $\vct{\omega}=0$ is the opposite intervention, and conflating the two reads
that result backwards. The parameterization
$\omega_{t,i}=\omega_{\min}+\operatorname{softplus}(f_\omega(\vct{x}_t))$ with $\omega_{\min}>0$
makes the limit unreachable by design.

The zero-process-noise filter is the correct estimator only when the associations are fixed for
the whole context and nothing must be overwritten, which is exactly the regime in which recursive
least squares is optimal. For a streaming memory it is the degenerate corner that $\omega_{\min}$
exists to exclude.

\section{Overwrite, Overconfidence, and Bounded Writes}
\label{sec:discussion-bounded-overwrite}

The information-scale results expose a distinction that is easy to miss. Increasing the
protection scale $s$ records more confidence after a write and, by
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-delayed-scale-effect}, lowers the effective gains of later tokens.
Better accuracy at larger $s$ is therefore consistent with an overwrite-limited memory: stronger
confidence protects existing associations from subsequent write--erase updates. It is not evidence
of a better calibrated posterior. The reverse-KL state at $s=1$ is already overconfident relative
to the exact marginal by Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-conditional-versus-marginal-variance}, and
$s>1$ decreases its retained variance further. Statistical overconfidence and excessive overwrite
are thus different, and often opposing, failure modes. The former suppresses future plasticity;
the latter changes the current memory too aggressively.

\subsection{Why a better diagonal projection cannot solve both problems}
\label{subsec:discussion-correlation-aware-approximation}

Avoiding posterior overconfidence and suppressing overwrite initially appear to ask for the same
change, but a purely diagonal covariance exposes a conflict between them. Suppress the token
index, let the predicted covariance be
$\vct{P}^{-}=\diag(p_1,\ldots,p_{d_k})$, and define
\begin{equation}
  q_i=p_i k_i^2,
  \qquad
  z=\sum_i q_i,
  \qquad
  D=r+z.
  \label{eq:discussion-correlation-comparison-notation}
\end{equation}
The exact one-step posterior, the reverse-KL diagonal projection, and the forward-KL moment
projection are respectively
\begin{align}
  \vct{P}^{\star}
  &=\vct{P}^{-}
    -\frac{(\vct{P}^{-}\vct{k})(\vct{P}^{-}\vct{k})^\top}{D},
  \label{eq:discussion-correlation-exact-posterior}\\
  p_i^{\mathrm{rev}}
  &=p_i-\frac{p_i^2k_i^2}{r+q_i},
  &
  p_i^{\mathrm{mom}}
  &=p_i-\frac{p_i^2k_i^2}{D}.
  \label{eq:discussion-correlation-diagonal-projections}
\end{align}
Because $r+q_i\leq D$, the reverse-KL state satisfies
$p_i^{\mathrm{rev}}\leq p_i^{\mathrm{mom}}=P_{ii}^{\star}$. Replacing it by the moment
projection therefore removes the one-step marginal overconfidence. It does not, however, protect
the direction that was just observed.

To see why, consider the posterior variance left along the same key. With
$f(x)=rx/(r+x)$,
\begin{align}
  v^{\star}
  :=\vct{k}^\top\vct{P}^{\star}\vct{k}
  &=f(z), \notag\\
  v^{\mathrm{rev}}
  :=\sum_i k_i^2p_i^{\mathrm{rev}}
  &=\sum_i f(q_i), \notag\\
  v^{\mathrm{mom}}
  :=\sum_i k_i^2p_i^{\mathrm{mom}}
  &=v^{\star}
    +\frac{2\sum_{i<j}q_iq_j}{D}.
  \label{eq:discussion-directional-variance-comparison}
\end{align}
Since $f$ is nonnegative and subadditive on $\mathbb{R}_{+}$,
\begin{equation}
  v^{\star}\leq v^{\mathrm{rev}}\leq v^{\mathrm{mom}}.
  \label{eq:discussion-directional-variance-ordering}
\end{equation}
Thus the reverse-KL approximation can underestimate every marginal variance while still
\emph{overestimating} uncertainty in the observed direction. The missing quantity is the
rank-one cross-coordinate correction in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-correlation-exact-posterior}, whose quadratic contribution along
$\vct{k}$ is negative. It preserves uncertainty in individual coordinates while recording that a
particular linear combination has already been measured. For an immediate repeated-key observation with noise
$r_{\mathrm{next}}$, the effective write
$v/(r_{\mathrm{next}}+v)$ is strictly increasing in $v$. The exact covariance can consequently be
better calibrated and less willing to overwrite the same association at once. A diagonal moment
projection keeps the correct marginals but discards precisely the correlations that provide this
directional protection, so it can increase repeated-key writes relative to reverse KL.

\paragraph{Matching the exact repeated-key write with a scalar scale.}
If the immediate objective is only to match the protection in the direction that was just
observed, the existing information scale provides a cheaper approximation than retaining the
full covariance. Apply a scale $s$ only to the diagonal precision passed forward, leaving the
current mean gain unchanged:
\begin{equation}
  p_i(s)
  =\left(p_i^{-1}+s\frac{k_i^2}{r}\right)^{-1}
  =\frac{rp_i}{r+sq_i},
  \qquad
  v(s):=\sum_i k_i^2p_i(s)
  =\sum_i\frac{rq_i}{r+sq_i}.
  \label{eq:discussion-directional-scale-update}
\end{equation}
The ordinary reverse-KL projection is $s=1$, for which
$v(1)=v^{\mathrm{rev}}\geq v^\star$. Hence an exact one-step directional calibration chooses
$s_{\mathrm{dir}}^\star$ to satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \sum_i\frac{x_i}{1+s_{\mathrm{dir}}^\star x_i}
  =\frac{X}{1+X},
  \qquad
  x_i=\frac{q_i}{r},
  \qquad
  X=\sum_i x_i.
  \label{eq:discussion-directional-scale-root}
\end{equation}
For $X>0$, the left-hand side is continuous and strictly decreasing in
$s_{\mathrm{dir}}^\star$, so the solution is unique. If $d_{\mathrm{act}}$ coordinates have
$q_i>0$, subadditivity at $s=1$ and concavity at $s=d_{\mathrm{act}}$ give
$1\leq s_{\mathrm{dir}}^\star\leq d_{\mathrm{act}}$. Balanced contributions
$q_i=q$ attain $s_{\mathrm{dir}}^\star=d_{\mathrm{act}}$; in particular, a dense symmetric key
recovers the width calibration $s=d_k$ derived earlier. In the low-SNR regime $z/r\ll1$, expanding
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-directional-scale-root} gives the participation-ratio approximation
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{dir}}^\star
  \simeq d_{\mathrm{eff}}
  :=\frac{\left(\sum_iq_i\right)^2}{\sum_iq_i^2}.
  \label{eq:discussion-directional-effective-dimension}
\end{equation}
Thus the empirically useful $s>1$ can be interpreted as compensating, on average, for the
directional confidence that was lost when the exact rank-one covariance correction was discarded.

By construction, Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-directional-scale-root} makes
$v(s_{\mathrm{dir}}^\star)=v^\star$. With no intervening state dynamics or process noise, it
therefore reproduces the exact effective gain
$v^\star/(r_{\mathrm{next}}+v^\star)$ for an immediate repeated key. This is only a directional
scalar, not the full Kalman gain vector. This is only a directional match: it generally does not
recover the exact marginal variances or gains for arbitrary future keys, and
$s_{\mathrm{dir}}^\star>1$ makes the coordinatewise marginal overconfidence of reverse KL more
severe. Moreover, the exact root depends on the recurrent quantities $q_i=p_i k_i^2$. Computing it
online couples the channels and prevents the current recurrence from being represented by
input-only scalar M\"obius maps. A fixed or headwise learned $s$, or an input-only proxy such as
$\|\vct{k}\|_2^4/\|\vct{k}\|_4^4$ when the predicted variances are nearly isotropic, preserves
the existing associative scan but only amortizes the directional match. Restoring covariance
geometry more generally requires a richer state family.

\paragraph{Block reverse-KL hierarchy.}
A structured compromise is to partition the key coordinates into blocks
$\mathcal{G}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{G}_m$ of size at most $b$ and retain a dense covariance inside
each block. For a block-diagonal predicted covariance, reverse-KL projection onto this family
sets
\begin{equation}
  \vct{P}_g^{\mathrm{BR}}
  =\left[
      (\vct{P}_g^{-})^{-1}
      +\frac{\vct{k}_g\vct{k}_g^\top}{r}
    \right]^{-1}
  =\vct{P}_g^{-}
   -\frac{\vct{P}_g^{-}\vct{k}_g\vct{k}_g^\top\vct{P}_g^{-}}
     {r+z_g},
  \qquad
  z_g=\vct{k}_g^\top\vct{P}_g^{-}\vct{k}_g.
  \label{eq:discussion-block-reverse-kl}
\end{equation}
The current posterior mean remains the exact Kalman mean under the block-diagonal predictive
prior, with gain
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_g
  =\frac{\vct{P}_g^{-}\vct{k}_g}
    {r+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}z_\ell}.
  \label{eq:discussion-block-gain}
\end{equation}
For the diagonal incoming covariance used in
Equations~\eqref{eq:discussion-correlation-exact-posterior}--
\eqref{eq:discussion-correlation-diagonal-projections}, every $i\in\mathcal{G}_g$ obeys
\begin{equation}
  p_i^{\mathrm{rev}}
  \leq (\vct{P}_g^{\mathrm{BR}})_{ii}
  \leq p_i^{\mathrm{mom}},
  \label{eq:discussion-block-marginal-ordering}
\end{equation}
while the retained variance along the key is
\begin{equation}
  v^{\mathrm{BR}}
  =\sum_{g=1}^{m}f(z_g),
  \qquad
  v^{\star}\leq v^{\mathrm{BR}}
  \leq v^{\mathrm{rev}}\leq v^{\mathrm{mom}}.
  \label{eq:discussion-block-directional-ordering}
\end{equation}
Coarsening a nested block partition therefore moves the marginal variances upward toward their
exact values while moving the repeated-key directional variance downward toward its exact value.
Singleton blocks recover the diagonal reverse-KL update; one full block recovers the exact
covariance. Unlike interpolation between forward and reverse diagonal KL, this hierarchy improves
both quantities because it restores the missing correlations. These are one-step statements for a
fixed predictive prior. They do not order arbitrary future keys, trajectories with intervening
dynamics and process noise, realized updates with different residuals, or the loss after end-to-end
training.

This improvement has a controlled but nontrivial cost. With fixed block size $b$, the covariance
state uses $O(d_kb)$ numbers. Each block Riccati update can in principle be represented as a
matrix-valued linear-fractional map and composed associatively, replacing each scalar $2\times2$
M\"obius scan by a $2b\times2b$ block scan when the block transitions are invertible and the
transition and process covariance preserve the fixed partition. The current diagonal
parameterization satisfies these structural conditions. A straightforward combine costs
$O(d_kb^2)$ work across all blocks, and stable factorizations and backward kernels are
substantially more involved than in the diagonal implementation. The sum
$\sum_g z_g$ required by Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-block-gain} is token-local and does not
couple the recurrent block maps. A diagonal-plus-low-rank covariance captures correlations across
blocks more directly, but its rank grows with every observation; any fixed-rank truncation is
nonlinear and order dependent, so exact associativity is lost.

For $d_k=128$, a practical first hierarchy is the nested comparison
$b\in\{1,8,16\}$, with $b=128$ used only as the dense reference. The variants should be matched
for their initial and stationary $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$, or for measured memory half-life, so
that the comparison isolates covariance geometry rather than a different write temperature.
Diagnostics should include marginal-variance error against dense filtering, uncertainty in the
most recent key direction, gains on repeated and unrelated keys, and downstream retrieval loss.

Block reverse KL is therefore the clean covariance-based candidate when a heavier scan is
acceptable. Under the strict $O(d_k)$ scalar-scan budget, the process-noise-floor update in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:discussion-process-floor-approximation} remains the better fidelity
approximation: it lies between reverse KL and moment matching and preserves the independent
M\"obius maps. Because it cannot retain posterior correlations, however, it does not guarantee
less overwrite. An alternative is to replace covariance fidelity by fidelity to the filtering
action itself.

\subsection{From covariance projection to gain matching}
\label{subsec:discussion-gain-matching}

The desired approximation can be posed directly before choosing a divergence between
covariances. Define the gain operator
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf{K}(\vct{P};\vct{k},r)
  :=\frac{\vct{P}\vct{k}}
    {r+\vct{k}^{\top}\vct{P}\vct{k}}.
  \label{eq:discussion-gain-operator}
\end{equation}
Let $\vct{P}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}$ denote the covariance from the unprojected dense Kalman trajectory,
and restrict the retained diagonal to the bounded positive family
$\mathcal{P}=[p_{\min},p_{\max}]^{d_k}$ with
$0<p_{\min}<p_{\max}<\infty$. Given a probe distribution
$\nu_t$ over key--noise pairs $(\vct{\ell},r')$ applied immediately to that covariance, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \vct{p}_t^{\mathrm{GM}}
  \in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\vct{p}\in\mathcal{P}}
  \mathbb{E}_{(\vct{\ell},r')\sim\nu_t}\!\left[
    \Delta_t^{\mathrm{KF}}(\vct{\ell},r')
    \left\|
      \mathsf{K}(\diag(\vct{p});\vct{\ell},r')
      -\mathsf{K}(\vct{P}_t^{\mathrm{KF}};\vct{\ell},r')
    \right\|_2^2
  \right],\\
  \Delta_t^{\mathrm{KF}}(\vct{\ell},r')
  :=r'+\vct{\ell}^\top\vct{P}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}\vct{\ell}.
  \label{eq:discussion-gain-matching-projection}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Unlike reverse or forward KL, Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-gain-matching-projection} asks for the
diagonal state whose induced filtering action is closest to the exact action. A known transition
and process-noise covariance can instead be incorporated by applying the full prediction map
$\vct{P}\mapsto\vct{D}\vct{P}\vct{D}^\top+\vct{\Omega}$ to both covariances before evaluating
the same objective. The probe law
makes the required notion of closeness explicit: emphasizing the most recent key optimizes
repeated-key gain fidelity to the dense filter, whereas a broader law protects gain fidelity across
likely future observation-key
directions. There is no distribution-free best diagonal state because different probes expose
different missing cross-coordinate correlations.

The weight in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-gain-matching-projection} follows from filtering risk
rather than being chosen heuristically. For the exact dense predictive covariance
$\widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}$, let
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_t^{\mathrm{KF}}
  =r_t+\vct{k}_t^\top\widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}\vct{k}_t,
  \qquad
  \vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}
  =\mathsf{K}(\widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{KF}};\vct{k}_t,r_t).
  \label{eq:discussion-exact-gain-target}
\end{equation}
For one value column, applying an arbitrary gain $\vct{g}$ to the same predictive mean and prior
has one-step mean-squared error
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}(\vct{g})
  =\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
    (\vct{I}-\vct{g}\vct{k}_t^\top)
    \widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}
    (\vct{I}-\vct{g}\vct{k}_t^\top)^\top
    +r_t\vct{g}\vct{g}^\top
  \right].
  \label{eq:discussion-exact-gain-risk}
\end{equation}
Completing the square gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}(\vct{g})
  -\mathcal{R}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}(\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{KF}})
  =\Delta_t^{\mathrm{KF}}
   \|\vct{g}-\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}\|_2^2.
  \label{eq:discussion-excess-risk-gain-distance}
\end{equation}
Thus innovation-weighted gain error is not merely a convenient proxy: for this one-step problem,
it is exactly the excess Bayes risk of the approximate mean update for every $\vct{g}$.
Equivalently, since the innovation has variance
$\Delta_t^{\mathrm{KF}}$, it is the expected squared distance between the approximate and exact posterior
means. For $d_v$ conditionally independent value columns, both sides are multiplied by $d_v$.

This identity suggests the direct constrained problem
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_t^\dagger
  \in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\vct{g}\in\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathrm{scan},t}}
    \Delta_t^{\mathrm{KF}}
    \|\vct{g}-\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}\|_2^2,
  \label{eq:discussion-direct-gain-projection}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathrm{scan},t}$ is the closure of the gains realizable by the
chosen fixed-state associative recurrence. The pointwise diagonal family induced by
$\mathcal{P}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{diag}}(\vct{k}_t,r_t;\mathcal{P})
  =\left\{
    \mathsf{K}(\diag(\vct{p});\vct{k}_t,r_t):
    \vct{p}\in\mathcal{P}
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:discussion-diagonal-gain-family}
\end{equation}
Ignoring the finite bounds, its closure is
\begin{equation}
  \overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathrm{diag}}(\vct{k}_t)
  =\left\{
    \vct{g}:g_i=\gamma_i k_{t,i},\ \gamma_i\geq0,\
    \vct{k}_t^\top\vct{g}\leq1
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:discussion-diagonal-gain-closure}
\end{equation}
For finite strictly positive diagonal variances, $\gamma_i>0$ on the support of $\vct{k}_t$ and
$\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{g}<1$. Moreover, the pointwise family in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-diagonal-gain-family} is only a relaxation of the gains jointly
reachable by a shared recurrent scan state.
The exact dense product $\widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}\vct{k}_t$ can rotate the key, reverse an
individual coordinate sign, or be nonzero where $k_{t,i}=0$. If the resulting gain violates the
alignment conditions in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-diagonal-gain-closure}, no diagonal
covariance can reproduce it exactly. Failure to lie only in the bounded family of
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-diagonal-gain-family} instead means that no diagonal within the
chosen numerical range can reproduce it. Ignoring those finite bounds, when a pointwise match exists its
action-equivalent predictive diagonal,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{act}}
  =\frac{r_t\kappa_{t,i}^{\mathrm{KF}}}
    {k_{t,i}(1-\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{KF}})}
  =\frac{(\widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}\vct{k}_t)_i}{k_{t,i}},
  \label{eq:discussion-action-equivalent-current-diagonal}
\end{equation}
for coordinates with $k_{t,i}\neq0$, is key dependent, may fail positivity, and is not a reusable
approximation to the covariance. Computing it also already requires the dense covariance--key
product that the approximation is intended to avoid.

There is a further causal distinction between optimizing a gain and optimizing the posterior state.
The projected $\vct{p}_t$ is formed after $\vct{\kappa}_t$ has updated the memory, so changing
$\vct{p}_t$ cannot improve the gain at the same token. It first changes the prediction and gain at
token $t+1$. For a candidate diagonal state $\vct{p}$, define
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\vct{P}}_{t+1}(\vct{p})
  &=\vct{D}_{t+1}\diag(\vct{p})\vct{D}_{t+1}^\top
    +\vct{\Omega}_{t+1}, \notag\\
  \widehat{\vct{P}}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{KF}}
  &=\vct{D}_{t+1}\vct{P}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}\vct{D}_{t+1}^\top
    +\vct{\Omega}_{t+1}, \notag\\
  \vct{\kappa}_{t+1}(\vct{p})
  &=\mathsf{K}(\widehat{\vct{P}}_{t+1}(\vct{p});
    \vct{k}_{t+1},r_{t+1}), \notag\\
  \Delta_{t+1}^{\mathrm{KF}}
  &=r_{t+1}+\vct{k}_{t+1}^\top
    \widehat{\vct{P}}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{KF}}\vct{k}_{t+1},
  \qquad
  \vct{\kappa}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{KF}}
  =\mathsf{K}(\widehat{\vct{P}}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{KF}};
    \vct{k}_{t+1},r_{t+1}).
  \label{eq:discussion-candidate-next-gain}
\end{align}
The gain-aware analogue of a one-step covariance projection is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \vct{p}_t^\dagger
  \in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\vct{p}\in\mathcal{P}}
    \mathbb{E}_{\nu_t}\!\left[
      \Delta_{t+1}^{\mathrm{KF}}
      \|\vct{\kappa}_{t+1}(\vct{p})
       -\vct{\kappa}_{t+1}^{\mathrm{KF}}\|_2^2
    \right],
  \label{eq:discussion-gain-aware-state-projection}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_t(\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_t)$ is the conditional distribution of the next transition,
process-noise covariance, key, and observation noise. A discounted multi-step version replaces the integrand by the corresponding sum
over future gains. Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-gain-aware-state-projection} makes explicit that
there is no distribution-free best diagonal covariance: the useful approximation depends on which
future key directions will query the state.

\paragraph{A tractable local approximation.}
The exact objective is nonlinear because the candidate covariance appears in both the numerator
and denominator of $\mathsf{K}$, and evaluating it against
$\vct{P}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}$ requires dense matrix--vector products. A tractable local surrogate
replaces the globally rolled covariance by the exact one-step posterior $\vct{P}_t^\star$ formed
from the retained diagonal predictive prior. This target has the diagonal-minus-rank-one form in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-correlation-exact-posterior}. Suppressing the token index, for a probe
$(\vct{\ell},r')$, abbreviate
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}^\star(\vct{\ell},r')
  =\mathsf{K}(\vct{P}^\star;\vct{\ell},r'),
  \qquad
  \Delta^\star(\vct{\ell},r')
  =r'+\vct{\ell}^\top\vct{P}^\star\vct{\ell}.
  \label{eq:discussion-probe-gain-notation}
\end{equation}
Linearizing the gain around $\vct{P}^\star$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf{K}(\diag(\vct{p});\vct{\ell},r')
  -\vct{\kappa}^\star(\vct{\ell},r')
  \simeq
  \frac{
    (\vct{I}-\vct{\kappa}^\star(\vct{\ell},r')\vct{\ell}^\top)
    (\diag(\vct{p})-\vct{P}^\star)\vct{\ell}}
    {\Delta^\star(\vct{\ell},r')}.
  \label{eq:discussion-gain-linearization}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
  \vct{B}(\vct{\ell},r')
  =(\vct{I}-\vct{\kappa}^\star(\vct{\ell},r')\vct{\ell}^\top)
    \diag(\vct{\ell}),
  \qquad
  \vct{y}(\vct{\ell},r')
  =(\vct{I}-\vct{\kappa}^\star(\vct{\ell},r')\vct{\ell}^\top)
    \vct{P}^\star\vct{\ell}.
  \label{eq:discussion-gain-linearization-design}
\end{equation}
Substitution into Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-gain-matching-projection}, with
$\vct{P}_t^{\mathrm{KF}}$ replaced by this local target, yields the nonnegative
Jacobian-linearized quadratic problem
\begin{equation}
  \vct{p}_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
  \in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\vct{p}\in\mathcal{P}}
    \mathbb{E}_{\nu_t}\!\left[
      \frac{\|\vct{B}(\vct{\ell},r')\vct{p}
       -\vct{y}(\vct{\ell},r')\|_2^2}
      {\Delta^\star(\vct{\ell},r')}
    \right].
  \label{eq:discussion-linearized-gain-projection}
\end{equation}
For a finite set of probe keys, this is a box-constrained least-squares problem. It retains
the denominator sensitivity of the Kalman gain. Its normal matrix is generally dense, although for
$M$ probes it is a diagonal matrix plus a correction of rank at most $2M$.

A cheaper diagonal-Hessian approximation freezes the candidate denominator at
$\Delta^\star(\vct{\ell},r')$, equivalently dropping the left factor
$\vct{I}-\vct{\kappa}^\star\vct{\ell}^\top$ in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-gain-linearization}. The coordinates then decouple:
\begin{equation}
  p_{t,i}^{\mathrm{FD}}
  =\Pi_{[p_{\min},p_{\max}]}\!\left(
    \frac{
      \mathbb{E}_{\nu_t}\!\left[
        \ell_i(\vct{P}^\star\vct{\ell})_i/
        \Delta^\star(\vct{\ell},r')
      \right]}
    {
      \mathbb{E}_{\nu_t}\!\left[
        \ell_i^2/\Delta^\star(\vct{\ell},r')
      \right]}
  \right).
  \label{eq:discussion-frozen-denominator-gain-projection}
\end{equation}
Here $\Pi_{[p_{\min},p_{\max}]}$ clamps the unconstrained coordinatewise minimizer to the retained
variance range.
If a coordinate has zero probe energy, the objective does not identify that coordinate and its
previous or moment-matched value can be retained. When $\vct{P}_t^\star$ is the exact one-step
posterior in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-correlation-exact-posterior}, rename its incoming
diagonal $\vct{p}^{-}$ and set $z=\sum_i p_i^-k_i^2$. Every required product is then available in
linear time from
\begin{equation}
  \vct{P}_t^\star\vct{\ell}
  =\vct{p}^{-}\odot\vct{\ell}
   -\frac{(\vct{p}^{-}\odot\vct{k})
     (\vct{p}^{-}\odot\vct{k})^\top\vct{\ell}}
    {r+z}.
  \label{eq:discussion-rank-one-probe-product}
\end{equation}
Thus, for this local rank-one target, $M$ probe directions cost $O(Md_k)$ without storing the
dense covariance. A general exact-KLA trajectory instead requires dense matrix--vector products
unless additional covariance structure is retained. For isotropic probes in the high-noise regime,
where the weight $1/\Delta^\star$ is approximately direction independent,
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-frozen-denominator-gain-projection} reduces to the moment projection
$p_{t,i}=P_{t,ii}^\star$ when the box constraints are inactive; otherwise it gives the clipped
moment solution. A point mass on the most recent key similarly gives the action-equivalent solution
below when that solution is admissible. A mixture of these probe laws continuously trades global
gain fidelity against repeated-key gain fidelity.

\paragraph{A closed-form repeated-key example.}
The distinction from scalar directional matching is already visible in the one-step setting of
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-correlation-exact-posterior}. With no intervening dynamics or
process noise, define
\begin{equation}
  p_i^{\mathrm{act}}
  =\frac{r}{r+z}p_i^-.
  \label{eq:discussion-repeated-key-action-state}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \diag(\vct{p}^{\mathrm{act}})\vct{k}
  =\vct{P}^\star\vct{k}
  =\frac{r}{r+z}(\vct{p}^{-}\odot\vct{k}),
  \qquad
  \vct{k}^\top\diag(\vct{p}^{\mathrm{act}})\vct{k}
  =\vct{k}^\top\vct{P}^\star\vct{k}.
  \label{eq:discussion-repeated-key-action-match}
\end{equation}
When $\vct{p}^{\mathrm{act}}\in\mathcal{P}$, it therefore reproduces the entire exact gain vector
for the next identical key, for any next observation noise. Outside the chosen box it remains the
unconstrained action-equivalent reference. In contrast,
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-directional-scale-root} enforces only the second equality and thus
matches only $\beta^{\mathrm{eff}}$. The action-equivalent state is still not an exact covariance
projection: on coordinates where $k_i\neq0$ it is the unique diagonal action match, while
coordinates with $k_i=0$ are unconstrained. The uniform extension in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-repeated-key-action-state} chooses to shrink those unobserved
coordinates as well. It has no guarantee for a different future key and couples all channels
through $z$. It consequently cannot be inserted into the
current independent M\"obius maps without losing their input-only form.

\paragraph{Computational consequence.}
Equations~\eqref{eq:discussion-linearized-gain-projection} and
\eqref{eq:discussion-frozen-denominator-gain-projection} are local approximations to the ideal
gain objective, not variational projections. For the one-step rank-one target they require only
the structured products in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-rank-one-probe-product}; a general dense
target requires dense products. Their coefficients depend on the incoming recurrent state through
$\vct{p}^{-}$, $z$, and $\Delta^\star$. The resulting update couples channels and therefore is not an
input-only collection of scalar M\"obius maps. The diagonal-Hessian rule reduces arithmetic and
storage to $O(Md_k)$ for fixed $M$, but it does not by itself preserve the present associative gain
scan.

To retain that scan, one can restrict the candidate in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-gain-matching-projection} to the existing scaled family
$\vct{p}(s)$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-directional-scale-update}. A fixed or headwise
$s$, or a prescribed token-input-only rule for $s_t$, preserves an input-only local map while
matching only the part of the dense gain reachable by that one-dimensional family. The
repeated-key approximation $s\simeq d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-directional-effective-dimension} is a useful initialization for
matching the scalar effective gain; it solves the full vector objective only in balanced symmetric
cases. Solving the exact gain objective for $s_t$ from the recurrent covariance, or allowing the
probe weights to depend on that state, gives a closer local solution but destroys the independent
scan.

Finally, $\vct{\kappa}$ is invariant under the common scale gauge
\begin{equation}
  \vct{P}\mapsto\lambda\vct{P},
  \qquad \vct{p}\mapsto\lambda\vct{p},
  \qquad r\mapsto\lambda r,
  \qquad (p_{\min},p_{\max})\mapsto
    \lambda(p_{\min},p_{\max}).
  \label{eq:discussion-gain-matching-scale-gauge}
\end{equation}
The risk weight $\Delta^\star$ scales by $\lambda$. Under this simultaneous transformation the optimizer
scales with $\lambda$ and the objective is multiplied by a common constant. Holding the candidate
box fixed would break this invariance. A fixed gauge, for example $r\equiv1$, or normalized weights
are useful when objectives with independently free scales are aggregated across heads or steps;
unweighted gain error is gauge invariant but loses the exact excess-risk interpretation.
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-excess-risk-gain-distance} is also a local identity for a common
predictive mean and prior. Across multiple recursively projected steps, the gain objective remains
a decision-aware approximation criterion, but it is not equal to the total trajectory-level
filtering error.

Gain matching seeks fidelity to the exact Kalman action; it does not deliberately make that action
more conservative. If the task prefers additional retention beyond the approximate Kalman
operating point, that preference should be introduced separately rather than hidden inside a
covariance projection.

\subsection{Learning confidence as a bounded protection state}
\label{subsec:discussion-decision-aware-protection}

The gain-matching objective addresses approximation error, whereas the observed benefit of a
larger information scale points to a different failure mode: the carried state does not protect
past writes strongly enough from later updates. The appropriate intervention should therefore
increase the confidence passed forward, rather than merely truncate the current gain after it has
been computed. A larger protection scale changes this retention--plasticity operating point,
whereas the $B=2$ channelwise cap changes only those current gains for which
$\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}>2$. The cap can therefore be inactive even when repeated writes are too
strong; when it is active, it can remove useful anisotropy without materially changing
$\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum_i k_{t,i}^2\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}$. Its failure to improve accuracy
is thus evidence against extreme channelwise amplification as the primary bottleneck, not against
the broader overwrite hypothesis. For the same reason, an off-key projection cannot reproduce the
benefit of a larger information scale: by construction it preserves $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$.
This diagnosis should be accompanied by the cap activation rate and the removed effective-write
mass; a nearly inactive cap is a null intervention rather than evidence about the mechanism.

A more principled scan-compatible response is to make the retention--plasticity tradeoff explicit
in the inference objective rather than clipping the resulting gain. Let
$\rho_h\geq1$ denote a protection-to-write ratio for head $h$ and recall
$V_t=\vct{k}_t^\top\diag(\vct{p}_t)\vct{k}_t$. Starting from the ordinary reverse-KL objective
$\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{t,j}^{(1)}$ in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-negative-power-elbo-q-objective}, define
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{J}_{t,j}^{\mathrm{ret}}(q_{t,j};\rho_h)
  =\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{t,j}^{(1)}(q_{t,j})
   +\frac{\rho_h-1}{2r_t}V_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-retention-objective}
\end{equation}
The additional term prices uncertainty remaining in the direction that has just been written. Its
unique minimizer leaves the current posterior-mean correction unchanged and strengthens
only the precision retained for future tokens:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat c_{t,i}
  =\frac{c_{t-1,i}}
    {\alpha_{t,i}^2+\omega_{t,i}c_{t-1,i}},
  \qquad
  \widehat p_{t,i}=\widehat c_{t,i}^{-1},
  \qquad
  \vct{\kappa}_t
  =\frac{\widehat{\vct{p}}_t\odot\vct{k}_t}
    {r_t+\sum_i\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2},
  \qquad
  \vct{c}_t
  =\widehat{\vct{c}}_t
   +\rho_h\frac{\vct{k}_t\odot\vct{k}_t}{r_t}.
  \label{eq:discussion-protection-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\rho_h$ does not alter the write at the token that supplies the evidence, but it
reduces the gains of later tokens whose coordinatewise key energy overlaps that evidence. This is
consistent with the causal effect indicated when a larger information scale improves accuracy. It
is also the $(s_{\mathrm{write}},s_{\mathrm{protect}})=(1,\rho_h)$ specialization of the split
variational objective in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-split-variational-objective}.

Because protection is a multiplicative scale, the smallest useful parameterization is one bounded
log-scale scalar per head,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_h
  =\exp\!\left(
      \log\rho_{\min}
      +\sigma(\theta_h)\log\frac{\rho_{\max}}{\rho_{\min}}
    \right),
  \qquad
  1\leq\rho_{\min}<\rho_{\max},
  \label{eq:discussion-bounded-protection-ratio}
\end{equation}
initialized at the best fixed-scale operating point. A token-dependent $\rho_{t,h}$ remains
scan-compatible provided that it depends only on the current token, but the headwise form is more
identifiable and is the cleaner first experiment. Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-protection-recurrence}
only changes the token-local coefficients of the M\"obius maps, so both the uncertainty scan and
the affine memory scan are preserved. A positive process-noise floor remains necessary to restore
plasticity and prevent the learned protection ratio from freezing the memory.

To encourage rather than merely permit confidence, initialize $\rho_h$ at a fixed value
$\rho_0>1$ selected by the information-scale sweep and optionally use the weak log-scale anchor
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\rho}
  =\lambda_{\rho}\sum_h
    \left(\log\rho_h-\log\rho_0\right)^2.
  \label{eq:discussion-protection-anchor}
\end{equation}
Regularizing the raw state $\vct{c}_t$ itself is less suitable: its magnitude grows with the
evidence history and participates in a common covariance--noise scale symmetry. The global scale
should first be fixed, for example with $r_t\equiv1$ and a fixed prior scale, so that $\rho_h$ has
an identifiable retention meaning. A bounded $\rho_h$, together with
$\omega_{t,i}\geq\omega_{\min}>0$, lets training choose stronger protection without reaching the
degenerate zero-plasticity limit. Reducing $\omega_t$ would also increase confidence, but it changes
the assumed drift of the latent memory and is therefore a less targeted intervention.

For $\rho_h=1$, Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-retention-objective} reduces to ordinary variational
filtering. For $\rho_h>1$, it is a decision-aware variational objective rather than the ELBO of the
original linear--Gaussian observation model: a single Gaussian likelihood cannot leave the current
mean update unchanged while increasing only future confidence. If exact Bayesian semantics are
required, the write and protection scales must instead be tied and the process and observation
noise calibrated under that coherent model. Under the fixed-size associative-memory objective,
however, $\rho_h$ has a direct interpretation as the cost of future overwrite. The retained
precision should then be described as a gain-conditioning or protection state, while channelwise
and off-key caps remain optional worst-case guardrails rather than the primary retention mechanism.

\paragraph{Separating a reference uncertainty state from protection.}
If an independently testable uncertainty approximation and overwrite control are both useful, a
more explicit design carries two conceptually distinct states. Let
$\vct{P}_t^{\mathrm{unc}}$ be a fidelity-oriented reference covariance obtained from either the
moment projection or the scan-compatible process-noise-floor rule. Given its prediction, define
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{unc}}
  =\frac{\widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{unc}}\vct{k}_t}
    {\Delta_t^{\mathrm{unc}}},
  \qquad
  \Delta_t^{\mathrm{unc}}
  =r_t+\vct{k}_t^\top\widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{unc}}\vct{k}_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-calibrated-kalman-gain}
\end{equation}
For one value column and an arbitrary linear gain $\vct{g}$, use the one-step reference risk
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{R}_t^{\mathrm{ref}}(\vct{g})
  &=
  \operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
    (\vct{I}-\vct{g}\vct{k}_t^\top)
    \widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{unc}}
    (\vct{I}-\vct{g}\vct{k}_t^\top)^\top
    +r_t\vct{g}\vct{g}^\top
  \right] \\
  &=
  \mathcal{R}_t^{\mathrm{ref}}(\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{unc}})
  +\Delta_t^{\mathrm{unc}}
   \|\vct{g}-\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{unc}}\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-calibrated-gain-risk}
\end{equation}

The second state records the importance of past key directions. The full discounted Gram state
would be
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\vct{A}}_t
  =\eta_t\vct{A}_{t-1},
  \qquad
  \vct{A}_t
  =\widehat{\vct{A}}_t
   +\frac{\xi_t}{r_t}\vct{k}_t\vct{k}_t^\top,
  \qquad
  0<\eta_t\leq1,\quad \xi_t\geq0.
  \label{eq:discussion-protection-full-gram}
\end{equation}
For the rank-one memory change
$\Delta\vct{S}_t=\vct{g}\vct{e}_t^\top$, its discounted squared effect at the protected keys is
proportional to
$\|\vct{e}_t\|_2^2\vct{g}^\top\widehat{\vct{A}}_t\vct{g}$. Absorbing an expected or normalized
residual scale into $\lambda_{\mathrm{ow}}$, the regularized decision rule is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t
  &=\arg\min_{\vct{g}}\left\{
      \mathcal{R}_t^{\mathrm{ref}}(\vct{g})
      +\lambda_{\mathrm{ow}}
       \vct{g}^\top\widehat{\vct{A}}_t\vct{g}
    \right\} \\
  &=\left(
      \Delta_t^{\mathrm{unc}}\vct{I}
      +\lambda_{\mathrm{ow}}\widehat{\vct{A}}_t
    \right)^{-1}
    \Delta_t^{\mathrm{unc}}\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{unc}}.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-overwrite-regularized-gain}
\end{equation}
This penalizes a write according to its predicted interference with previously important keys
rather than according to an instantaneous channel threshold.

The full Gram state costs $O(d_k^2)$. Its diagonal approximation,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\vct{a}}_t
  =\eta_t\vct{a}_{t-1},
  \qquad
  \vct{a}_t
  =\widehat{\vct{a}}_t
   +\xi_t\frac{\vct{k}_t\odot\vct{k}_t}{r_t},
  \qquad
  \overline\kappa_{t,i}
  =\frac{\Delta_t^{\mathrm{unc}}}
    {\Delta_t^{\mathrm{unc}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{ow}}\widehat a_{t,i}}
    \kappa_{t,i}^{\mathrm{unc}},
  \label{eq:discussion-protection-importance-scan}
\end{equation}
is a basis-dependent surrogate for key-overlap interference, but its recurrence is an associative
affine scan whenever $\eta_t$ and $\xi_t$ depend only on the current token. An isotropic
$\widehat{\vct{A}}_t=a_t\vct{I}$ instead gives basis-invariant radial shrinkage. Combined with the
process-noise-floor approximation, the diagonal design requires one M\"obius uncertainty scan and
one affine importance scan before the unchanged affine memory scan, and remains $O(d_k)$ in
recurrent state. A moment-projected uncertainty state does not retain this scan property.

There is an unavoidable consistency caveat. Once
$\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t\ne\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{unc}}$,
$\vct{P}_t^{\mathrm{unc}}$ is a counterfactual reference state, not the error covariance around the
actual regularized mean. The covariance consistent with that mean would use the Joseph update
\begin{equation}
  \vct{P}_t^{\mathrm{err}}
  =(\vct{I}-\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t\vct{k}_t^\top)
    \widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{\mathrm{err}}
    (\vct{I}-\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t\vct{k}_t^\top)^\top
   +r_t\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t^\top.
  \label{eq:discussion-protected-joseph-covariance}
\end{equation}
Projecting this covariance feeds the protection state back into the uncertainty recurrence and
generally loses the independent scalar M\"obius scan. The two-state construction is therefore an
honest decision-aware controller with a reference uncertainty model, not an exact Bayesian filter.
If covariance around the actual mean must remain calibrated, the structured or sequential
alternatives above are required. Otherwise the single bounded $\rho_h$ protection state is the
lower-cost design supported most directly by the present ablation.

\subsection{A bounded residual can hide an unbounded channel gain}

Let
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_t
  =r_t+\sum_j\widehat p_{t,j}k_{t,j}^2,
  \qquad
  \beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}
  =\frac{\widehat p_{t,i}}{\Delta_t},
  \qquad
  \kappa_{t,i}=\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}k_{t,i}.
  \label{eq:discussion-channel-write-strength}
\end{equation}
Here $\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the per-channel write multiplier, not a probability or a
residual-contraction fraction. For an $\ell_2$-normalized key, its key-energy-weighted average is
the effective write strength
\begin{equation}
  \beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}
  =\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\kappa}_t
  =\sum_i\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}k_{t,i}^2
  =\frac{\sum_i\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2}{\Delta_t}
  \in[0,1).
  \label{eq:discussion-channel-effective-write}
\end{equation}
Consequently the residual at the current key contracts safely by
$1-\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$. This scalar guarantee does not bound any individual
$\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}$, the gain norm, or the effect at another key.

The failure is structural. Fix $b\in(0,1)$, set $z=b/(1-b)$ and $r_t=1$, and consider
\begin{equation}
  \vct{k}_\epsilon
  =\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon&\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}\end{bmatrix}^{\!\top},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\vct{p}}_\epsilon
  =\begin{bmatrix}
      \dfrac{z}{2\epsilon^2}&\dfrac{z}{2(1-\epsilon^2)}
    \end{bmatrix}^{\!\top}.
  \label{eq:discussion-unbounded-channel-construction}
\end{equation}
For every $\epsilon\in(0,1)$, the key is normalized and
$\sum_i\widehat p_i k_i^2=z$, so $\beta^{\mathrm{eff}}=b$ is fixed. Nevertheless,
\begin{equation}
  \beta_1^{\mathrm{ch}}=\frac{b}{2\epsilon^2}\longrightarrow\infty,
  \qquad
  \kappa_1=\frac{b}{2\epsilon}\longrightarrow\infty
  \qquad(\epsilon\to0).
  \label{eq:discussion-unbounded-channel-limit}
\end{equation}
A small key coordinate can therefore hide arbitrarily large channel uncertainty from the shared
denominator. With residual
$\vct{e}_t=\vct{v}_t-\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t$, the induced change at a unit read key
$\vct{q}$ is
\begin{equation}
  (\vct{S}_t-\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t)^\top\vct{q}
  =\vct{e}_t(\vct{\kappa}_t^\top\vct{q}).
  \label{eq:discussion-cross-key-overwrite}
\end{equation}
Thus the same-key residual can remain contractive while an off-key read, or the nonnormal erase
operator $\vct{I}-\vct{\kappa}_t\vct{k}_t^\top$, is arbitrarily amplified. This is the
channelwise overwrite failure that $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ alone cannot detect.

\subsection{Capping the channelwise write strength}

A direct trust region clips the channel multipliers before the memory update:
\begin{equation}
  \overline\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}
  =\operatorname{clip}_{[0,B]}\!\left(\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}\right),
  \qquad
  \overline\kappa_{t,i}
  =\overline\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}k_{t,i}.
  \label{eq:discussion-channel-gain-cap}
\end{equation}
The lower bound is automatic under the positive covariance model; it is written explicitly to
state the admissible gain class. Because the cap only decreases nonnegative coefficients,
\begin{equation}
  0\leq\overline\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}
  :=\vct{k}_t^\top\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t
  \leq\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}<1.
  \label{eq:discussion-capped-effective-write}
\end{equation}
The capped update therefore preserves same-key residual contraction and cannot overshoot that
residual. More importantly, unit-normalized keys give the new gain-norm bound
\begin{equation}
  \|\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t\|_2^2
  =\sum_i(\overline\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}})^2k_{t,i}^2
  \leq B\sum_i\overline\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}k_{t,i}^2
  =B\overline\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}<B.
  \label{eq:discussion-capped-gain-norm}
\end{equation}
For the proposed $B=2$, the gain norm is strictly below $\sqrt{2}$. If
$\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t
=\overline\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\vct{k}_t
+\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_{t,\perp}$, then
\begin{equation}
  \|\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_{t,\perp}\|_2^2
  \leq2\overline\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}
       -(\overline\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}})^2
  \leq1.
  \label{eq:discussion-capped-off-key-gain}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
$\|(\vct{I}-\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t\vct{k}_t^\top)\vct{k}_t\|_2\leq1$
and
$\|\vct{I}-\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t\vct{k}_t^\top\|_2\leq\sqrt{2}$.
The operator need not be nonexpansive when an off-key component remains, but it is now uniformly
bounded. The realized correction at any unit read key satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \|(\vct{S}_t-\widetilde{\vct{S}}_t)^\top\vct{q}\|_2
  \leq\sqrt{2}\,\|\vct{e}_t\|_2.
  \label{eq:discussion-capped-cross-key-write}
\end{equation}
The value $B=2$ is also permissive around the isotropic regime: if
$\widehat p_{t,i}=\widehat p_t$ for every channel, then
$\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}=\widehat p_t/(r_t+\widehat p_t)<1$, so the cap is inactive. It targets
only anisotropic amplification beyond the scalar-gain range.

The cap has a constrained estimation interpretation. For any linear gain $\vct{g}$, let
$\mathcal{R}_t(\vct{g})$ be the trace of its conditional estimation-error covariance. Completing
the square around the Kalman gain $\vct{\kappa}_t$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}_t(\vct{g})
  =\mathcal{R}_t(\vct{\kappa}_t)
   +\Delta_t\|\vct{g}-\vct{\kappa}_t\|_2^2.
  \label{eq:discussion-capped-gain-risk}
\end{equation}
Minimizing this risk over
$g_i=\gamma_i k_{t,i}$ with $0\leq\gamma_i\leq B$ yields
$\gamma_i=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,B]}(\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}})$, exactly
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-channel-gain-cap}. The cap is therefore the minimum-risk linear
correction within this bounded channel-aligned class.

This constrained correction is no longer the unconstrained Kalman posterior mean when the cap is
active. Retaining the original information update also leaves the confidence state unchanged even
though less of the residual was assimilated, so the mean and covariance no longer describe one
Gaussian posterior. The cap should therefore be presented as an overwrite guardrail, not as a
solution to reverse-KL overconfidence. A covariance update consistent with the capped gain would
use its Joseph error covariance and would generally couple channels, forfeiting the independent
M\"obius scan. The gain-only cap is token-local after that scan and before the affine memory scan,
so it preserves the current parallel algorithm. A hard cap has zero gain-path gradient in saturated
channels; a smooth saturation is an alternative if this impedes training.

\subsection{Gain-norm and off-key control}
\label{subsec:discussion-gain-norm-control}

The channelwise cap in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-channel-gain-cap} acts in the coordinate
system of the diagonal covariance. Two complementary alternatives operate directly on the gain
vector: a radial cap bounds the entire update, while an off-key cap changes only the component
responsible for anisotropic cross-key interference.

A basis-invariant radial cap projects the gain onto an $\ell_2$ ball. Define
\begin{equation}
  m_t=\operatorname{RMS}(\vct{\kappa}_t)
     =\frac{\|\vct{\kappa}_t\|_2}{\sqrt{d_k}},
  \qquad
  a_t^{\mathrm{rad}}
     =\min\!\left\{1,\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{rms}}}{m_t}\right\},
  \qquad
  \vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{rad}}
     =a_t^{\mathrm{rad}}\vct{\kappa}_t,
  \label{eq:discussion-radial-rms-cap}
\end{equation}
with $a_t^{\mathrm{rad}}=1$ when $m_t=0$. This one-sided RMS cap is exactly radial norm clipping
with radius $\tau_2=\sqrt{d_k}\tau_{\mathrm{rms}}$. It preserves the gain direction and satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \|\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{rad}}\|_2\leq\tau_2,
  \qquad
  \beta_{t,\mathrm{rad}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
    =a_t^{\mathrm{rad}}\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}
    \leq\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}<1.
  \label{eq:discussion-radial-rms-guarantees}
\end{equation}
It therefore bounds every cross-key correction, but also weakens the intended same-key write
whenever active. Choosing $\tau_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{2/d_k}$ matches the
$\|\overline{\vct{\kappa}}_t\|_2<\sqrt{2}$ ceiling of the $B=2$ channel cap. A raw RMS threshold
of $2$ would instead permit norm $2\sqrt{d_k}$ and is not comparable to a channel cap of $2$.

To change only the anisotropic component, decompose
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp}
    =\vct{\kappa}_t-\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\vct{k}_t,
  \qquad
  a_t^\perp
    =\min\!\left\{1,
      \frac{\tau_{\perp,t}}{\|\vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp}\|_2}\right\},
  \qquad
  \vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{off}}
    =\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\vct{k}_t
     +a_t^\perp\vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp},
  \label{eq:discussion-off-key-gain-cap}
\end{equation}
taking $a_t^\perp=1$ when the off-key norm is zero. This preserves
$\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{off}}=\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ exactly, so the current
residual contraction is unchanged, while every unit
$\vct{q}\perp\vct{k}_t$ obeys
$|\vct{q}^\top\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{off}}|\leq\tau_{\perp,t}$.
A fixed $\tau_\perp$ gives a direct cross-talk budget. The adaptive choice
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{\perp,t}
    =\sqrt{2\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}
           -(\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}})^2}
  \label{eq:discussion-off-key-adaptive-radius}
\end{equation}
yields
$\|\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{off}}\|_2^2
  \leq2\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ and
$\|(\vct{I}-\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{off}}\vct{k}_t^\top)\vct{k}_t\|_2\leq1$,
matching the useful $B=2$ envelope without changing the scalar write strength. Setting
$\tau_{\perp,t}=0$ recovers the key-aligned gain
$\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\vct{k}_t$; a learned shrinkage between these extremes is the continuous
version of the $\eta\in\{0,1\}$ ablation in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-gain-direction-ablation}.

Full RMS normalization is qualitatively different and is not recommended here. For a nonzero
gain, setting
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{RMSNorm}}
    =\frac{\rho}{\operatorname{RMS}(\vct{\kappa}_t)}\vct{\kappa}_t
  \label{eq:discussion-full-rms-normalization}
\end{equation}
forces $\|\vct{\kappa}_t^{\mathrm{RMSNorm}}\|_2=\rho\sqrt{d_k}$ and replaces the effective
write by
$\rho\sqrt{d_k}\,\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}/\|\vct{\kappa}_t\|_2$. It rescales small and large
gains alike and can amplify a nearly zero gain that the uncertainty state intentionally
suppressed. In the isotropic case
$\vct{\kappa}_t=\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\vct{k}_t$, every nonzero write becomes
$\beta_{t,\mathrm{RMSNorm}}^{\mathrm{eff}}=\rho\sqrt{d_k}$, independent of its original
confidence. Hence a conventional unit-RMS normalization can destroy residual contraction. The
one-sided cap in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-radial-rms-cap} is preferable because it never
amplifies a gain. Both norm controls remain token-local and preserve the two scans when the
information recurrence is left unchanged; when active, however, they share the mean--covariance
inconsistency noted above.

\subsection{Available controls and what each one solves}

Together with the channelwise and norm controls above, the remaining principal interventions are
as follows.
\begin{description}
  \item[Future-protection scale.]
  Increasing $s_{\mathrm{protect}}$ leaves the current write unchanged locally and suppresses later
  gains. It directly addresses repeated overwrite, but deliberately increases confidence and can
  freeze rewriting if taken too far.

  \item[Coherent current-write temperature.]
  Increasing $r_t$, or equivalently reducing a likelihood-precision multiplier in both the gain
  and posterior update, weakens current assimilation under a valid Gaussian observation model.
  Because it also leaves more posterior uncertainty, it need not protect against later writes.

  \item[Effective-write cap.]
  Rescaling the complete gain by
  $\min\{1,\beta_{\max}/\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}\}$ for
  $0<\beta_{\max}<1$ directly limits same-key overwrite while preserving the gain direction. It
  does not control a large off-key component when $\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ is already below the
  threshold.

  \item[Better calibrated uncertainty.]
  Reducing $s_{\mathrm{protect}}$, inflating the retained covariance, or keeping a positive process
  noise floor prevents the state from becoming too confident. The process-floor approximation is
  the tighter scan-compatible projection developed above; the moment projection matches the exact
  one-step marginal variances but loses the independent M\"obius scan; structured or dense
  covariance tracking is more faithful still and more expensive. These changes can increase future
  gains, so better posterior calibration need not reduce overwrite.

  \item[Noise-scale and gauge controls.]
  Fixing $r=1$ and $\mu=1$, bounding relative observation reliability, and starting process noise
  isotropically improve identifiability and avoid extreme gain temperatures. Reducing process
  noise can suppress future writes but risks covariance windup; increasing transition decay is not
  clean protection because it erases the stored memory directly.

  \item[Residual, update, or erase clipping.]
  Clipping $\|\vct{e}_t\|_2$ or the realized rank-one update controls large values as well as large
  gains. Separating the write and erase gates can limit destructive editing directly. Both options
  depart further from the Kalman residual update, and residual-dependent nonlinearities generally
  lose the input-only affine memory scan.
\end{description}

The clean experiment crosses one confidence-state intervention
($s=1$, $s=d_k$, process-floor, or moment projection) with one current-write intervention
(uncapped, an effective-write cap, the $B=2$ channel cap, or off-key shrinkage). It should report
$\beta_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$, $\max_i\beta_{t,i}^{\mathrm{ch}}$,
$\|\vct{\kappa}_t\|_2$, $\|\vct{\kappa}_{t,\perp}\|_2$, cap activation rate, residual magnitude,
and write--erase magnitude. This factorial comparison separates posterior calibration from
overwrite control instead of asking one scalar information scale to solve both.

\section{Mamba as a Controlled-Prediction Specialization}
\label{sec:discussion-mamba-controlled-prediction}

The zero-mean process model used for KDN does not, as written, contain the additive input write of
Mamba. The minimal extension is to include a known token-dependent control in the state dynamics,
\begin{equation}
  \vct{S}_t^\star
  =\vct{D}_t\vct{S}_{t-1}^\star+\vct{U}_t+\widetilde{\vct{W}}_t,
  \qquad
  \widetilde{\vct{W}}_t
  \sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{col}}(\vct{0},\vct{\Omega}_t).
  \label{eq:discussion-controlled-process}
\end{equation}
Conditioned on the token-generated coefficients, both the transition and $\vct{U}_t$ are known,
so the recurrence remains linear and $\vct{U}_t$ is deterministic. Equivalently, the control is
the conditional mean of the original process increment, separated from its centered random part.
Consequently, it changes the predicted memory but not its covariance:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\vct{S}}_t
  =\vct{D}_t\vct{S}_{t-1}+\vct{U}_t,
  \qquad
  \widehat{\vct{p}}_t
  =\vct{\alpha}_t^2\odot\vct{p}_{t-1}+\vct{\omega}_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-controlled-prediction}
\end{equation}
This is therefore a controlled linear--Gaussian state-space model with exactly the same
uncertainty scan as KDN.

The per-head SSD/Mamba-2 core can be written in matrix-state form as
\begin{equation}
  \vct{H}_t
  =a_t\vct{H}_{t-1}+\vct{B}_t\vct{x}_t^\top,
  \qquad
  \vct{o}_t
  =\vct{H}_t^\top\vct{C}_t+\vct{D}_{\mathrm{skip}}\vct{x}_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-mamba-ssd}
\end{equation}
where the discretization scale is absorbed into $\vct{B}_t$.
It is recovered from Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-controlled-process} by setting
$\vct{S}_t^\star=\vct{H}_t$, $\vct{D}_t=a_t\vct{I}$,
$\vct{U}_t=\vct{B}_t\vct{x}_t^\top$, and $\vct{\Omega}_t=\vct{0}$, with
$\vct{C}_t$ serving as the read query. The direct path, output gates, and surrounding projections
sit outside this recurrent state equation. Mamba-1 fits the same template after vectorizing its
channel- and state-dependent diagonal dynamics. Mamba-3 SISO replaces the single rank-one control
by a trapezoidal two-token control of the form
$\vct{U}_t=\beta_t\vct{B}_{t-1}\vct{x}_{t-1}^\top
+\gamma_t\vct{B}_t\vct{x}_t^\top$, while its MIMO variant uses a finite-rank sum of such
controls. These remain deterministic controls once their token-generated coefficients are given.

This mapping is exact at the recurrent-state level, but it does not make Mamba a nontrivial
special case of the Kalman posterior update. With the controlled prediction above, KDN would
still apply
\begin{equation}
  \vct{S}_t
  =\widehat{\vct{S}}_t
   +\vct{\kappa}_t
    \left(\vct{v}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t\right)^\top.
  \label{eq:discussion-controlled-correction}
\end{equation}
The standard Mamba recurrence omits this observation-correction step: its additive write is a
known control, its $\vct{C}_t$ is a read query rather than a measurement used to condition the
state, and it carries no explicit filtering covariance under this embedding. Trying instead to
reinterpret the Mamba write as an observation correction by setting
$\vct{\kappa}_t=\vct{B}_t$ and $\vct{v}_t=\vct{x}_t$ would introduce the additional erase term
$-\vct{\kappa}_t\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{D}_t\vct{S}_{t-1}$, which is absent from Mamba.

The precise unification is thus a division of roles within the controlled state-space template.
Mamba is its zero-noise, prediction-only specialization, with $\vct{U}_t\neq\vct{0}$ and no
observation correction. Delta-rule models set $\vct{U}_t=\vct{0}$ and prescribe a residual gain,
whereas KDN derives that gain from propagated uncertainty. A combined model could use both a
selective-SSM control and an uncertainty-weighted innovation, but that hybrid is a new model rather
than an alternative interpretation of existing Mamba. Accordingly, the defensible paper-level
claim is that Mamba and KDN share a controlled matrix-state scaffold and specialize complementary
prediction and correction branches---not that Mamba itself performs Kalman filtering.

\section{Predictive Innovation Likelihood and Covariance Calibration}
\label{sec:discussion-predictive-innovation-likelihood}

The language-modeling objective can learn useful uncertainty-dependent gates without making their
numerical values calibrated variances. A direct way to test, and optionally train, the probabilistic
interpretation is to score the current value under the one-step predictive distribution. This
criterion is distinct from the expected likelihood inside the variational objective above: it uses
the uncertainty before the current observation is assimilated and evaluates the marginal
distribution of that observation.

\subsection{Marginal likelihood of the pre-write innovation}

For value coordinate $j$, the predicted state and observation model are
\begin{equation}
  \vct{s}_{t,j}^{\star}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
  \sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j},\widehat{\vct{P}}_t),
  \qquad
  v_{t,j}=\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{s}_{t,j}^{\star}+e_{t,j},
  \qquad
  e_{t,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,r_t).
  \label{eq:discussion-predictive-nll-model}
\end{equation}
Marginalizing the uncertain state gives
\begin{equation}
  v_{t,j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},\vct{k}_t
  \sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat v_{t,j},\Delta_t),
  \qquad
  \widehat v_{t,j}=\vct{k}_t^\top\widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j},
  \qquad
  \Delta_t=r_t+\vct{k}_t^\top\widehat{\vct{P}}_t\vct{k}_t.
  \label{eq:discussion-predictive-innovation-distribution}
\end{equation}
Thus $\Delta_t$ combines irreducible observation noise with uncertainty in the predicted memory
along the current key. Under the columnwise model, all $d_v$ value coordinates share this scalar
variance. With
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\delta}_t
  =\vct{v}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t,
  \label{eq:discussion-predictive-nll-innovation}
\end{equation}
their joint predictive negative log likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred},t}
  =\frac{\|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2}{2\Delta_t}
   +\frac{d_v}{2}\log\Delta_t
   +\frac{d_v}{2}\log(2\pi).
  \label{eq:discussion-predictive-innovation-nll}
\end{equation}
An equivalent dimension-normalized loss, up to an additive constant, is
\begin{equation}
  \overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{pred},t}
  =\frac12\left(
    \frac{d_v^{-1}\|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2}{\Delta_t}
    +\log\Delta_t
  \right).
  \label{eq:discussion-predictive-innovation-nll-normalized}
\end{equation}
Both the innovation and its variance must use the exclusive, pre-write state. Evaluating the
residual after the update would let the current value help predict itself and would systematically
understate the forecasting error.

The two NLL terms prevent opposite degeneracies. The standardized squared error
$\|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2/\Delta_t$ penalizes a variance that is too small for the realized
innovation, while $d_v\log\Delta_t$ penalizes variance inflation. In particular,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred},t}}
       {\partial\log\Delta_t}
  =\frac12\left(
    d_v-\frac{\|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2}{\Delta_t}
  \right),
  \qquad
  \Delta_t^{\star}
  =\frac{\|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2}{d_v}
  \quad\text{for a fixed realized innovation}.
  \label{eq:discussion-predictive-nll-optimum}
\end{equation}
Across repeated contexts, the population optimum is the conditional mean squared innovation per
value coordinate. The log term is essential whenever an uncertainty scale is learned: omitting it
leaves the objective free to reduce the standardized error by sending the variance to infinity.

\subsection{Diagonal and isotropic specializations}

For Diagonal KDN, the predictive covariance is
$\widehat{\vct{P}}_t=\diag(\widehat{\vct{p}}_t)$, so the scored innovation variance is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_t^{\mathrm{diag}}
  =r_t+\sum_i\widehat p_{t,i}k_{t,i}^2.
  \label{eq:discussion-diag-predictive-innovation-variance}
\end{equation}
This is exactly the scalar denominator of the Kalman gain. The predictive likelihood therefore
tests whether the same uncertainty that controls the write also predicts the observed residual
scale. It scores only the directional covariance visible at the current key; it cannot by itself
detect covariance error in directions that the observed keys do not probe.

For Isotropic KDN, $\widehat{\vct{P}}_t=\widehat b_t\vct{I}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_t^{\mathrm{iso}}
  =r_t+\widehat b_t\|\vct{k}_t\|_2^2,
  \label{eq:discussion-iso-predictive-innovation-variance}
\end{equation}
which reduces to $r_t+\widehat b_t$ for unit-normalized keys. The same NLL can therefore compare
the two covariance families without changing their state budgets: each model is scored using the
predictive variance implied by its own retained state.

\subsection{Relation to the VAE objective and variational expected NLL}

The connection to a standard variational autoencoder (VAE) objective is algebraic. For one value
coordinate, let
$\widehat\pi_{t,j}=p(\vct{s}_{t,j}^{\star}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})$ denote the pre-write predictive
prior, and let $q_{t,j}=\mathcal{N}(\vct{\mu}_{t,j},\vct{P}_t)$ be a candidate filtering posterior
that may depend on the current observation $v_{t,j}$. The negative ELBO has the familiar
reconstruction-plus-regularization form
\begin{equation}
  -\operatorname{ELBO}_{t,j}(q_{t,j})
  =
  \underbrace{
    \mathbb{E}_{q_{t,j}}\!\left[
      -\log p(v_{t,j}\mid\vct{s}_{t,j}^{\star},\vct{k}_t)
    \right]
  }_{\text{expected reconstruction NLL}}
  +
  \underbrace{
    \operatorname{KL}\!\left(q_{t,j}\,\|\,\widehat\pi_{t,j}\right)
  }_{\text{regularization to the predictive prior}}.
  \label{eq:discussion-negative-elbo-decomposition}
\end{equation}
For the Gaussian observation model, the reconstruction term is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}_{q_{t,j}}\!\left[
    -\log p(v_{t,j}\mid\vct{s}_{t,j}^{\star},\vct{k}_t)
  \right]
  =\frac{1}{2r_t}\left[
    \left(v_{t,j}-\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\mu}_{t,j}\right)^2
    +\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{P}_t\vct{k}_t
  \right]
  +\frac12\log(2\pi r_t).
  \label{eq:discussion-expected-versus-predictive-nll}
\end{equation}
This term is evaluated under a post-observation distribution: $q_{t,j}$ is selected after
$v_{t,j}$ has been observed, so its mean may already incorporate the value being reconstructed.
The KL term charges the posterior for moving away from the pre-write prior. The reconstruction
term alone is therefore not a one-step forecasting score.

By contrast, the predictive innovation NLL marginalizes the latent state under the pre-write
prior,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred},t,j}
  =-\log p(v_{t,j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},\vct{k}_t)
  =-\log\!\int
    p(v_{t,j}\mid\vct{s},\vct{k}_t)
    \widehat\pi_{t,j}(\vct{s})\,d\vct{s}.
  \label{eq:discussion-predictive-nll-marginal}
\end{equation}
It therefore evaluates whether the exclusive pre-write mean and variance predict the current
innovation. In the Gaussian model, this integral is available exactly and yields the scalar
coordinate terms summed in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-predictive-innovation-nll}; no latent
sampling or variational bound is needed to evaluate it.

Bayes' rule gives the exact variational identity
\begin{equation}
  -\operatorname{ELBO}_{t,j}(q_{t,j})
  =\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred},t,j}
   +\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
      q_{t,j}\,\|\,
      p(\vct{s}_{t,j}^{\star}\mid
        v_{t,j},\mathcal{F}_{t-1},\vct{k}_t)
    \right).
  \label{eq:discussion-predictive-nll-variational-identity}
\end{equation}
Thus the negative ELBO upper-bounds the predictive marginal NLL and equals it when $q_{t,j}$ is
the exact posterior. A diagonal or isotropic variational family generally leaves a nonnegative
gap equal to its reverse-KL error. Importantly, it is the complete negative ELBO, not the expected
reconstruction term in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-expected-versus-predictive-nll}, that equals
the predictive NLL at the exact posterior.

The analogy to a VAE is therefore limited. The latent memory column plays the role of the VAE
latent variable, the value coordinate plays the role of the observation, and $q_{t,j}$ plays an
encoder-like role. Here, however, inference is an online filtering update with a history-dependent
prior, the linear-Gaussian predictive marginal is analytic, and both $\vct{k}_t$ and $\vct{v}_t$
are learned internal representations rather than observed language tokens. Consequently, the
predictive NLL is an internal probabilistic-consistency and covariance-calibration objective, not
a variational lower bound on the language-model likelihood.

Finally, a single Gaussian likelihood assigns the same multiplier to the mean-residual and
posterior-variance terms in Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-expected-versus-predictive-nll}. The
unequal $(s_{\mathrm{write}},s_{\mathrm{protect}})$ objective above remains a valid
decision-oriented criterion, but it is not the ELBO of one coherent Gaussian observation model.
If $r_t$ is learned, the $\log r_t$ normalizer must be retained; otherwise increasing $r_t$ can
reduce both quadratic terms without paying the corresponding likelihood cost.

\subsection{A VAE-style auxiliary loss on the latent memory}

The preceding identity also suggests an explicit variational auxiliary loss on the complete
memory state. Under the columnwise model, define the post-write approximation and pre-write prior
over the latent memory matrix by
\begin{equation}
  q_t(\vct{S}^{\star})
  =\prod_{j=1}^{d_v}
    \mathcal{N}(\vct{s}^{\star}_{:,j};\vct{s}_{t,j},\vct{P}_t),
  \qquad
  \widehat\pi_t(\vct{S}^{\star})
  =\prod_{j=1}^{d_v}
    \mathcal{N}(\vct{s}^{\star}_{:,j};
      \widehat{\vct{s}}_{t,j},\widehat{\vct{P}}_t).
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-variational-family}
\end{equation}
Here $\vct{S}_t$ is the post-write posterior mean represented by the recurrent memory, whereas
$\widehat{\vct{S}}_t$ is its exclusive pre-write prediction. With
\begin{equation}
  p(\vct{v}_t\mid\vct{S}^{\star},\vct{k}_t)
  =\mathcal{N}\!\left(
    \vct{v}_t;(\vct{S}^{\star})^\top\vct{k}_t,
    r_t\vct{I}_{d_v}
  \right),
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-observation-model}
\end{equation}
the VAE-style memory loss is the negative one-step ELBO
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{mem},t}
  =\underbrace{
    \mathbb{E}_{q_t}\!\left[
      -\log p(\vct{v}_t\mid\vct{S}^{\star},\vct{k}_t)
    \right]
  }_{\text{memory reconstruction}}
  +\underbrace{
    \operatorname{KL}\!\left(q_t\,\|\,\widehat\pi_t\right)
  }_{\text{regularization to the pre-write memory}}.
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-vae-loss}
\end{equation}
No stochastic memory sample is required. Because both distributions and the observation model are
Gaussian, the two terms are available in closed form. The reconstruction term is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}_{q_t}\!\left[
    -\log p(\vct{v}_t\mid\vct{S}^{\star},\vct{k}_t)
  \right]
  =\frac{1}{2r_t}\left[
    \left\|\vct{v}_t-\vct{S}_t^\top\vct{k}_t\right\|_2^2
    +d_v\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{P}_t\vct{k}_t
  \right]
  +\frac{d_v}{2}\log(2\pi r_t),
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-reconstruction-closed-form}
\end{equation}
and the KL term is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{KL}(q_t\,\|\,\widehat\pi_t)
  =\frac12\Bigg\{
  &\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
    \widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{-1}
    (\vct{S}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t)
    (\vct{S}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t)^\top
  \right] \\
  &+d_v\left[
    \operatorname{tr}(\widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{-1}\vct{P}_t)
    -d_k
    +\log\frac{\det\widehat{\vct{P}}_t}{\det\vct{P}_t}
  \right]
  \Bigg\}.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-kl-closed-form}
\end{equation}
For diagonal or isotropic covariances, the traces and log determinants reduce to elementwise sums,
so this loss has no sampling estimator and does not require materializing a dense covariance.
The mean-dependent terms also simplify under the rank-one KDN write. With
$\vct{\delta}_t=\vct{v}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t^\top\vct{k}_t$ and
$\vct{S}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t=\vct{\kappa}_t\vct{\delta}_t^\top$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \left\|\vct{v}_t-\vct{S}_t^\top\vct{k}_t\right\|_2^2
  &=\left(1-\vct{k}_t^\top\vct{\kappa}_t\right)^2
    \|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2, \\
  \operatorname{tr}\!\left[
    \widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{-1}
    (\vct{S}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t)
    (\vct{S}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t)^\top
  \right]
  &=\|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2
    \vct{\kappa}_t^\top
    \widehat{\vct{P}}_t^{-1}\vct{\kappa}_t.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-rank-one-loss}
\end{equation}
Diagonal and Isotropic KDN can therefore evaluate the auxiliary using their existing states and
token-local reductions.

The two terms have the same roles as in a VAE. The reconstruction term makes the post-write memory
explain the current value and penalizes posterior uncertainty along the observed key. The KL term
limits how far the posterior mean and covariance can move from their pre-write predictions.
Because the posterior is allowed to depend on the current value, the reconstruction term alone can
reward copying that value into memory; the KL term is what turns the complete objective into a
valid variational bound.

Summing Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-predictive-nll-variational-identity} over value coordinates
gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{mem},t}
  =\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred},t}
  +\sum_{j=1}^{d_v}
    \operatorname{KL}\!\left(
      q_{t,j}\,\|\,
      p(\vct{s}_{t,j}^{\star}\mid
        v_{t,j},\mathcal{F}_{t-1},\vct{k}_t)
    \right).
  \label{eq:discussion-memory-elbo-identity}
\end{equation}
Thus this is not an independent copy of the predictive calibration loss. It reduces to the
predictive NLL when the retained posterior is exact; for Diagonal or Isotropic KDN it additionally
penalizes the full posterior-approximation gap. Under the ordinary ELBO projection, this gap is
the reverse-KL error imposed by the restricted covariance family. Under a split
$(s_{\mathrm{write}},s_{\mathrm{protect}})$ update with $s_{\mathrm{protect}}\ne
s_{\mathrm{write}}$, it also penalizes the deliberate deviation from the ordinary Gaussian
posterior and may therefore weaken the intended protection against future overwrites. The
predictive NLL is the more direct choice when the goal is only to calibrate the pre-write
innovation variance, whereas $\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{mem},t}$ is appropriate when the goal also
includes regularizing the post-write memory approximation toward the coherent Gaussian posterior.

A dimension-normalized auxiliary objective can be added to language-model training as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  =\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LM}}
  +\lambda_{\mathrm{mem}}
    \mathbb{E}_{t,h}\!\left[
      \frac{1}{d_v}\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{mem},t,h}
    \right].
  \label{eq:discussion-lm-plus-memory-elbo}
\end{equation}
This term should initially be tested with a small $\lambda_{\mathrm{mem}}$, a fixed covariance
gauge, and all Gaussian normalizers retained. Full gradients can also change the scale and geometry
of the learned keys and values rather than only calibrating uncertainty, so they should be compared
with a route that treats the innovation and key geometry as fixed targets. Because
$\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{mem},t}$ already contains $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred},t}$, the two auxiliary
losses should normally be evaluated separately rather than summed without explicit reweighting.

\subsection{Calibration diagnostics and optimization caveat}

Under a correctly specified predictive model, the normalized innovation squared
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{NIS}_t
  =\frac{\|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2}{\Delta_t}
  \label{eq:discussion-normalized-innovation-squared}
\end{equation}
has a $\chi^2_{d_v}$ distribution, with mean $d_v$ and variance $2d_v$. Accordingly, useful
diagnostics are $\operatorname{NIS}_t/d_v$, the predictive NLL, and calibration bins comparing
predicted $\Delta_t$ with the empirical quantity
$\|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2/d_v$. These should be stratified by layer, head, position, and key
similarity. A mean normalized innovation near one is necessary but not sufficient: averages can
hide systematic overconfidence on repeated keys and underconfidence on novel keys.

The predictive NLL also breaks the covariance-only scale symmetry when the innovation units are
held fixed, because rescaling $(\widehat{\vct{P}}_t,r_t)$ changes $\Delta_t$ and hence changes the
score. In the neural mixer, however, both $\vct{v}_t$ and $\vct{k}_t$ are learned internal
representations rather than externally observed quantities. They can change scale or geometry
together with the uncertainty heads and downstream projections. The resulting objective is
therefore best described as an internal probabilistic consistency loss, not automatically as a
calibrated likelihood of the language data.

This distinction suggests a staged experiment. First evaluate the NLL and normalized innovation
without adding them to training. If they are optimized, compare: (i) full gradients through the
internal representation; (ii) a calibration-only route in which the realized innovation and key
geometry are treated as fixed targets and gradients are sent only to the uncertainty parameters;
and (iii) the language-modeling objective alone. Fixing a covariance gauge, preferably $r=1$ and
$\mu=1$, remains useful in all three cases. An auxiliary loss
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  =\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LM}}
   +\lambda_{\mathrm{pred}}
    \mathbb{E}_{t,h}\!\left[
      \overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{pred},t,h}
    \right]
  \label{eq:discussion-lm-plus-predictive-nll}
\end{equation}
should begin with small $\lambda_{\mathrm{pred}}$ and be judged not only by predictive NLL but
also by language-modeling loss, write strength, covariance quantiles, and downstream recall. A
lower internal NLL is evidence that the noise heads predict their own innovation statistics more
consistently; it is not by itself evidence of a better language model or a more faithful full
covariance approximation.

\section{Causal Associative Replay as a Direct Overwrite Objective}
\label{sec:discussion-associative-replay}

The predictive NLL evaluates whether pre-write uncertainty matches the current innovation, and the
memory ELBO additionally evaluates the quality of the post-write posterior approximation. Neither
objective directly states that an association written in the past should remain recoverable after
intervening writes. A causal replay objective can provide this missing behavioral signal: at time
$t$, sample an earlier token $\tau<t$, query the current memory with its key $\vct{k}_\tau$, and
compare the result with a detached target from time $\tau$.

\subsection{Raw replay mixes writing and retention}

The most direct construction uses the old value itself,
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\vct{v}}_{\tau\mid t}
  =\vct{S}_t^\top\vct{k}_\tau,
  \qquad
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{raw-replay}}
  =\mathbb{E}_{t,\tau\sim\mathcal{Q}_t}\!\left[
    \frac{1}{d_v}
    \left\|
      \widetilde{\vct{v}}_{\tau\mid t}
      -\operatorname{sg}(\vct{v}_\tau)
    \right\|_2^2
  \right],
  \label{eq:discussion-raw-replay-loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{Q}_t$ is a causal distribution over past indices and
$\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ denotes stop-gradient. This objective is causal and adds no inference
cost, but it conflates three effects: whether the association was written accurately at $\tau$,
whether the transition intentionally decayed it, and whether later writes interfered with it.

To see the first issue, define the post-write read and effective gain at the original token by
\begin{equation}
  \vct{y}_\tau^+
  =\vct{S}_\tau^\top\vct{k}_\tau,
  \qquad
  g_\tau=\vct{k}_\tau^\top\vct{\kappa}_\tau.
  \label{eq:discussion-replay-post-write-target}
\end{equation}
The KDN update gives
\begin{equation}
  \vct{y}_\tau^+
  =\widehat{\vct{S}}_\tau^\top\vct{k}_\tau
   +g_\tau\vct{\delta}_\tau,
  \qquad
  \vct{v}_\tau-\vct{y}_\tau^+
  =(1-g_\tau)\vct{\delta}_\tau.
  \label{eq:discussion-replay-immediate-residual}
\end{equation}
Consequently, raw replay pressure can increase $g_\tau$ toward one in order to make the initial
write reproduce $\vct{v}_\tau$ more exactly. This may strengthen the very current-token overwrite
that the experiment is intended to diagnose. It also asks a finite-capacity memory to preserve
every sampled internal value, including associations that the language-modeling objective may
prefer to forget.

\subsection{Post-write retention consistency}

A cleaner overwrite target preserves what was actually stored rather than requiring perfect
initial reconstruction. Define
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{retain}}
  =\mathbb{E}_{t,\tau\sim\mathcal{Q}_t}\!\left[
    w_{t,\tau}
    \frac{
      \left\|
        \vct{S}_t^\top\operatorname{sg}(\vct{k}_\tau)
        -\operatorname{sg}(\vct{y}_\tau^+)
      \right\|_2^2
    }{
      \|\operatorname{sg}(\vct{y}_\tau^+)\|_2^2+\epsilon
    }
  \right].
  \label{eq:discussion-retention-consistency-loss}
\end{equation}
This loss does not change the target when the model later changes its key or value representation,
and the normalization prevents the scale of the internal value space from dominating the loss.
The weight $w_{t,\tau}$ may prioritize associations that were actually written, for example using
a detached function of $g_\tau$ or of the original write magnitude. Compared with
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-raw-replay-loss}, this construction separates initial write quality
from subsequent retention. It can still oppose intentional transition decay, because its target is
the undiminished post-write read.

If the goal is to isolate interference from later writes while preserving the model's chosen
decay, define the transition product
\begin{equation}
  \vct{\Phi}_{t\leftarrow\tau}
  =\vct{D}_t\vct{D}_{t-1}\cdots\vct{D}_{\tau+1}
  \label{eq:discussion-replay-transition-product}
\end{equation}
and the decay-only counterfactual
\begin{equation}
  \vct{y}^{\mathrm{decay}}_{\tau\to t}
  =\left(
    \vct{\Phi}_{t\leftarrow\tau}\vct{S}_\tau
  \right)^\top\vct{k}_\tau.
  \label{eq:discussion-replay-decay-only-target}
\end{equation}
Replacing $\vct{y}_\tau^+$ in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-retention-consistency-loss} with a stopped-gradient copy of
$\vct{y}^{\mathrm{decay}}_{\tau\to t}$ penalizes only the deviation caused by intervening writes.
This is the most specific test of overwrite, but it requires retaining or recomputing the anchor
state and its decay-only trajectory. The simpler post-write target requires storing only
$(\vct{k}_\tau,\vct{y}_\tau^+)$ and is therefore a cheaper first experiment.

\subsection{A local interference surrogate}

An even cheaper objective measures the disturbance produced by the current write at a sampled
past address. Since
\begin{equation}
  \vct{S}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t
  =\vct{\kappa}_t\vct{\delta}_t^\top,
\end{equation}
the instantaneous change in the read at $\vct{k}_\tau$ is exactly
\begin{equation}
  (\vct{S}_t-\widehat{\vct{S}}_t)^\top\vct{k}_\tau
  =\vct{\delta}_t
    (\vct{\kappa}_t^\top\vct{k}_\tau).
  \label{eq:discussion-instantaneous-interference}
\end{equation}
This yields the sampled penalty
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{interfere}}
  =\mathbb{E}_{t,\tau\sim\mathcal{Q}_t}\!\left[
    w_{t,\tau}
    \frac{
      (\vct{k}_\tau^\top\vct{\kappa}_t)^2
      \|\vct{\delta}_t\|_2^2
    }{
      \|\operatorname{sg}(\vct{y}_\tau^+)\|_2^2+\epsilon
    }
  \right].
  \label{eq:discussion-instantaneous-interference-loss}
\end{equation}
Only the past key and a normalization statistic need to be cached. This surrogate directly
penalizes overlap between the current write direction and past addresses, but it treats every
change as harmful. Repeated keys can legitimately revise an old association, so the sampling or
weighting rule must identify associations intended to persist.

\subsection{Sampling, optimization, and relation to calibration}

Evaluating all pairs $(\tau,t)$ would be quadratic. A practical implementation can retain a small
number of detached anchors per chunk and evaluate them at later chunk boundaries, turning the
additional reads into a small batched matrix multiplication. A log-uniform lag distribution covers
both local and long-range retention. Mixing these samples with hard candidates selected from a
small pool by key similarity concentrates supervision on pairs most likely to interfere without
forming the full pairwise similarity matrix.

The recommended first experiment is the post-write consistency loss in
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-retention-consistency-loss}, with one or a few anchors per chunk,
detached keys and targets, a small loss weight, and explicit reporting by recall distance. The raw
value loss should be kept as an ablation because it combines stronger initial writing with
retention. The local interference loss is useful as a low-cost diagnostic or regularizer, but it
does not measure accumulated corruption from several individually small writes. To isolate changes
after $\tau$, the auxiliary gradient should also be truncated at the anchor state; otherwise the
loss is an end-to-end retention objective that may change how the original association is stored.
Likewise, an uncertainty-only version of
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-instantaneous-interference-loss} should detach
$\vct{\delta}_t$ and both keys, routing gradients only through $\vct{\kappa}_t$, so that the model
cannot reduce the penalty merely by shrinking values or rotating the key representation.

With a fixed information scale of one, replay supplies a decision-oriented retention signal that
the coherent Gaussian ELBO does not contain. It does not calibrate $\vct{P}_t$; instead, it states
which past associations should remain useful. The two goals can be combined as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  =\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LM}}
  +\lambda_{\mathrm{pred}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}
  +\lambda_{\mathrm{retain}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{retain}},
  \label{eq:discussion-lm-calibration-retention-loss}
\end{equation}
but the losses should first be ablated separately. Predictive NLL rewards uncertainty that matches
one-step errors, whereas replay rewards memory that survives interference. If a diagonal model
cannot improve both simultaneously, that conflict is evidence that one covariance state is being
asked to serve incompatible calibration and protection roles. In that case, separating a
calibrated covariance from a protection state is more principled than forcing either loss to absorb
the mismatch. A clean factorial experiment should compare unit and $d_k$-scaled protection with
replay disabled and enabled, reporting predictive NLL or NIS, replay error by lag, effective-write
strength, language-modeling loss, and downstream recall.

\section{Conclusion}

The diagonal KDN has a genuine common-scale non-identifiability in
$(\vct{\omega},r,\mu)$, but the completed noisy-MQAR experiments show that random initial
anisotropy is the dominant observed instability. The appropriate design is therefore
twofold: start the gain isotropically, and fix a covariance unit---preferably $r=1$ and
$\mu=1$---so the model learns only uncertainty ratios that affect its predictions.

Process noise should be constrained by that gauge, not removed by it. Section
\ref{sec:discussion-zero-process-noise} shows that $\vct{\omega}$ is not merely the third carrier
of the scale symmetry: it sets the stationary plasticity floor
$\Theta(\sqrt{\omega r})$, and without it the layer degenerates into recursive least squares with
a gain that decays as $d_k/(st)$. The fidelity of the diagonal approximation, meanwhile, is
governed by key geometry through
Equation~\eqref{eq:discussion-diagonal-exactness-condition} and is unaffected by $\vct{\omega}$
either way. Fixing $r$ and $\mu$ while keeping a positive, isotropically initialized
$\vct{\omega}$ is therefore the design that removes the flat direction without removing the
mechanism.

Finally, reverse-KL overconfidence and anisotropic overwrite require separate controls. The
proposed $B=2$ channel cap is a scan-compatible guardrail against unbounded current-write geometry;
it should be evaluated alongside off-key shrinkage and explicit effective-write caps rather than
interpreted as a more faithful covariance approximation.
